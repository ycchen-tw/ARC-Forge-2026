# AR25 — Mirror reflections must cover targets
Keywords: mirror, reflection, symmetry, coverage, selectable object, cycle selection, undo, block grid, multiple pieces, exact tiling, overhang, move budget, orbit
Looks like: A 21×21 board (3×3-pixel cells) with a light-blue mirror stripe across it, one or two pieces, and yellow target shapes. The rightmost column is a step counter. All seven actions exist: arrows move the selected object, a click selects an object for free, ACTION5 cycles the selection, and ACTION7 is undo. On boards with a movable mirror (from level 2 on), the mirror, not a piece, is the selected object at the start (white dots). On the first board the mirror is fixed, so a piece is selected.

## Rules
- Mirrors are selectable objects. A vertical mirror moves only left/right, and a horizontal one only up/down. A piece reflects as x'=2m−x (or y'=2m−y), so moving a mirror by d moves every reflection by 2d.
- With two mirrors, each piece has up to four copies: itself, one reflection per mirror, and the 180° copy. Reflections are drawn in colour 4. Off-board copies are not drawn but can be reflected back onto the board.
- Win = every target cell is covered by a physical piece or any reflection, checked after a direction action. Overhang onto empty cells is allowed. It is not an exact-match test.
- A covered target still shows its centre pixel as yellow, so "no yellow visible" is not the test. Read the cell's outer pixels instead.
- No collisions: pieces and mirrors pass through each other freely.
- Budget is 64/64/128/128/128/320/320/320 moves. Only direction actions that actually move something, plus ACTION5, are charged. Clicks, undo, board-edge bumps and wrong-axis presses on a mirror are free, and undo does not refund.
- Everything sits on a 3-cell block lattice, so plan in blocks.
- Objects are fixed per level. One "object" can look like several diagonally touching pieces.

## Levels
- L1: one fixed vertical mirror and one piece. Its reflection, not the piece, must land on the target.
- L2: the mirror is movable and starts selected. Moving the mirror shifts the reflection twice as far.
- L3: horizontal mirror, two pieces, four targets that form two mirror pairs. The mirror must be moved onto the pair's symmetry line, and the pieces parked on the near targets (hard: cleared 13/13, median 10 min).
- L4: two targets, each the union of both pieces' shapes. A piece need not sit on the same-side target as its image.
- L5: first level with two mirrors. One piece gives four copies, and the solution needs overhang (hard: cleared 13/13, median 24 min).
- L6: two objects, one of them drawn as several diagonally touching parts, and 52 targets. The copies tile the target exactly, which forces the mirrors onto the target's symmetry axes (hard: cleared 13/13, median 15 min).
- L7: sparse target, symmetric only under 180° rotation. Overhang is unavoidable and the mirror crossing is forced to the rotation centre (hard: cleared 13/13, median 19 min).
- L8: the 60-block target is exactly four copies of two pieces. All 13 copies cleared it quickly.

No copy ended stuck. The cost was minutes and wasted moves.

## Blind spots — where our player lost time
1. **Win test misread (9 copies, mostly L5, L7, L8).**
   **Believed:** the union of piece and images must equal the target, so exact-cover searches were run. Others thought covered yellow was "consumed".
   **Actually:** it is a coverage test with overhang allowed, and the yellow centre pixel never disappears.
   **Cue:** the target has fewer blocks than four times the piece blocks, or has no mirror symmetry.
   **Test:** count blocks. If the target is smaller than the orbit, or has no mirror symmetry, search for cover with spill. Do not search for equality.
2. **Wrong object selected (9 copies, L2, L3, L5).**
   **Believed:** arrows act on the piece. In fact the mirror was selected at the start, so presses did nothing or moved the stripe.
   **Cue:** white dots along the stripe's centre line. Up/down on a full-height vertical mirror does nothing.
   **Test:** press one arrow, or click the intended object, and see which dots turn white and what moved. Clicks are free.
3. **Piece on its target felt like a solved level (3 copies, L3).**
   **Believed:** parking the black piece exactly on a target should clear it.
   **Actually:** the paired target on the other side also had to be covered by the reflection, which required moving the mirror.
   **Cue:** the targets come in vertically flipped pairs, one of them a copy of the piece's own shape.
   **Test:** check whether two targets are mirror images of each other. If so, the mirror belongs on their midline.
4. **Object identity guessed from connectivity (8 copies, L6, L8).**
   **Believed:** pieces that touch diagonally are one object, and later that touching pieces merge.
   **Actually:** the objects are fixed per level. One may be drawn as several parts, and two separate objects can touch without merging.
   **Cue:** the number of white dots on selection equals that object's block count.
   **Test:** select an object, move it one step, and see which blocks moved.
5. **Reading target or coverage off the current frame (5 copies, L3, L6, L7, L8).**
   **Believed:** the yellow cells left on screen are the target still uncovered.
   **Actually:** covered cells keep their centre pixel, and an object's holes show the colour underneath.
   **Cue:** the yellow count drops as pieces move, yet the level does not clear.
   **Test:** take the target from the level's first board and check coverage by block.
6. **Hand-read geometry and solver slips (5 copies, L5, L6, L7).**
   **Believed:** eyeballed shapes, miscounts, or a solver that skipped negative offsets or swapped (row, col).
   **Actually:** these gave false "impossible" verdicts and wasted replays.
   **Cue:** a solver reports no solution, or the action count disagrees with the plan.
   **Test:** recompute a known state (the current reflections) with the solver before trusting its verdict.

## Breakthroughs
- Targets in mirror pairs (L3, L4) put the mirror on the pair's midline, with the pieces parked on the near side. The reflections then land on the far side.
- Target symmetry fixes the mirrors (L5–L8): the crossing goes at the target's symmetry axes or rotation centre. If the target has exactly four times as many blocks as the pieces, the copies must tile it exactly.
- Brute-force search in block units over mirror positions and piece translations gave a unique answer, which could then be played blind.
- A single probe move of a selected object shows which blocks belong to it.

## Lessons that carry to other games
- Check which object currently receives input before reading a null result as "action does nothing".
- When a goal object is derived from the one you steer, work backwards from the target to the control that places it.
- Count the sizes: if the derived copies number about as many cells as the target, expect an exact tiling. If they number more, expect overhang to be allowed.
- Don't judge a win condition by what is visible on screen. Verify against the level's first board.
- An object may be several visible parts, and parts that touch need not be one object. Test by moving it.
- Validate a solver on the current board before trusting an "impossible" verdict.
