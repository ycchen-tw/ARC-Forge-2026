# BP35 — Gravity-flip cavern with clickable blocks
Keywords: gravity reversal, click to remove blocks, left/right only, falling to rest, spikes at fall end, scrolling camera, off-screen click limit, toggle blocks, split blocks, move budget, undo, hidden second switch

Looks like: a tall, narrow cavern on a 64x64 screen that scrolls only vertically. A small blue character with a side mark sits in it, with 5x5 rimmed blocks of several colours, purple cup-shaped spikes, and a pink plus-shaped gem somewhere far above or below. Actions are only left, right, click at a screen position, and undo. A bottom bar counts moves. Our players kept reading the open area as water and the character as a bubble, boat or fish that floats.

## Rules
- The character walks one cell left or right and cannot jump. Every height change is a fall in the gravity direction, which is upward on screen by default. It clings to the underside of platforms. A blocked walk still costs a move.
- Touching the gem wins, by walking into it or by ending a fall on it. The budget is 64 moves in levels 1–6 and 128 in levels 7–9, and it resets each level. Water rises in levels 1–3, but only some left/right moves advance it.
- A click acts only if the target is on screen and holds exactly one special block. On screen means roughly 11–12 map rows around the character, so a block far away is not clickable.
- Block types:
  - Core 14 vanishes when clicked.
  - Core 8 vanishes and reverses gravity. It is single use and often buried in rock.
  - Solid 12 becomes dotted, which can be passed through. Dotted 12 becomes solid again.
  - Core 15 vanishes and grows four copies around it, which lets the player drill one cell at a time.
- Removing the block underfoot makes the character fall. After a reversal, dotted 12 no longer counts as floor.
- Spikes are cups facing the arriving character. Ending a fall on one loses, including a one-cell fall after a sideways step. Walking into a spike sideways only blocks.
- Undo restores the previous state but still costs a move. After a loss only RESET works.

## Levels
- L1: remove 14 blocks to open a path up to the gem (cleared 15/15, median 32 min).
- L2: spikes appear (hard: cleared 12/15, median 166 min, 3 stuck copies).
- L3: dotted and solid 12 toggles (cleared 11/12, median 56 min, 1 stuck copy).
- L4: first gravity-reversal blocks, and the gem is on the opposite side (cleared 11/11, median 53 min).
- L5: reversed-gravity spikes, and a second reversal block is buried in rock (hard: cleared 10/11, median 78 min, 1 stuck copy).
- L6: heavy mix of toggles and three reversal blocks, with no 14 blocks (hard: cleared 0/10, 7 stuck copies).
- L7: a whole wall column of reversal blocks and a 128-move budget. Not reached.
- L8: splitting blocks, and the only reversal block is out of click range. Not reached.
- L9: combined; the gem sits in an outer shaft. Not reached.

## Blind spots — where our player lost time
1. **Believed:** the level has only the gravity blocks seen so far, so it is a parity puzzle with too few flips. Copies declared L6 impossible, or a rule wrong, and tested odd hypotheses. **Actually:** L6 has three reversal blocks, and copies found two. The L5 stuck copy likewise assumed a single block, but a second one is buried in rock far below. **Cue:** a shaft or corridor running off the top or bottom of the screen, or a solid mass below the floor, that was never scrolled to. **Test:** move so the camera covers the unseen end before concluding. Do not trust a stitched map that stops at the last seen row. (7 copies)
2. **Believed:** an inexplicable death, with ad-hoc rules such as "a hazard kills through a wall row" or "unsolvable under every model". **Actually:** the character died because its fall ended with a spike cup in its path. **Cue:** the character stops directly in front of a cup-shaped purple object, then shrinks. **Test:** before opening a column, look at the object at the far end of the fall. Open one block at a time and use Undo if the stop cell is next to a spike. (4 copies)
3. **Believed:** a click works on any block "at any distance". **Actually:** only blocks on screen can be clicked, and the camera offset depends on gravity direction and stop height. **Cue:** a block half visible or hidden at the screen edge. **Test:** note which rows are currently on screen before planning. Stop on a ledge or cap so the next target scrolls into view. (4 copies)
4. **Believed:** L3 is unsolvable under the current model. **Actually:** the dotted-12 marks can be clicked into solid blocks to cap a column, and the stuck copy never tried that. **Cue:** faint X-shaped marks in the open, sitting below a spike. **Test:** click one and see whether it turns solid. (1 copy)

## Breakthroughs
- Copies that cleared L3 treated dotted 12 as caps: make a ceiling before opening the column below it, so the fall stops short of the spike. (11 copies)
- Copies that cleared L5 found the buried second reversal block by sinking until the camera reached it. (7 copies)
- In L4, copies stopped the fall on a platform so the next reversal block scrolled into view. (2 copies)

## Lessons that carry to other games
- If a puzzle appears to need one more of a single-use switch than you have found, scroll to the unseen end of the map first.
- When a death has no visible cause, find what sits at the end of the motion, not at the starting cell.
- A click range tied to the camera is a constraint. Choose where to stop so the target is on screen.
- Faint, pale or dotted markers may be toggleable objects. Click one early.
- Order matters. Do the protective step before the step that opens the danger.
