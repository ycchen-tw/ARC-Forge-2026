# CD82 — Overwrite painting with orbiting brushes
Keywords: paint order, overwrite layers, half-plane strokes, orbiting tool ring, palette select, stamp brush, copy target pattern, backward peeling, unpainted background, refused move, last-stroke-wins

Looks like: The board shows a small target picture (10×10) at the upper left, an all-blank 10×10 canvas in the centre, and a row of colour swatches along the top. A bucket-like tool sits above the canvas. Actions are four direction keys (move the tool), ACTION5 (stroke) and ACTION6 (click a swatch, or later a small attached brush). The task is to reproduce the target pattern.

## Rules
- The canvas starts all colour 0, which is also the "white" swatch. Leaving a region unpainted is the same as painting it 0.
- The tool occupies one of 8 positions on the 3×3 ring around the canvas. The centre is blocked. The four direction keys are literal screen directions (up/down/left/right), and a key that has no ring neighbour that way is refused. A refused key costs an action, changes nothing and shows no animation.
- ACTION5 paints with the currently selected colour and plain overwrite. An orthogonal position paints the half of the canvas on the tool's side. A diagonal position paints a triangle, and its edge includes the canvas diagonal. The two complementary diagonal strokes therefore overlap on the diagonal.
- The small brush exists from level 3 and shows only at the four orthogonal positions. Clicking it with ACTION6 paints a small rectangle (4×3, or 3×4 on the left or right) flush against the canvas edge nearest the tool. The rectangle is a fixed, centered strip along that edge (rows 3-6 or cols 3-6). There is only one small brush at a time, beside the selected orthogonal tool position, and it is reusable.
- Colour persists until another swatch is clicked, so click the swatch before each stroke. Swatch clicks, moves and refused keys each cost 1 action.
- Win: the canvas matches the target on 80 cells. The 20 cells on the two diagonals are ignored. The check runs only when a stroke ends. There is no undo, and the limit is 100 actions per level.

## Levels
- L1: two colours, big strokes only; just top half versus bottom half. (hard: cleared 13/13, median 53 min; nobody was stuck, the time went on discovering the controls)
- L2: three colours; the target mixes a diagonal split with a half. Two strokes work if the blank canvas supplies the white wedge. (cleared 13/13, median 19 min)
- L3: the small brush appears, with 7 swatches. The target has a rectangle that no half or triangle can produce. (cleared 13/13, median 25 min)
- L4: the small brush now lands on the left edge as a 3×4 rectangle, so it works at other positions. Layering of two halves and a diagonal is needed.
- L5: scattered leftover cells that come from carving layers, plus a small-brush rectangle.
- L6: five colours, two small-brush rectangles, and a white wedge that must stay unpainted.

## Blind spots — where our player lost time
1. **Movement model (8 copies).**
   **Believed:** the tool has 5 poses or a one-way or clamped cycle, so some positions (below, left) are unreachable.
   **Actually:** it is a ring of 8 positions on a 3×3 grid, and the keys are screen directions.
   **Cue:** the up and down keys "do nothing" at the start position.
   **Test:** from the start, try all four keys. Then try all four from each new position and write down which are refused.
2. **Small brush discovered late or mislocated (4 copies).**
   **Believed:** the extra small box is decoration, works only at one position, or is "tied to the start position".
   **Actually:** it is a second brush, usable at the orthogonal positions, and some target regions can only be made with it.
   **Cue:** a small framed box in the selected colour rides on the tool's far side.
   **Test:** click its coloured interior with a colour that differs from the canvas, at the start position and at one side position.
3. **Move silently refused, then a stroke fired from the wrong place (5 copies).**
   **Believed:** the key worked.
   **Actually:** the key was refused, so the stroke or brush click happened at a different position.
   **Cue:** no tool movement and a tiny diff.
   **Test:** after every move, check the tool's position before pressing ACTION5 or clicking the small brush.
4. **Colour state and click targets (3 copies).**
   **Believed:** the stroke uses the last colour, or any click near a swatch works.
   **Actually:** ACTION5 uses the currently selected colour, and the swatch is a small target.
   **Cue:** a stroke changes 0 cells, or the canvas fills with the wrong colour.
   **Test:** read the selection marker under the swatch before every stroke.
5. **Stroke order (2 copies).**
   **Believed:** the order of strokes is not critical.
   **Actually:** the last stroke covering a cell decides its colour.
   **Cue:** a leftover triangle in the wrong colour.
   **Test:** peel backwards from the last stroke. Its whole region must be one colour in the target.
6. **Diagonal ownership (3 copies).**
   **Believed:** the two diagonal strokes split the diagonal, or one omits it.
   **Actually:** both include it.
   **Cue:** a diagonal line of unexpected colour.
   **Test:** stroke one diagonal onto a pre-coloured canvas and count the changed cells.

## Breakthroughs
- **Move graph (4 copies):** copies that treated the keys as screen directions on a 3×3 ring planned routes correctly and finished levels 4-6 in minutes.
- **Backward peeling (5 copies):** the last stroke must cover a region that is one colour in the target. When no half or triangle qualifies, the small-brush rectangle is the last action.
- **Unpainted background (4 copies):** copies noticed that the blank canvas already supplies one target colour, which saves strokes. The white swatch can also erase.
- **Small-brush landing (3 copies):** the rectangle lands flush on the near edge, centered along it, so the tool's side selects which edge strip is painted.
- **Simulate before playing (4 copies):** copies that simulated candidate stroke sets against the target before acting avoided wasted actions.

## Lessons that carry to other games
- When controls look dead at the start, map which inputs are accepted from each state before concluding the reachable space is small.
- After every move, check that the state really changed. A refused input can look identical to success.
- If a goal region has a shape that none of your known tools can make, look for a small extra instrument. Test it in every position it can occupy.
- With overwrite-style painting, plan backwards. Ask what the last action can cover in a single colour, and check whether the initial colour already supplies some regions.
- Mode state such as a selected colour persists. Check it before each use.
- Check whether the boundary cells of two complementary regions belong to both or to neither.
