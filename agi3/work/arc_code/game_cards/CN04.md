# CN04 — Pair up matching pins

Keywords: pairing, pin matching, hidden type, translate and rotate, selection, variant stack, ping-pong cycle, fog of war, assembly puzzle, action budget, click to select, unique assembly

Looks like: A 20×20 board with 2–5 wireframe shapes, each with a few single-cell pins on its edges. Actions are move ×4, ACTION5 (rotate), and ACTION6 (click to select). Later levels paint every unselected shape a flat dark colour, and some shapes change size and pin count instead of rotating.

## Rules
- Pick a shape by clicking it, then translate it by one cell per step. ACTION5 rotates it 90° clockwise about its bbox top-left. Shapes pass through each other freely.
- Win needs every pin on every visible shape paired. Pairing means exactly 2 pins of the same hidden type on one cell. There are two types, and both are drawn identically.
- One pin of type A on one pin of type B is also drawn as "paired" (colour 3). It does not count, so colour 3 is not proof of progress.
- In levels 3–6 only the selected shape shows its pins. Unselected shapes are flat dark silhouettes, and their paired pins are not drawn as paired.
- Clicking the selected shape deselects it. Moves and rotations then do nothing but still cost an action.
- In levels 5–6, shapes that start at the same position form a stack with several size and pin-count variants. Only one variant is visible at a time. ACTION5 switches variant instead of rotating, or you can click a colour-0 body cell of the selected variant.
- Stack variants step back and forth through the list. The direction flag is shared by every stack in the level.
- Each level has a step limit (75 to 200, reset per level). Every action counts, including bad clicks and wall bumps.

## Levels
- L1 — Two shapes with two pins each. (hard: cleared 13/13, median 56 min)
- L2 — Four shapes, 12 pins, one chain-shaped assembly. (hard: cleared 11/13, median 32 min)
- L3 — Dark-silhouette display begins. Three shapes, with the pairing forming a triangle.
- L4 — Four shapes, all pins of one type, pairing in a cycle.
- L5 — One 5-variant stack plus three rigid shapes. Only the largest variant admits a solution. (hard: cleared 11/11, median 38 min)
- L6 — Two stacks plus three rigid shapes. Both stacks must be at maximum size, and they share the cycle direction. (cleared 11/11, median 23 min)

## Blind spots — where our player lost time
1. **Believed:** a touching pair that turns colour 3 but does not clear means "the bodies must not overlap". Players also tried bar-in-hole, jigsaw and tiling goals. **Actually:** the pins have hidden types, and colour 3 also appears for mismatched types. Overlap is not a rule. Zero-overlap poses cleared only because they happened to pair like with like (inferred). 11 copies. **Cue:** every pin is coincident and drawn as paired, yet there is no win. **Test:** keep the same geometry but swap which pins meet, or move one piece by a cell to re-pair differently, before adding geometric constraints.
2. **Believed:** a shape whose ACTION5 does not rotate it is rigid, and pin counts are fixed. **Actually:** it is a stack of variants, and the level is only solvable at one variant (L5 and L6). 4 copies. **Cue:** an odd total pin count, or a solver reporting "no assembly". **Test:** press ACTION5 once on it and compare the silhouette, then cycle through all variants.
3. **Believed:** each stack has its own cycle position and direction. **Actually:** the direction flag is shared, so pressing one stack can flip the other. 3 copies. **Cue:** a press shrinks a stack you expected to grow. **Test:** press one stack to its end, then press the other once and see which way it moves.
4. **Believed:** a click always "activates" the piece under the cursor, and the next moves go to the piece you meant. **Actually:** clicking the selected shape deselects it, a click on empty cells selects nothing, and the last-clicked shape gets all moves. Paired pins are also invisible on unselected shapes. 5 copies. **Cue:** a move changes 0 cells. **Test:** after each click, nudge once and confirm the intended shape moved.
5. **Believed:** the offline search says the level is impossible. **Actually:** the search itself was wrong (mask typo, off-by-one on coincident pins, stale rigid assumption). 8 copies. **Cue:** a solver contradicting an earlier level's pattern. **Test:** rerun the solver on the previous level's known answer, and re-render the first board from the model and diff it.

## Breakthroughs
- Count pins and search over variants. Only the maximal variant of each stack gives a solution, so growing the stacks is the puzzle. 6 copies.
- Treat the solution as a cycle or chain of pairings. No single pair of pieces matches on its own, so pair-by-pair checks wrongly say "impossible". 2 copies.
- Realise the cycle direction is global across stacks. 2 copies.
- Prefer poses where only the pin cells touch. 3 copies.

## Lessons that carry to other games
- If a "match" indicator lights but nothing clears, suspect a hidden attribute the indicator ignores. Check it by re-pairing the same geometry before inventing new spatial constraints.
- If an action does not do what it did on earlier objects, check whether it changes the object's shape or state instead.
- Check whether state such as a direction flag is shared across objects, not per object.
- When only the selected object is fully drawn, confirm after every click which one is selected. A click on the active one may toggle it off.
- Distrust an "impossible" verdict from your own tooling until it reproduces a known solution.
- A count mismatch (odd totals) can be the cue that an object must be changed before the goal is reachable.
