# FT09 — Clue tiles constrain a light grid
Keywords: click-only, cycle colors, constraint satisfaction, clue tile, neighbor directions, linked toggles, Lights Out, GF(2), equal/not-equal, multi-level, move budget, tutorial demonstration boards

Looks like: The board shows a lattice of solid 3×3 tiles (4-cell pitch) on a dark background, plus a few special patterned tiles drawn as 3×3 mini-grids of white, gray, dark-gray and sometimes magenta cells. Only a click on a cell exists (action 6). A thin bar along the bottom edge and a small color-swatch legend at the top right are visible. Level 1 has no legend and shows several already-solved demonstration copies.

## Rules
- Clicking a light tile advances it to the next color in the cycle. The cycle is the legend order, top to bottom. With two colors it acts as a toggle; with three it is a true cycle. All lights start at the cycle's first color on each level.
- A clue tile (patterned mini) has a center color, its reference. Each of its 8 edge cells refers to the light in that direction.
  - White: that light must equal the reference color.
  - Light gray: that light must differ from the reference.
  - Dark gray: there is no light in that direction, so the constraint is skipped.
  - Clue tiles themselves are never constrained.
- The level clears the moment all clues are satisfied simultaneously. Lights that no clue mentions are free, so there is no single hidden target picture.
- Clicking a clue tile or blank space costs nothing and does not change anything. Only a click on a light spends budget (the bottom bar is only a rough counter).
- Linked lights carry magenta cells on their face. Each magenta cell marks a direction whose neighbor also flips when this light is clicked.
  - The magenta pattern is a permanent instruction, not a clue.
  - The effect does not chain, and clues and empty cells in a marked direction are unaffected.
  - Linked lights are themselves lights, so they are constrained by clues.
- With three colors, "differs" is a set. Choose the allowed color with the fewest cycle steps.
- With only ordinary lights, each light is independent. With linked lights over two colors, it is a Lights Out system over GF(2).

## Levels
- L1: Tutorial with no legend. One framed live panel and three already-solved copies of the same pattern. The clue is the panel's own center mini.
- L2: A larger grid with two clues whose neighborhoods overlap and agree. The reference color is now the second palette color.
- L3: An irregular, cross-shaped grid with four clues. The clues have different center colors, so white means "this clue's own center color" and gray means "the other color."
- L4 (harder: cleared 13/13, median 9 min): Three-color cycle. Gray means "not the center color," and cells constrained by clues with different centers are forced to the third color.
- L5 (hard: cleared 13/13, median 24 min): Eight clues plus the dark-gray "no light" cell. Three linked lights flip themselves plus their 4 orthogonal neighbors. The linked lights must also end in a specific color.
- L6 (cleared 13/13, median 8 min): All 22 lights are linked, and each click flips itself plus the light directly above. The columns form independent chains that are solved bottom-up.

## Blind spots — where our player lost time
1. **Linked lights read as clues or ignored (L5).**
   **Believed:** The magenta-marked tiles were hint tiles (their colors read as constraints), or plain lights alone decide the win. Several copies set every ordinary light correctly and saw no clear.
   **Actually:** They are lights that are constrained by neighboring clues, and their click also flips neighbors that must then be repaired.
   **Cue:** A fully "correct" board that does not clear, and magenta cells that never change when the tile's color changes.
   **Test:** Click one marked tile and diff the board to see which neighbors flipped. Then check whether any clue points at the marked tile itself.
2. **Click effects assumed to be self-only after a new marking appears (L6).**
   **Believed:** The self-only click model from L1–L5 still applied, so plans overshot.
   **Actually:** Every light flips itself plus the one above, so a light is changed by its own click and the click of the light below it.
   **Cue:** Every tile carries one magenta cell at its top-middle.
   **Test:** Click an interior light and a top-row light, then count changed tiles (two vs. one). Click a light below a clue to see whether the clue is affected. Check the direction of propagation before solving; one copy had it transposed.
3. **Three-color cycle and "differs" treated as a fixed target (L4).**
   **Believed:** Clicks toggle between two colors, and gray means the base color. Players clicked once to reach the third color and landed on the middle one.
   **Actually:** Clicks cycle in legend order, and gray means any color other than the clue's center.
   **Cue:** A legend with three swatches.
   **Test:** Click one light three times to see the cycle, then read the legend before planning clicks.
4. **Clue masks read by eye, and clue polarity assumed fixed (L1–L4).**
   **Believed:** White and gray map to fixed colors, or a hand reading of the mini is correct.
   **Actually:** White means "the clue's own center color" and gray means "other." Hand-reading cells off a dump repeatedly miscounted a white cell, costing wrong clicks.
   **Cue:** Overlapping clues disagree under a global mapping but agree under the center-relative one.
   **Test:** Parse the clues with code and check that overlapping clues never conflict. Verify a plan against the board it produces, not only against the solver's own target.
5. **Solver bug: constraints skipped for non-first colors (L6).**
   **Believed:** The solver's plan was complete. It returned 10 clicks that did not satisfy the board.
   **Actually:** The "differs" test was silently skipped when the center color was not the first palette color.
   **Cue:** A plan that "should" clear but doesn't.
   **Test:** Re-derive the clues from the simulated final board and count violations.

## Breakthroughs
- Treating the magenta pattern as the click's footprint, not as a clue, made the structure clear: the effect is Lights Out over GF(2). Once the linked lights' own required colors were included, the plan was a short solve with collateral repairs.
- Checking whether overlapping clue windows agreed under a candidate reading validated the center-relative rule and the dark-gray "no light" cell with zero conflicts.
- A single probe click per level, diffed at the tile level, revealed each new click footprint quickly.

## Lessons that carry to other games
- If a state satisfying every visible rule does not win, check whether an object you classified as a tool or decoration is itself also constrained.
- When a new marking appears on controllable objects, treat it as a possible change to what a click does. Probe one click and diff which objects changed before reusing the old model.
- A symbol's meaning can be relative to a local reference, not a global one. Test candidate readings by whether overlapping constraints agree.
- Check the full cycle length of the state change; a toggle assumption fails when there are three states.
- Verify a computed plan by simulating it and re-checking the rules, not only by trusting the solver's own target.
