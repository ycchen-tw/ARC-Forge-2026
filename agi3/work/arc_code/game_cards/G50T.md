# G50T — Record clones to hold switches

Keywords: record-and-replay, clone, rewind, pressure plate, hold gate, toggle gate, teleporter pair, patroller, edge-triggered, timing/parity, action budget, grid maze, no-op unavailable

Looks like: A 64×64 maze of corridors and walls. A small 5×5 hollow block you steer with up/down/left/right moves one 6 px cell per step. There is a hollow 9-colored frame with a gap (the goal), and colored wires linking plates to sliding gates. A row of small squares at the top-left is a body counter. Actions are 1–4 for movement and 5 for "record and rewind"; there is no click and no undo. A shrinking bar on the bottom row shows the action budget.

## Rules
- Move cell by cell. A move is legal only if the player's center lands on floor and no closed gate covers the cell. Entering the goal frame exactly with the current body clears the level. A clone on the goal does not count.
- Plates work only while somebody stands on them, and you have one body. ACTION5 stores the current trip as a clone, rewinds you to the start, and switches to the next body.
- Each later legal step you take makes every clone replay its next recorded step. A finished clone freezes where it ended and keeps holding whatever plate it sits on.
- **Only your legal moves advance time.** A refused move (into a wall or closed gate) costs budget but moves nothing. There is no wait action, so alignment needs detour steps.
- Every level gives about 130 actions. There are 1 usable clone in level 1 and 2 in levels 2–7. Pressing ACTION5 with the last body clears all clones, so the third recording wipes the first two. A gate toggled earlier may not return to its start state.
- Four device types, each wired to a plate:
  - Color 8 is a hold-open gate: open only while the plate is occupied.
  - Color 11 is a toggle gate: it flips on each unoccupied→occupied edge. Simultaneous arrivals count once, leaving does nothing, and clone replay re-triggers it.
  - Color 15 is a teleporter: a plate plus two pads. Pressing the plate swaps the contents of the two pads. The swap happens after all movement in that action, so someone who steps onto a pad in the same action is carried.
  - Color 14 is an autonomous patroller (the "green block") on a track the player cannot enter. It steps once per legal player step, presses plates, and is crushed by a closing gate. It is a remote button, not a threat.
- Within one action the order is release → move → press. Loss is checked before victory.

## Levels
- L1: one hold gate, one clone. Park a clone on the plate, then walk past the gate. (hard: cleared 14/15, median 102 min; one copy stuck 165 min)
- L2: two hold gates and two clones. The second recording must pass through the gate the first clone holds.
- L3: adds a toggle gate, so parity matters when clones cross the toggle plate. (hard: cleared 13/14, median 45 min; one copy stuck 29 min)
- L4: a teleporter. The goal area is sealed, so a pad must be entered on the same action a plate is pressed. (hard: cleared 12/13, median 34 min; one copy stuck 27 min)
- L5: a teleporter plus two toggle gates and a hold gate, with three jobs for two clones.
- L6: a patroller. Two clones hold open the gates on its route so it can toggle the gates that guard your pocket. (hard: cleared 10/12, median 50 min)
- L7: two teleporter pairs plus a patroller. The patroller fires the first pair, and a clone replays the teleport jump to reach the second.

## Blind spots — where our player lost time
1. **ACTION5 read as reset/rewind/mirror (10 copies).**
   **Believed:** ACTION5 is a restart or a cosmetic HUD toggle. The gray figure was a mirror of the player, or a ghost that exists only after an odd number of rewinds or after a run whose length is a multiple of 4.
   **Actually:** ACTION5 records the trip as a clone that replays it step for step and then parks, for any non-empty trip.
   **Cue:** the gray block is invisible while it sits under the player. A gate opens or closes while the player is far from its plate.
   **Test:** walk a few steps, press ACTION5, walk a different route, and watch whether a gray block repeats your first route one step per move.
2. **Purple teleporter treated as a door or held state (7 copies).**
   **Believed:** a clone parked on the plate keeps the link open, or the visible bridge between the rings can be walked.
   **Actually:** the swap fires only on the action the plate is newly occupied. The player must be inside a ring on that same action, and the bridge is just the animation.
   **Cue:** the board shows a charging animation of a little over ten frames before the swap. A ring entered while the plate was merely occupied did nothing.
   **Test:** enter a ring while a clone sits parked on the plate. Then time a clone's arrival to the same action as your ring entry.
3. **Toggle gate treated as hold or permanent (8 copies).**
   **Believed:** the color-11 gate behaves like the color-8 gate, or opens once and stays open.
   **Actually:** it flips on every arrival, by the player or by any clone replaying across the plate. Rewind does not guarantee restoring it: a trip that starts or ends on the toggle plate leaves one toggle behind.
   **Cue:** the gate stays moved after you step off, but flips back on the next visit. A recorded route that crosses the plate re-flips it every replay.
   **Test:** step on and off the plate twice and compare gate position after each arrival.
4. **Clone timing and recording length (6 copies).**
   **Believed:** a long, wandering recording or a recording that does not end on the plate will do.
   **Actually:** the clone arrives after exactly as many of your legal moves as its recording has. It parks only at the recording's end, so recordings must be short and end on the plate. Plan the player's route so it needs the gate after the clone has parked, padding with back-and-forth steps.
   **Cue:** a gate opens too late, or a clone ends on a useless cell.
   **Test:** count the recording length against the move number at which you need the gate.
5. **Recording slots wasted or wiped (6 copies).**
   **Believed:** extra rewinds are free, or a bad recording can be discarded.
   **Actually:** the third recording wipes the first two. A rewind with no moves does nothing but still costs an action. A level reset is the only clean fix.
   **Cue:** the top-left counter returns to its start state.
   **Test:** read the counter row before each rewind and count how many bodies you still have.
6. **Patroller modelled wrongly or crushed (6 copies).**
   **Believed:** it mirrors the player's direction, or it can be walked around.
   **Actually:** it advances one track cell per legal player move, with fixed turn rules, in a region the player cannot enter. A gate closing on it crushes it, and it stays stopped until ACTION5 rewinds and revives it.
   **Cue:** it stays put during refused moves and bounces off closed gates.
   **Test:** make several legal moves in different directions and log its position per move. Pin the gates on its route with clones first.
7. **Direction slips and miscounted batches (6 copies).**
   **Believed:** up/down were swapped, or a batch had enough moves.
   **Actually:** refused moves cost budget and do not advance clones or patrollers, so one wrong key breaks timing.
   **Cue:** a frame with almost no change.
   **Test:** recount each batch against the route before sending it.

## Breakthroughs
- Level 1 was cracked by recording a short trip that ends on the plate, then walking the real route while the clone holds it.
- On the chain levels, each recording was made to end on one plate it must hold. The shortest routes of clone and player were built to coincide, with even-length detours for slack.
- For teleporters, copies made the clone's recording length equal the player's route length to the ring, so arrival and press coincide.
- For patrollers, copies recorded short clones to hold the gates on its route early, simulated its cycle, and solved for the player's windows. They treated it as a remote presser.

## Lessons that carry to other games
- If a "reset" button changes nothing visible, check whether it records and replays what you just did.
- An object that acts on your behalf may only advance when you take a legal step, so illegal moves cost budget but do not advance time.
- Check whether a trigger is level-based (held) or edge-based (fires on arrival) before trusting it. A replayed path re-fires edge triggers.
- A bounded set of helpers is spent by each save. Count the slots before the third use.
- When an effect needs two things to coincide, make the path lengths equal by construction rather than hoping to wait.
- An autonomous agent can be a tool; log its position per legal step to find its rule and period.
