# KA59 — Knock blocks into frames
Keywords: push, impact transfer, sliding on ice, timed bomb, blast shockwave, hollow target frames, shape matching, all-targets-required, sealed regions, click to select, action budget, partial-overlap push, charge on blocked moves
Looks like: A small board with green 3×3-multiple blocks, hollow dark frames, and large pale-purple regions or walls. Arrows move the selected block one cell (3px). A click selects another block. Later levels add yellow plus-shaped blocks and maroon/orange 6×6 or 3×3 blocks whose orange rows fill with each move. A counter bar at the bottom drains.

## Rules
- Every frame must hold a block of exactly its shape, inset 1px. Filling only some frames never clears. The check runs once an action has fully resolved.
- Only ordinary blocks can be selected. Yellow special blocks and bombs cannot be selected; they can only be struck.
- Moving into another object leaves the mover in place. The struck object, and anything it hits, is pushed up to 5 cells. Walls stop it. Frames never affect motion.
- Large pale-purple regions are ice. The block you steer cannot step onto ice. A struck or blasted object can enter ice and keeps sliding until it is off the ice or hits something.
- A push needs only the mover's destination to overlap the target, even at a corner or notch. A push that is fully blocked changes nothing visible.
- Bombs are pushable objects. Every movement action charges every bomb one row; blocked moves count and clicks do not. A full bomb fires a shockwave as wide as itself, about 3 cells long, and pushes anything in it away from the muzzle (about 9/6/3px, nearer is stronger). It then resets and cycles forever. The muzzle side is invisible at zero charge. Bombs can also ride-shove the controlled block across ice.
- An old velocity table is re-run after each explosion, so objects still on ice may drift again.
- Each level has a fixed action budget (100–200). Clicks and blocked moves cost actions. There is no undo.

## Levels
- L1: two rooms split by an ice strip; a bump sends a block across. Both frames are required.
- L2 (hard: cleared 13/13, median 53 min): four block shapes, mostly ice, one frame each. Chained bumps and ice slides move blocks between sealed rooms.
- L3: yellow plus-shaped special blocks and plus-shaped frames, no ice. Push them from their corner notches.
- L4 (cleared 11/13, median 28 min; 1 stuck copy at 35 min): an ice band splits the map. A block must be bumped through, then clicked on the far side to take control.
- L5 (cleared 10/11, median 18 min): first bombs. A bomb plugs an ice-walled channel, and another fires across the goal frame.
- L6 (hard: cleared 10/10, median 67 min): three bombs at zero charge, and two sealed top halves. The blast is the only way across the ice band.
- L7 (hard: cleared 3/10, median 117 min; 6 stuck copies, 100–303 min): large and small blocks, a plus block, and two bombs. Regions connect only by bumps and blasts, and the plus starts sealed in a corner room.

## Blind spots — where our player lost time
1. **L7 "unsolvable".**
   **Believed:** no block can reach the sealed corner room, so the plus can't be moved, or one block must be sacrificed there.
   **Actually:** bombs are pushable. Bump a bomb along its row so its blast column covers the target, then push from a notch.
   **Cue:** a bomb in the same area as an unreachable block.
   **Test:** bump the bomb once, wait for the next explosion, and see where its blast now lands. (4 copies)
2. **Partial fills.**
   **Believed:** one filled frame, or every plain frame, was enough.
   **Actually:** every frame, including the odd-shaped ones, must be filled at the same time.
   **Cue:** the block sits in the frame but nothing clears.
   **Test:** count the frames and the pieces. A leftover frame is a leftover piece. (8 copies)
3. **Slide endpoints explained by invented rules** ("fixed 15", "goal snap", "ring snap", "pusher size + 2").
   **Actually:** struck objects move up to 5 cells. On ice they keep sliding until off it. Only walls stop them early.
   **Cue:** objects always end just past an ice band or against a wall.
   **Test:** strike one block from two offsets across the same ice and see whether the end point tracks the ice edge. (5 copies)
4. **Bomb model wrong or mistimed.**
   **Believed:** the blast aims at the active piece, or it is a decorative "pulse".
   **Actually:** the muzzle is fixed and the blast is a fixed column. Blocked moves also charge it.
   **Cue:** orange rows grow one per move.
   **Test:** park a block in the column and press into a wall until the fill wraps.
   In L5 the blast knocked the block back out of the frame on the arrival action. Arrive one action earlier or later. (6 copies)
5. **"Nothing happened" read as "not pushable", plus over-deduction.**
   **Believed:** a pusher must face the target square on; a press with no change meant an inert object.
   **Actually:** corner or notch overlap pushes, and a blocked push shows no change.
   **Test:** spend one action on the suspicious press before 30 minutes of planning. (3 copies)
6. **Key mapping.**
   **Believed:** ACTION2 is up.
   **Actually:** ACTION2 is down.
   **Test:** one throwaway move per level. (5 copies)

## Breakthroughs
- **L7:** the three clears pushed a bomb sideways in 15px hops until its blast column hit the plus. The blast carried the plus out of the sealed room. A blocker then pushed it down from a west nook, and one block shoved the other through the ice band.
- **L6:** copies that cleared rode the blast across the ice band. They stood touching the bomb for the longest shove and burned charge with blocked moves to time it. Parking inside the column first "cracked the level".
- **L4/L3:** push a plus block from the empty corner notches of its bounding box. Send one block through the ice, click it to take control there, and fill that block's own frame last.
- **L5:** remove the plugging bomb by pushing it away instead of walking through its blast.

## Lessons that carry to other games
- If a level looks unsolvable, a "static" object is probably movable. Test pushing the hazards or helpers themselves.
- When nothing clears after one target is filled, count the targets again.
- Don't invent a rule for a landing point. Map where it stops against the terrain before building a plan.
- A timer that ticks on refused moves can be advanced for free. Check what an action does when it is blocked.
- Objects may follow different rules when you drive them and when they are struck. Check both.
- An action with zero visible change may be a blocked version of a working action. Probe before declaring something inert.
