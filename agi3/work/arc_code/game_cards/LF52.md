# LF52 — Peg-solitaire jumps with rail carts

Keywords: peg solitaire, jump capture, rail carts, ferry between boards, scrolling camera, pillars as stepping stones, immortal helper piece, off-screen world, solvability dead-end, global arrow keys, last-survivor count, parity

Looks like: Light-blue screen with one or more small boards of off-white square cells with white borders holding green plus-shaped pieces. Click a piece to select it, then click a ring to jump. From level 2 on, thin black rails carry orange-and-yellow carts, and ACTION1-4 move them. Level 1 has no rails and the arrows do nothing. ACTION7 (Undo) exists. Later levels add purple pillars and red or blue pieces. The world is wider than the 64×64 view.

## Rules
- A move is a two-click jump: select a piece, then click a ring. The piece jumps orthogonally over an adjacent piece or pillar into an empty cell and captures it. Green over green captures. Green over a pillar, red, or blue captures nothing.
- Win is reaching the required remainder, usually 1 counted piece. Levels 6 and 7 need 2 (green plus red). From level 8 the blue pieces are not counted.
- ACTION1-4 move every cart one cell along its own rail, all at once. A blocked cart stays put. A piece on a cart rides it. Carts never enter board cells.
- A cart's cell is a legal landing even when the cart is empty. A cart can serve as the jumped-over middle only when it carries a piece or a pillar.
- Bare rail and background are never landings. A pillar is never captured and is never a landing. Red and blue pieces are clickable and jump like green but capture nothing and are never captured.
- The camera scrolls only when a cart carrying a green moves, or when a piece jumps onto certain cells. Empty carts never scroll it. Levels 4 and 9 scroll in both axes, level 8 vertically, levels 3, 5, 6 horizontally, and levels 1, 2, 7, 10 never scroll.
- Dead-end detection exists only in levels 1, 2, 3 and 6, at hardcoded cells. Pieces turn grey and a purple reset key appears. Other levels give no warning, so use Undo. Hitting the budget (64 in level 1, 320 in levels 2-5, 640 in levels 6-10, reset per level) loses.

## Levels
- L1: one L-shaped board, 5 pieces, no rails, budget 64. A built-in hint appears after a blank click.
- L2 (hard: cleared 15/15, median 41 min): first cart. The two boards cannot be solved separately; a piece must jump onto the cart, ride it, and jump off.
- L3 (hard: cleared 13/15, median 103 min; 2 stuck): 14 pieces, 3 boards, 2 carts that move together, horizontal scroll. The two-piece board can only be emptied by an arriving rider.
- L4 (hard: cleared 10/13, median 65 min; 3 stuck): pillars as stepping stones, scrolling in both axes, a lower half hidden at first, 3 carts.
- L5 (hard: cleared 9/10, median 56 min; 1 stuck at 234 min): pillars can ride carts. Pieces at dead ends of 1-wide corridors.
- L6 (hard: cleared 6/9, median 62 min; 3 stuck): first red piece, 2 pieces remain. Sealed rooms.
- L7 (cleared 6/6, median 104 min): 2 green, 1 red, 6 carts, 15 pillars. Pure route planning.
- L8 (hard: cleared 2/6, median 63 min; 3 stuck): blue pieces, vertical scroll, separate rail networks.
- L9 (hard: cleared 1/2, median 24 min; 1 stuck): one cart on a long rail, with the second green far to the east.
- L10 (reached by 1, not cleared): 8 carts each carrying a blue piece, some off-screen at the start.

## Blind spots — where our player lost time
1. **Believed:** each board, or each room, should be reduced to a single piece, or the boards solved independently. **Actually:** the total across all boards must reach the target, and an isolated piece can only be removed by a rider arriving on a cart. **Cue:** an orange block sits on a black line between two boards, and the arrows did nothing in level 1. **Test:** press one arrow and see whether the orange block slides. Then park it next to a piece and select a neighbouring piece to see whether the cart's cell offers a ring.
2. **Believed:** the loss rule is "no legal jump left". Others tried "the moved piece is stranded". **Actually:** loss is a hardcoded solvability check (levels 1, 2, 3, 6 only), and it fires even while other jumps exist. Moves that park a piece where nothing can ever reach it were the usual trigger. **Cue:** all pieces turn grey and a purple key appears. **Test:** press Undo and try a different capture order. Treat the grey flash as an oracle for "this jump loses". A key appearing in levels 4, 5, 7, 8 and 9 is not expected.
3. **Believed:** a piece at the dead end of a 1-wide corridor, or inside a sealed room, can never be removed, so the level is unsolvable. **Actually:** such a piece can be the jumper; bring a partner next to it and let it jump. A piece or cart carrying a piece or pillar can serve as the middle at the doorway. **Cue:** the pieces beyond have nowhere to land. **Test:** ask who could stand next to it, and whether a carried helper could be the middle.
4. **Believed:** the first view is the whole level, or the world is closed. **Actually:** the world extends past the view, and empty carts never scroll it. **Cue:** frame cut-offs at the screen edge, or a rail running off-screen. **Test:** ride a green on a cart a few steps. Treat the board coordinates as invalid after any slide.
5. **Believed:** in levels 6-9 the red or blue pieces and the pillars are obstacles, the count is "1 green", or the other green can never be reached, so the win must be something else. **Actually:** the red or blue piece is a portable stepping stone. Red-green leapfrog moves a pair along a corridor, and a cart carrying the helper can be a middle. Jumping preserves cell parity, so only riding a cart changes a piece's class. **Cue:** pieces that cannot approach each other because of parity, and a second rail network that looks disconnected. **Test:** try leapfrog with the helper; look for a cart whose rail ends next to a piece you can jump over onto another cart.

## Breakthroughs
- Work backward from the isolated board: the last jump must come from the carried rider. Clear everything else first, and ferry the last piece to the isolated region.
- The helper (red or blue) plus a cart carrying it gave a mobile middle. Parking the loaded cart at a doorway let the green jump into a sealed room.
- Bridging networks: a rider on one cart jumps over a piece standing between two rail ends and lands on a second cart, then rides on. In level 9 the second green had to be walked through a long chain of pillars to the one cell next to the rail.
- Park a cart on the landing cell before the jump that needs it.

## Lessons that carry to other games
- If a goal looks unreachable, ask whether the "frozen" pieces can be the active ones. The immobile object can be the one that acts.
- A global input that "does nothing" in one level may be the whole mechanic in the next; probe it again in new levels.
- Check whether the world is larger than the visible window, and what makes the camera move. Hidden regions can hold pieces needed for the count.
- Treat a loss flash as a free oracle if undo is available, and use it to search over orders instead of reasoning about hidden rules.
- A helper object that is never consumed may serve as a stepping stone. Check which carrier states count as a middle versus a landing.
- Check the win count carefully: which piece types are included in "remaining", and are any of them uncapturable.
