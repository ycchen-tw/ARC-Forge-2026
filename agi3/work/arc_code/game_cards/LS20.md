# LS20 — Transform attributes to match target

Keywords: attribute matching, state-changing stations, cyclic operators, maze, one-way launchers, patrolling objects, step budget, refills, multiple targets, fog of war, HUD readout, reset

Looks like: A 64×64 top-down maze of 5×5 cells with a small block, a few target tiles, and wall-colour corridors. There are only four move actions (up/down/left/right). A panel at the bottom-left shows a small stamp, and each target carries a stamp of its own. A yellow bar at the bottom shows steps, and three red squares show lives.

## Rules
- The block's shape, colour and orientation are readable only in the bottom-left stamp, not on the block. A target tile can be entered only when that stamp matches the target's stamp in all three. Otherwise the move is blocked and the screen flashes.
- Stations change one attribute by +1 in a fixed cycle each time the block enters their cell. There are 6 shapes, 4 colours and 4 orientations. Stations are reusable, so you step off and back on to fire again. Players read the shape operator as a fixed per-level list of shapes, and reading it by pressing was reliable.
- One-way launchers throw the block along the arrow until the next wall or target tile. They fire on entering the cell. Stations and refills at the landing cell work, but a win is not checked on landing, so the last move into a target must be a normal one.
- The step bar holds 42 and each charged move costs 1 or 2, depending on the level. Reaching 0 is still playable, and the next charged move costs a life (3 lives, which also resets the level). Bumping a target is free, but bumping a wall is charged. Ring items refill the bar fully, are free to collect, and vanish until the level resets.
- A lost life resets the level, including attributes, refills and targets. Deliberately burning a life is a legal way to restart.
- From level 5, some stations patrol fixed tracks and advance one cell after each successful move. A blocked move leaves them in place. The block must land on the cell the station occupies after its move.
- A completed target disappears but stays counted, and all targets must be completed. Level 7 has fog: everything beyond about 4 cells is painted the same black as target interiors, while the bottom panel stays visible.

## Levels
- L1: orientation only; a one-time hint flash appears when the stamps match.
- L2: orientation only, a longer maze, and two refills (hard: cleared 12/12, median 38 min).
- L3: colour plus orientation, with the first launchers (hard: cleared 12/12, median 45 min).
- L4: shape plus colour, with 8 launchers and no orientation station (cleared 12/12, median 27 min).
- L5: all three attributes plus a patrolling orientation station (hard: cleared 9/12, median 38 min, 1 stuck copy at 43 min).
- L6: two targets with different stamps, three patrolling stations, and a launcher that is the only way into the target corridor (hard: cleared 8/9, median 59 min).
- L7: fog, one patrolling station, 6 refills (one unreachable decoy), and a launcher that is the only way into one half of the maze (hard: cleared 8/8, median 74 min).

## Blind spots — where our player lost time
1. **Colour must match too (9 copies).**
   **Believed:** matching the shape in the HUD was enough to open the target.
   **Actually:** shape, colour and orientation must all match. L3 starts with the wrong colour.
   **Cue:** the target is refused with the right shape and the panel flashes.
   **Test:** compare the panel colour with the target stamp colour before approaching. A free bump at the target settles it.
2. **Every station is the same kind of "rotation" (10 copies).**
   **Believed:** each station rotates the stamp, or acts differently depending on the direction of entry, or a press overwrites everything else.
   **Actually:** the orientation station rotates, the shape station steps a fixed list of shapes (cell count can change), and the colour station cycles colours. Rotation is preserved across shape presses. The target is usually a shape from the list plus rotations, so both stations are needed.
   **Cue:** the target has a different number of lit cells than the start, so no rotation can reach it.
   **Test:** press each station 1–6 times and read the stamp after each press. The stuck L5 copy kept pressing one shape station hoping the target would appear.
3. **Stations move (7 copies).**
   **Believed:** stations are fixed, so stepping on the spot where one was seen presses it.
   **Actually:** they patrol and advance only on successful moves. Bumps freeze them and they blink while under the block.
   **Cue:** a press that worked once does nothing a few moves later, or the object is seen in a new cell.
   **Test:** make two real moves while watching the object's cell. Then follow it step for step to press every move.
4. **First target done is not the win (8 copies).**
   **Believed:** matching the first target clears the level, so the first target was the goal.
   **Actually:** with two targets, the first one only disappears and the other still has to be matched.
   **Cue:** after a match the level did not advance.
   **Test:** after any consumed target, re-read the board for another stamped tile and restart the plan for it.
5. **Budget miscounts (7 copies).**
   **Believed:** the bar cost 1 per move, or the plan was affordable.
   **Actually:** the cost differs per level, wall bumps are charged, and the next charged move after 0 costs a life.
   **Cue:** the bar drops faster than expected.
   **Test:** measure the cost over 2 moves on every new level, and plan legs that fit between refills. A reset is cheaper than a continuation that cannot finish.
6. **Launcher misreads (7 copies).**
   **Believed:** launchers behave like walls or only fire when you move toward them, and a launch can finish the level.
   **Actually:** they fire on entering the cell from any side, can't be rested on, never deliver you onto a target, and a slide does not re-fire launchers it passes. The last step into a target is always a normal move. Some regions are reachable only by a launch.
   **Cue:** a wall-looking light line, or a long move from one step.
   **Test:** step onto the suspect cell once and see where you land.
7. **Fog hides the map (4 copies).**
   **Believed:** hand-planned routes over remembered scenery would hold.
   **Actually:** the fog is the same black as the target interiors. Cells re-fog when you leave, so the map must be accumulated.
   **Cue:** large black regions near the player.
   **Test:** stitch a map from every frame and have a program plan the route.

## Breakthroughs
- Copies that wrote a simulator or search over position, attributes, refills, bar and patrol phase found the shortest route. They validated it against recorded steps before sending batches.
- Counting lit cells and noting that rotation cannot change them showed a station was a different operator. Searching all (shape index, rotation) pairs found the unique pair that reaches the target, which fixed the order of stations.
- Shadowing a patrolling station gave a press on every move.
- Resetting when the remaining bar could not cover the remaining route beat losing a life or grinding on.

## Lessons that carry to other games
- Read the state display after every action. Check whether each station changes the same attribute and whether a sequence of presses matches the target.
- Check whether two objects of a similar shape share an operator before assuming they do. The same-looking object can mean different things on different levels.
- If an object seems to move or blink, count real moves before planning around it.
- A goal that disappears may not be the last goal. Look for another target before assuming the level is over.
- Re-measure every cost on each new level, and check whether a failed move is charged.
- When a move ends in a launch, check where the launch lands and whether the final step must be taken normally.
- Reset when the remaining resources cannot cover the plan.
