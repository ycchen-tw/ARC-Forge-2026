# M0R0 — Mirrored twin avatars, break symmetry

Keywords: mirrored controls, two avatars must meet, symmetry breaking, pinning against a wall, hatched lethal cells, movable blocks selected by click, remote-controlled obstacle, colour buttons and gates, hold-to-open gate, per-level control flip, action budget, level reset on trap

Looks like: A top-down maze split into a left and right half with different wall colours. Two identical small squares sit one per half, and the maze is a mirror image about the centre line. Arrow keys move both squares at once. ACTION5 does nothing, and ACTION6 (click) is inert at first but becomes live from level 3. A bar along the top and bottom edges ticks with each action.

## Rules
- One arrow press moves both avatars. Vertical moves are shared. Horizontal moves are mirrored for one avatar, and which avatar that is stays fixed for the whole level. In level 5 the avatar that starts on the right is the one that follows the raw key, so the sense flips.
- An avatar that hits a wall stays put while the other still moves. Avatars never block each other, and traps and buttons do not block them. Walls, closed gates and movable blocks do. Blocked moves still cost an action.
- Win: both avatars end an action on the same cell. There is also a fallback: if they were horizontally adjacent on the previous step and one moves into the other's old cell, they merge. A vertical swap does not count, and adjacency alone does nothing.
- Hatched (red/black checkerboard) cells are traps. Stepping on one is allowed. It blinks for about 8 frames, then everything returns to its level-start position with no refund. Solid red wall colour is just a wall.
- Small pushable blocks (a blue dot) are moved by clicking them anywhere on the board, at any distance.
  - While one is selected, the avatars turn off-white and freeze, and the arrow keys move only that block, not mirrored.
  - A click on any non-block cell inside the board deselects. A click outside the board does nothing.
  - A block is solid to avatars and cannot be pushed onto a trap or a button. It can pass through an open gate.
- Buttons and gates come in colours. While any avatar stands on a button, all gates of that colour vanish and look like floor. When it leaves, they return. A gate can close on an avatar standing in it, which is harmless, and the avatar can walk out later.
- There is a budget of 150 actions per level. The edge bar is only a step counter.

## Levels
- L1: Plain maze. The two halves join only along a top corridor. (cleared 12/12, median 28 min, mostly learning the mirror and the win rule)
- L2: Adds hatched trap cells in bands and a central column. The halves join only through two lower rows.
- L3: Three pushable dots plug the chokepoints, and all three must be parked in dead ends before the avatars can meet. (hard: cleared 12/12, median 44 min, 72 actions)
- L4: One dot plugs the only crossing, and traps cap the rooms. There is no wall to stop a vertical move, so the dot must also serve as a stopper. (cleared 12/12, median 12 min)
- L5: Colour buttons and gates, with the control sign inverted. (cleared 12/12, median 25 min)
- L6: No walls, with traps as walls, four rooms, crossed-colour buttons and gates, and a dot plugging the only crossing. (hard: cleared 11/12, median 34 min)

## Blind spots — where our player lost time
1. **Hatched cells treated as walls.**
   **Believed:** hatched cells just block, so a search over safe moves can aim at them.
   **Actually:** they are enterable and lethal, and the level resets with no refund. A hatched cell can never pin an avatar.
   **Cue:** a checkerboard texture unlike the solid walls, repeated on every trap level.
   **Test:** treat it as fatal in your own planner, and never lean on it as a stopper.
2. **Win condition misread as a position or a crossing.**
   **Believed:** the goal was swapping cells, changing sides, exchanging left/right order, or being edge-adjacent.
   **Actually:** the goal is the same cell. The adjacent swap only works horizontally, and ACTION5 never registers a clear.
   **Cue:** adjacency produced no clear. Several copies thought the clear came "on the next action".
   **Test:** try one same-cell merge, not only a head-on swap.
3. **Control sign assumed constant.**
   **Believed:** the left/right mapping from earlier levels carries over, or follows the avatar's current side.
   **Actually:** it is tied to the avatar's identity, and in L5 the sign is inverted. In L6 it is back to the L1–L4 sense.
   **Cue:** the first press moves the wrong way.
   **Test:** press one horizontal key at the start of each level and watch both squares.
4. **Click assumed inert, and the dot's role missed.**
   **Believed:** ACTION6 does nothing, as shown on L1–L2.
   **Actually:** from L3 a click selects a movable block and freezes the avatars. These blocks are the whole puzzle, since they must be parked off the critical cells or used as stoppers.
   **Cue:** small blue 2×2 or 3×3 dots sitting in corridor chokepoints, and a maze that BFS says is unsolvable.
   **Test:** click the dot once, then press an arrow.
5. **Coupling invariant not recognised.**
   **Believed:** the avatars can be steered freely, or any plan avoiding the dot would work.
   **Actually:** the sum and difference of their coordinates stay fixed unless exactly one avatar is blocked. Only a wall, a closed gate or a parked dot breaks the lock. Parity matters too, because a head-on merge needs an odd gap.
   **Cue:** the search finds only a few dozen reachable joint states.
   **Test:** list every possible stopper, and make sure your search key tells states apart.
6. **Gate model wrong or over-feared.**
   **Believed:** a button is used up and the gates stay open, or standing in a closing gate might kill.
   **Actually:** gates are open only while someone stands on the button, and standing in a closing gate is harmless and is a useful parking trick. Standing on a gate cell does not hold it open.
   **Cue:** gate cells reappear the moment the holder steps off.
   **Test:** step off a button and watch the bars.

## Breakthroughs
- Park each movable block in a dead end of the maze so it no longer cuts the route. A search over parking sets found only one working set.
- Use a parked dot or a wall as a stopper against one avatar. A pressed key then moves only the partner, which is how one avatar holds a button while the other crosses the gate. This is how L4 and L6 were won.
- Compute the invariants before searching, then hunt for the move that breaks them. Settle "impossible" verdicts with an exhaustive search that includes the death rule.

## Lessons that carry to other games
- Re-derive the control mapping at each new level. Which object follows the raw key can flip.
- A texture that differs from the walls may be lethal rather than solid, so plan as if it kills until a test shows otherwise.
- When two things are driven by one input, look for what stays constant and for exactly which events can break it: a wall, a closed gate, or a movable obstacle.
- Re-test actions that were inert earlier whenever a new kind of object appears.
- A goal can be an event as well as a state. Distinguish "adjacent", "swapped" and "overlapping" with separate cheap tests.
- When a search says "no solution", suspect the model or its state key before you suspect the level.
- A mechanic that holds something open while you stand on it often leaves a free parking spot. Weigh an untested edge case against the cost of a longer plan.
