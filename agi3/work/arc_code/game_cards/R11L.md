# R11L — Move legs; body follows their centroid

Keywords: click-only, selection state, centroid of controlled pieces, derived object scored, hazard rewind with mistake counter, sum constraint, color absorption, decoy targets, color-set matching, bounding-box hit area, simultaneous goals, action budget

Looks like: A 64×64 cave with gray walls, one or more small diamond "legs" (one drawn white, selected, the rest gray) and a 5×5 rounded body joined to its legs by dotted lines. Dashed ring-shaped X targets sit elsewhere. The only action is a click at x,y. Column 0 is a shrinking action bar. Later levels add light-blue hazard blobs, multicolor bodies, decoy targets, and loose half-colored fragments on the floor.

## Rules
- Only legs are controllable. Body top-left = floor(mean of its legs' top-lefts), so a leg moving by d shifts the body by about d/n. The body itself is never clicked or steered.
- A click inside any leg's 5×5 bounding rectangle selects that leg, and this costs an action. Any other click teleports the selected leg there, with no path check. The selection persists, so the next move click moves the same leg unless you re-select.
- A leg whose solid pixels would land on a wall (color 2) is rejected, but the action is still spent. A body touching a hazard (large color-10 region) rewinds the move with a 23-frame bounce and adds a mistake. The fifth mistake in a level loses. Legs may stand on hazards.
- All real targets must be satisfied at the same time. A body only has to touch a target (including its invisible interior), not sit on its centre. Satisfying one target early does not clear the level.
- Levels 4–6 have decoy targets. In levels 5–6, a body matches only a target whose set of positive colors equals its own.
- Levels 5–6 bodies start blank. Touching a loose fragment removes it and stamps its colors onto the body at the same cell indices, and later fragments overwrite earlier ones. Colors are therefore order-dependent.
- Budget is 59 usable clicks per level. Color 10 means hazard in some levels and plain paint or decoration in others.

## Levels
- L1: one body, two legs, one target. Learn the midpoint rule.
- L2: two bodies (3 legs and 2 legs) and one hazard blob. Every body must reach its own target (hard: cleared 12/12, median 41 min).
- L3: a 4-leg body and a 2-leg body. A large hazard forces the bodies to travel around it (hard: cleared 12/12, median 30 min).
- L4: three bodies with multicolor patterns, 8 targets of which 5 are decoys, and fake X decorations. The matching target has the same color set (cleared 12/12, median 22 min).
- L5: blank bodies, four fragments and decoys. Each body must absorb two fragments and then touch the target with exactly that color set (hard: cleared 12/12, median 39 min).
- L6: nine fragments, with distractors that must not be touched. Each body needs three fragments, and one target is impossible (hard: cleared 10/12, median 37 min).

## Blind spots — where our player lost time
1. **Invented rejection and loss theories (L2–L3).**
   **Believed:** moves were refused for crossing a ring, for a rope over water, or because of a "6-action budget"; a lost game was "hub on water".
   **Actually:** a body on a hazard adds a counted mistake and rewinds, and the fifth mistake ends the game. Legs only care about walls, and the path is never checked.
   **Cue:** the 23-frame bounce where only the action bar changes.
   **Test:** repeat one rejected click after changing a single factor, and count how many bounces have happened. Do not fit geometry to one or two rejections (6 copies).
2. **Fragments (L5–L6).**
   **Believed:** the body must land exactly on a fragment's centre. This forced about 6 clicks per waypoint, and unwanted fragments got brushed by accident.
   **Actually:** any contact absorbs the fragment, and it overwrites existing colors.
   **Cue:** a fragment vanishes when a body only partly overlaps it.
   **Test:** park the body 2 cells off a fragment and watch whether it disappears. Route past distractors only through stops that touch nothing (6 copies).
3. **Click on a leg's box selects it.**
   **Believed:** only the 13-cell diamond selects.
   **Actually:** the whole 5×5 box selects, so a move destination inside another leg's box silently becomes a select.
   **Cue:** a 1-frame, ~25-cell change instead of a move.
   **Test:** before sending a move, check that the destination is more than 2 cells (Chebyshev distance) from every leg centre (6 copies).
4. **Targets need an exact centre (L3–L5).**
   **Believed:** the body centre must equal the target centre.
   **Actually:** touching suffices, and the target flashes on contact.
   **Cue:** in L1 the body 3 cells off still cleared the level.
   **Test:** try a 2–3 cell offset first; it saves moves (4 copies).
5. **Color 10 and wall masks (L3–L6).**
   **Believed:** color 10 is always water, and terrain can be built by unioning frames.
   **Actually:** per level it is hazard, paint or ring color, and clear-animation frames pollute the masks.
   **Cue:** a "hazard" that is only 10 cells and coincides with a fragment or ring arc.
   **Test:** take the terrain from the first clean board of the level, then probe one suspicious cell (4 copies).
6. **Scored object is not the controlled one (L1).**
   **Believed:** moving a leg onto the target scores it.
   **Actually:** the centroid body must touch the target.
   **Cue:** the dashed pattern matches the body's shape, not the leg's.
   **Test:** put a leg on the target and watch for the clear animation (9 copies).
7. **Stale selection.**
   **Believed:** the next click would move another leg.
   **Actually:** the last-moved leg stays selected, so a "move" click moves it again.
   **Cue:** one leg is drawn white.
   **Test:** read which leg is white before every batch (2 copies).

## Breakthroughs
- Solving by sums: choose leg positions whose sum equals n × target. The last leg's position is forced, and every leg that must travel costs a select plus a move (3 copies).
- Decoy targets are those whose color set matches no body, or needs a color no fragment supplies (4 copies).
- Fragments overlap on shared cells, so absorption order matters for the final colors (3 copies).

## Lessons that carry to other games
- Check whether the scored object is the one you click or one derived from it; a quick probe of the derived object settles it.
- A click's hit area can exceed the drawn shape, so keep destinations out of other objects' boxes.
- A failed move that bounces back may feed a hidden counter. Count bounces and avoid theories built on one or two cases.
- Look for the loosest success condition (contact, not exact alignment) before paying for precision.
- A color or symbol's meaning may change per level; rebuild terrain from clean frames.
- When pickups accumulate, the order of collection and unintended contacts along the route can change the result.
