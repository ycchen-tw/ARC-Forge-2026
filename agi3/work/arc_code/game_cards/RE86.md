# RE86 — Reshape and recolor wireframes onto markers
Keywords: coverage goal, color matching, recolor pads, reshape device, elastic shape, rigid blocker, shape switching, hidden objects, refused move, aspect-ratio change, route planning, last-touch-wins

Looks like: A 64×64 board shows several movable thin-line shapes (crosses, X's, diamonds, boxes, one bare line) and scattered 3×3 markers whose gray ring has a one-cell colored center. Only ACTION1-4 (move the selected shape by 3 cells) and ACTION5 (select the next shape) exist; there is no click. Later boards add framed color squares (recoloring pads) and off-white blocks with a hole (reshapers), plus a step bar along the bottom edge.

## Rules
- Win: every marker's center cell must be covered by a shape cell of the same color. The check uses the topmost pixel after compositing shapes in list order. The gray ring is ignored, shapes pass through each other, and nothing is consumed.
- Markers can start hidden under a shape, so the first frame may show fewer markers than exist, or a wrong center color.
- ACTION1-4 move only the selected shape (a white dot marks its center). ACTION5 cycles the selection in a fixed order. Only the shape's center point is bounded by the board edge, so shapes may hang off-screen. Every action costs one step, including a switch, a bump, or a failed reshape. The last step can still win.
- Recolorer (framed square, border color 2): any contact recolors the whole shape, permanently, within one action. A shape already of that color is unaffected. If it touches several recolorers of other colors at once, the earliest in the hidden sprite list wins, which cannot be read from the screen.
- Reshaper (off-white block with a hole): the only thing that changes proportions. A box or diamond pushed into it gets 3 shorter along the push axis and 3 longer across. If that side is ≤6 the press fails and the shape bounces back (a refused press that costs a step). A cross keeps its bounding box and one bar slides inside it, so the bars cannot decouple beyond the box.
- A reset after bad deformations is possible (some copies used RESET), but reshaping has no undo.
- Each level resets all shapes, recolors and step budget.

## Levels
- L1: two crosses, each aligned to the four markers of its own color.
- L2: three shape families (diamond, cross, X), one color each.
- L3: all shapes and markers one color, so only the union of coverage matters; the thin line appears here.
- L4: recolorers appear, and coverage in the wrong color does not win (hard: cleared 12/12, median 27 min).
- L5: five recolorers; two markers start hidden under a cross (hard: cleared 12/12, median 28 min).
- L6: the reshaper appears; a box and a cross must be reshaped (hard: cleared 11/12, median 61 min, 1 stuck copy at 55 min).
- L7: recoloring plus reshaping, with three shapes including a long cross (hard: cleared 9/11, median 67 min).
- L8: two boxes, 14 recolorers in a row across the board, 2 reshapers. Both boxes must be recolored and reshaped to exact sizes (hard: cleared 8/9, median 58 min, 1 stuck copy at 27 min).

## Blind spots — where our player lost time
1. **Shapes treated as rigid / reshaping modelled wrongly (L6-L8).**
   **Believed:** shapes only translate, or a blocked shape just stays put, or the growth side is predictable.
   **Actually:** pushing a box into the reshaper trades length for width, and a cross's bar slides inside a fixed bounding box. This is the only way to change proportions, and the targets are only reachable that way.
   **Cue:** the markers of one color form a rectangle or cross that no shape at its current size can cover.
   **Test:** press once into the block and compare shape size before and after; re-measure after every press.
2. **Covering everything in the wrong color (L4, L5).**
   **Believed:** L1-L3 only needed coverage, so coverage by any shape wins.
   **Actually:** the marker's color must match the covering cell.
   **Cue:** all markers are covered and nothing happens; marker colors are not among the shapes' colors.
   **Test:** with every marker covered, check whether the shape colors match. One mismatched cover that does not clear settles it.
3. **Markers hidden under a shape (L5, 5 copies).**
   **Believed:** the colored cell visible at a marker is the marker's own color.
   **Actually:** a shape drawn over a marker shows its own color there, so some markers were misread or missed entirely, and solvers "covered everything" without winning.
   **Cue:** a marker position sits inside a shape's footprint, or the count of visible markers looks low.
   **Test:** move the covering shape one step away and look at what is revealed.
4. **Touching several recolorers at once, and what counts as contact (L4-L8).**
   **Believed:** the last or rightmost one wins, or only the colored interior counts.
   **Actually:** the winner among simultaneous contacts is hidden list order.
   **Cue:** a recolor animation fires and the resulting color is not the one expected.
   **Test:** plan routes touching exactly one recolorer near the end, and verify the color after each contact.
5. **Cross bars assumed independent (L7).**
   **Believed:** the two bars could be pulled apart arbitrarily.
   **Actually:** the crossing must stay inside the fixed bounding box, so a decoupled route is refused.
   **Cue:** a computed route freezes with zero cells changed.
   **Test:** try the first move that would separate the bars and diff the frame.
6. **Refused presses and per-shape state (L8, costliest).**
   **Believed:** a no-op press means "try again"; the growth side alternates globally.
   **Actually:** presses at the size limit change nothing but still cost a step, and each shape tracks its own growth side. Early deformation can also strand a shape (a box too wide to cross the recolorer row).
   **Cue:** the same press twice with zero cells changed, or a box ending off from the predicted position after the second shape.
   **Test:** repeat a press once and diff; replay recorded steps through a simulator to find the first mismatch.
7. **Losing track of the active shape or direction (L2-L5).**
   **Believed:** the white dot is always visible and ACTION2 means up.
   **Actually:** another shape can hide the dot, and ACTION2 is down.
   **Cue:** a press changes zero cells, or the wrong shape moves.
   **Test:** press ACTION5 and re-read which center is white before a long batch.

## Breakthroughs
- Derive the forced final shape from conserved totals: for an outline with fixed cell count, only one rectangle passes through four markers, so the target is computed rather than searched.
- Deliberately push a box against the reshaper to reach the needed aspect ratio, then move it rigidly.
- Build a simulator and replay every recorded step to expose the first mismatch (this exposed the per-shape growth side in L8).
- In L8, flatten the box to the minimum thickness before crossing the recolorer row through its only clean gap, recolor last with a single contact, then widen again at the second reshaper.
- A wrong recolor on the way is harmless if the intended one is touched later.

## Lessons that carry to other games
- If a win condition fits earlier levels with a looser reading, check on the first level where colors differ whether the stricter reading (matching property) is required.
- Anything drawn over an object can hide its true state; reveal it by moving the cover before trusting what is on screen.
- When an action has an effect on the player's own object and a different one on what it hits, test both separately before planning routes.
- A zero-change response to an action is information, not noise: repeat once, diff, then change the plan instead of looping.
- When several triggers fire at once, the outcome may be hidden state; prefer routes that fire exactly one.
- If a target cannot be reached with the shape's current form, look for a device that changes the form, and compute the target from conserved quantities.
