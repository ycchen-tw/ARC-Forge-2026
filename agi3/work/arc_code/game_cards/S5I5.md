# S5I5 — Arm links: extend, rotate, carry subtrees

Keywords: articulated arm, tree of linked bars, parent carries child, slider extend/shorten, rotate button, one control per color, collision rollback, hidden offscreen walls, multiple targets at once, step limit, reach through a gap, fixed-length obstacles

Looks like: A black board with color bars (3 cells thick, each with a dark gray fixed end) and small maroon hand markers. The same maroon color marks the target crosses. Walls (purple) or panels sit at the edges. The only action is a click. Clicking a control panel at the screen edge changes a whole color of bars: left/top half shortens, right/bottom half extends, and from level 6 a "+" box rotates. Clicking bars, hands, targets or background does nothing, but still costs a step.

## Rules
- Only clicks exist. Win when every target has a hand exactly on it at the same moment. Any hand may serve any target. A hand that is already on a target may leave and come back, and one hand home is not enough.
- Each slider controls all links of its color. Extend adds one segment, shorten removes one (minimum 1), and the exact divider cell does nothing. Same-color links always have equal length.
- A rotation button turns all links of its color 90° counter-clockwise about their fixed ends, carrying their whole subtree. A child never lands exactly opposite its parent: that direction is skipped, so a child has only 3 resting directions relative to the parent. Root links have 4.
- Links form a tree fixed by the level data. When a parent extends, shortens or rotates, every descendant (including hands and uncontrolled links) moves with it, in both directions. The descendants include passive links such as off-white bars. They have no controls.
- If a move would make any two links or a wall overlap, the entire action is undone. It still costs a step. A same-color link that is blocked elsewhere blocks the whole click.
- Every click costs 1 step (50–200 per level). The last allowed step can still win.
- Links and walls can extend offscreen. The screen edge does not tell you whether a wall exists beyond it. In level 7 the top edge has a wall only where x ≥ 39, and the left edge only where y ≥ 27.
- In levels 3–7 a hand may sit on a link other than the one you expect. Check which color's slider actually moves it.

## Levels
- L1: two independent bars, two targets. Both must be home together.
- L2 (hard: cleared 14/14, median 45 min): four-link chain, walls, one target. Shortening pulls descendants back as well as extending pushes them.
- L3 (hard: cleared 11/14, median 57 min, 2 stuck): six sliders and two free bars that carry the hands. Two chains of rigid off-white riders sit in the way, along with a fixed wall block between the two targets.
- L4 (hard: cleared 10/11, median 26 min, 1 stuck): one slider drives five yellow links at once. At full length they cover a column, so four of them must be parked in separate bands, three above the hand and one below it.
- L5: mixed chains and a hand that starts on its target. A three-deep rider chain has to be threaded past walls.
- L6: first rotation buttons, a 3-link arm that must enter a walled room through a gap.
- L7 (hard: cleared 6/10, median 157 min, 2 stuck): four-link chain plus two free links as obstacles. Offscreen space is used, and one hand starts home but must be returned.
- L8: a pinwheel hub inside a box with one exit. Only the arm pointing out of the exit can grow long. Riders must be lifted past wall stubs before their parents shorten.

## Blind spots — where our player lost time
1. **Contact-push models instead of a fixed tree** (L2, L3).
   **Believed:** extend pushes whatever it touches, with side/face rules ("only TOP/RIGHT drags", "never backward", "blocks never transmit"); shortening moves nothing. A copy concluded the goal was impossible.
   **Actually:** children are permanently attached and ride the parent in both directions, so shortening pulls them.
   **Cue:** a rider moved when its parent grew; nothing explained why it would stay put on shrink.
   **Test:** grow a parent once, then shorten it once, and watch whether the descendants return.
2. **"Impossible" verdicts from untested assumptions** (L4, L3).
   **Believed:** panels are walls, so only three bands fit above the hand. The stuck L4 copy kept "a mechanic is missing" and listed panel solidity as untested.
   **Actually:** panel frames are only drawn graphics and do not block links. A planner treating them as walls finds no solution.
   **Cue:** a search that proves unsolvability means a model assumption is wrong.
   **Test:** extend a link toward a panel and see whether it is refused.
3. **Rotation skip rule misread** (L7, costliest).
   **Believed:** a blocked rotation is refused, or it tries 180° or 270°, or "skips illegal poses".
   **Actually:** collision rolls the whole action back. The skip applies only to the direction that would fold onto the parent, so a child cycles straight → cw → ccw.
   **Cue:** a click gave 180° or a no-op where a collision was not the cause.
   **Test:** click a child's rotation button repeatedly in open space and record each direction.
4. **Offscreen treated as illegal or as a HUD band** (L6, L7).
   **Believed:** growing or rotating past the edge is refused, and bars must stay fully on screen.
   **Actually:** links keep their true length while clipped. The refusals came from the offscreen wall at the top for x ≥ 39. A chain entirely offscreen is still legal geometry.
   **Cue:** an extension refused near the top edge at a column where the wall exists, but accepted elsewhere.
   **Test:** repeat the same move at a different column, or rotate a link offscreen and click again to see it return.
5. **Blocked same-color link elsewhere** (L4, L8).
   **Believed:** a refused click means the target link is blocked.
   **Actually:** one blocked link of that color cancels the click for all of them (a box wall capped three of the hub arms at 5).
   **Cue:** a refusal while the link you are moving has free space.
   **Test:** locate every link of that color and look for any contact.
6. **Hand on a target is not a win** (L1, L3).
   **Believed:** reaching the first target would clear.
   **Actually:** all targets must be satisfied at once.
   **Cue:** the target ring stays drawn and no animation plays.
   **Test:** count the hands and targets before expecting a clear.

## Breakthroughs
- Treat the pose as a function of one length per slider and one phase per rotation button, search that parameter space with a simulator checked against recorded boards, then order the clicks.
- Lift a parent so its rigid rider clears a wall stub, shorten the child, then lower the parent again (L3, L8).
- L4: the one link that can go below the hand is carried by the parent whose fixed end sits low. Shortening that parent drags it under the hand.
- L7: fold the chain flat so it fits the one-row corridor, slide it with the root link, and unfold at the far end. To free the obstacle, shorten the free link that has a hand home, rotate the obstacle, then re-extend so that hand returns.

## Lessons that carry to other games
- If a piece moves when another is operated, check whether it is attached to it. Test both the extend and the shorten directions before modelling contact rules.
- When a solver proves a level impossible, list the assumptions it uses (what blocks, what is offscreen, what a refusal means) and test the cheapest one.
- A refused action tells you something. Find every object the control affects, not only the one you are watching.
- Check whether an edge or a wall continues beyond the visible frame. A refusal near the border may be an unseen wall, not a rule about borders.
- Verify whether the win needs all goals at once. One goal reached with no clear is information.
- When the same control can be applied again and again, record each resulting pose. Skipped states reveal hidden constraints.
