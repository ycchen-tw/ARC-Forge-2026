# SC25 — Cast rune spells through a maze
Keywords: click-to-cast pattern, spell selected by pattern shape, size toggle, teleport destination by size, ray hits switch, aim by last move, paired switch and door by color, step budget bar, enclosed start, narrow passage, refund pickup, cycling destinations
Looks like: A 64×64 maze with a small square character, an exit at the far end, and a 3×3 grid of clickable cells at the bottom. Small frames (the spellbook) show lit-cell patterns, and a 2-column bar on the right edge drains. Actions are 4 directions plus a click at (x,y). There is no spell key, so spells are cast by lighting the right cells.

## Rules
- The character moves by its own body length: 4 cells at 4×4, 2 cells at 2×2. A bump into a wall still costs a step. Walking into the exit clears the level. The UI panels and the bar are solid obstacles.
- Casting means lighting an exact pattern on the 3×3 grid. The shape alone picks the spell: diamond is resize, a 3-cell corner shape is teleport, the middle column is fireball. An extra or missing lit cell means no cast. Only the spells in this level's spellbook work, and unlocks do not carry over between levels. Hint clicks on the spellbook are free.
- Cost: each cell click costs 1, and the automatic cast costs 1 more. Wall bumps also cost 1. Total steps are capped per level (50/25/50/35/65/60) and you lose only when the cap is exceeded. A color-14 pickup refunds 10 steps.
- Resize toggles 4×4 and 2×2. Shrinking always works and keeps the top-left corner. Growing searches positions up to 2 cells up and left, and ignores constrictions, switches and the exit. If no 4×4 fits, it fails but the full 5 steps are still charged.
- Fireball is a ray in the facing direction, and facing is the last direction pressed (a blocked press counts). It follows the row or column of the character's upper-left corner, not its size. Only walls, closed doors and switches stop it, and it ignores the very dark narrowing marks. Hitting a switch removes it and every door of the same center color. Hitting anything else wastes the cast.
- Teleport lands on a destination matching the current body size. Several same-size destinations cycle in order, and a ring marks the next one. The ring is drawn only while the size matches, so the next large destination is unreadable while small. Teleporting does not trigger the exit or collect pickups.
- Fireball facing and the hint carry across levels. Levels clear state, step count and teleport indices.

## Levels
- L1: only resize. A 2-tall passage blocks the 4×4 body, so it must shrink. The first action is eaten by a hint demo (hard: cleared 12/12, median 24 min).
- L2: only teleport. A narrow constriction blocks the route and a marked destination sits beyond it. Needs 3 clicks and 2 moves (cleared 12/12, median 5 min).
- L3: only fireball. A switch at the end of a corridor opens a door; the ray passes through the constriction (hard: cleared 12/12, median 33 min).
- L4: fireball plus resize. The ray row must line up with a 2-tall corridor, so shrink first. A pickup sits in the room and two spellbook frames move to the upper left (hard: cleared 12/12, median 29 min).
- L5: all three spells. The start room is sealed, so teleport is the only way out. There are two switch and door pairs of different colors, a small and a large destination, and a pickup (hard: cleared 12/12, median 44 min).
- L6: all three spells. Two large destinations cycle, the pickups are hidden under the switches, and the order of unlocks is forced (hard: cleared 9/12, median 45 min).

## Blind spots — where our player lost time
1. **Spell identity.** **Believed:** the pattern's effect is level-specific ("L1 shrank, L2 teleported") or depends on position, or acts as a shortcut that advances the piece. **Actually:** pattern shape selects the spell, and the spellbook lists what is available. **Cue:** the small frames each show a different lit pattern, and the effect changes when the pattern shape changes. **Test:** cast two different shapes in the same spot and compare the effects. Complete a pattern one cell at a time and watch for exact-match behavior (an extra lit cell blocks the cast).
2. **Fireball aim and what it hits.** **Believed:** it is a "push", "dash" or "ghost" that moves the piece and reverts, "destroys maroon" or "all colour d", and that pressing toward a wall is harmless. **Actually:** a ray in the last-pressed direction (default carried over) that only matters when it hits a switch. Doors, walls and switches block it. A blocked or wasted cast still costs 4 steps. **Cue:** the cast animation shows a travelling shape that returns, and nothing changes unless a colored switch lies on the line. **Test:** before casting, press toward the switch (a bump is enough to set facing), then cast and check whether the paired door vanished.
3. **Ray row versus body size.** **Believed:** a 4×4 shot cannot enter a 2-tall corridor, or the shot is body-sized. **Actually:** the ray uses the upper-left corner's row or column. The 4×4 failed because that row missed the corridor, which is why shrinking and aligning worked. **Cue:** the shot died at the corridor mouth. **Test:** shift the body one step so the upper-left row sits inside the corridor and fire again.
4. **Teleport destination rule (L6).** **Believed:** farthest same-size pad, parity-matched pad, the pad named by the clicked pattern, or a fixed cycle on every resize. **Actually:** same-size destinations cycle in order, and the ring marks the next one. The ring is hidden while the body size mismatches. **Cue:** after a teleport, a ring appears on a different pad of the same size. **Test:** teleport twice at the same size and note which pad the ring sits on before each cast.
5. **Resize grow and alignment.** **Believed:** growth keeps a fixed anchor (bottom-right, or the facing direction), and parity of position is invariant, so some spots are unreachable. **Actually:** the engine tries offsets up to 2 up and left, upper-left first. Failure charges the full cost and flashes the body. Alignment can change when growing, on wall-induced 2-cell moves, and on teleport. **Cue:** after a grow the body shifted, or flashed color 4 with no change. **Test:** grow once in an open spot and note the shift. Before growing in a tight spot, check that a free 4×4 exists up and left.
6. **Budget accounting.** **Believed:** blocked presses and hint clicks are free, the bar is unpredictable, taking a pickup early is fine. **Actually:** each bump costs 1 step, and a pickup refunds a fixed 10. Running out ends the level and forces a reset and full replay. **Cue:** the bar drains on moves that changed nothing. **Test:** count bar cells over five bumps, and plan the whole route (including the return leg) before casting.

## Breakthroughs
- Treating each lit pattern as its own spell, with the shape deciding the action. In L4 two frames showed two spells, and the copies that said "the pattern picks the effect" planned shrink then fire.
- Aim first, then cast: face the switch (a bump sets facing), then fire. A wasted cast only happens when the ray misses a switch.
- Size matters for destinations (L5): shrink to reach the small destination, grow before leaving through the large one.
- L6 order: shoot the maroon switch first because its door blocks the ray to the orange one. Arrive on the far side as a 4×4 and teleport while large. Read the ring for the next cycling destination.

## Lessons that carry to other games
- If one input produces different outcomes in different levels, check whether the shape of the input (not the level) selects the action.
- A "ray" action may follow a reference point of the body rather than its full outline. Check where the shot's row or column comes from before blaming size.
- When something destroys a whole class of objects, check for a pairing (same color) between the trigger and the targets.
- When a destination changes, look for an on-screen marker that shows the next target, and check whether the marker hides in some states.
- When a resource counter drains, test whether failed or null actions still charge before assuming they are free.
- A transform (grow) can shift the object and fail in tight spots. Check the space before spending the cost.
