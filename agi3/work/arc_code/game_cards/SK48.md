# SK48 — Telescoping arms matching a template sequence
Keywords: telescoping arm, extend/retract, push chain, template sequence, ordered colors, check lights, rails, obstacle, pin blocks, selectable pieces, click to switch, step budget, undo, reordering

Looks like: A small box holds a few colored 4x4 blocks. At its left or top edge sits a 6x6 base with a 2x2 colored center and an arm of square checker segments, plus a thin rail strip. Below a budget bar, a copy of the same base carries a fixed row of blocks, with small lights drawn on it. Actions are 1–4 (extend, retract or slide the arm), 6 (click) and 7 (undo).

## Rules
- Bases come in same-color pairs. Only the arm above the budget bar (row 53) is yours. The copy below it is a fixed template with check lights.
- Win: the colors lying beneath the arm's segments, read from the base outward with empty segments skipped, must equal the template's first N colors (N = template segments − 1). This is recomputed from the current state, so there is no history. Extra blocks beneath the arm are harmless, exact positions don't matter, and over-extending is fine. If the last move completes it, you win even at 0 steps.
- Pressing along the arm's facing direction extends it, and the opposite direction retracts it (minimum 1 segment). A sideways press slides the whole arm only if a rail lies on that side. Slides are all-or-nothing and carry every block under the arm.
- Extension is never blocked by blocks. They are pushed ahead, in chains. If a block can't move, the arm slides underneath it, which is the only way to pick a block up. Retracting drags blocks back, but an immovable block is left behind, which is the only way to put one down.
- A block can't be pushed or slid if the move leaves the box, enters an obstacle cell, or has a blocked chain ahead of it. It also can't move sideways if it lies under, or would enter a cell under, an arm perpendicular to the motion. Such a perpendicular arm clamps its blocks, which can then only travel along that arm's direction.
- The obstacle looks like background, but it shows as a 6x6 hole in the box. It blocks only its own cell.
- Budget: 196 steps per level. Only presses of keys 1–4 cost steps, including refused ones. Click and undo are free, and undo does not refund steps. Level change refills the bar.
- Click switches arms only on bases with a hidden selectable tag that also have a pair. In L3 and L4 the extra arms are unselectable. In L6 and L7 you have two board arms.

## Levels
- L1: one arm and 3 blocks; learn push, pick-up and the template. (cleared 13/14, median 62 min; 1 stuck copy)
- L2: 4 blocks start in exactly reversed order, so reordering is needed. (median 62 min)
- L3: a perpendicular fixed arm (a "rack") clamps blocks. (cleared 11/13, median 60 min)
- L4: two pairs. Only the horizontal loading arm moves, and it delivers blocks beneath two fixed vertical arms. (cleared 10/11, median 47 min)
- L5: first obstacle, with the target blocks sealed behind it. (hard: cleared 5/10, median 147 min; 4 stuck copies)
- L6: two board arms (click to switch) plus an obstacle. (hard: cleared 2/5, median 136 min; 2 stuck copies)
- L7: two arms, no obstacle. (0/2 cleared; 1 copy stuck after 28 min)
- L8: very few rails. Our player never reached it.

## Blind spots — where our player lost time
1. **Believed:** the blocks behind the obstacle are frozen, because the arm can never reach them and the vertical push touches only the arm's own band. The panel goal was then "impossible" (6 copies). **Actually:** blocks push blocks in chains. A move started from a line adjacent to the obstacle's line shoves the sealed block out of its row. **Cue:** the 6x6 hole, with all required blocks on its far side. **Test:** from the neighbouring line with the arm long enough, press toward the sealed line and see whether the far block moves.
2. **Believed:** click does nothing, since it was inert in L1–L5 (2 copies). **Actually:** in L6 a click on a base switches the controlled arm. The blocks the first arm can't reach belong to the second. **Cue:** a second base-like 6x6 sprite on another edge and a second template group below. **Test:** click the new base once. The selection shading flips if it works.
3. **Believed:** the win needs the template's exact layout, a row packed at the wall, or the arm fully extended (5 copies). **Actually:** only the color order under the arm matters. **Cue:** the template's check lights light individually. **Test:** put the first one or two colors under the arm anywhere and watch their lights.
4. **Believed:** the perpendicular fixed arm is a decoration or scenery (4 copies). **Actually:** it clamps blocks under it sideways, and they slide only along its axis. **Cue:** blocks stacked along a striped bar that won't slide sideways. **Test:** push one block sideways. If it flashes and stays, drag it vertically out of the bar's span first.
5. **Believed:** in L4 the L1–L3 goal still applies, or "all blocks on the fixed arms" suffices (4 copies). **Actually:** each fixed arm needs its own template colors, in order from its base. **Cue:** the bottom strip now shows two groups, each led by a box. **Test:** load one fixed arm correctly and see whether its lights come on.
6. **Believed:** the bar is cosmetic or a loose timer (2 copies). **Actually:** it's a hard 196-step budget, and running out ends the attempt. **Cue:** row 53 shrinks as key presses are spent (about 3 presses per bar pixel), including refused ones. **Test:** count how many presses one bar cell takes.

## Breakthroughs
- Copies that cleared L5 realised vertical pushes chain through a column. A block in the adjacent line shoves the sealed one out (5 copies).
- Copies that cleared L6 clicked the second base to switch arms (2 copies).
- Picking up works by blocked motion. Pin a block against a wall, then extend and the arm slides under it. Park a block out of reach to leave it behind (3 copies).
- For reordering, lift blocks into other lines so they re-enter on the other side of what stays; park the rest out of the arm's reach (3 copies).

## Lessons that carry to other games
- If an action looked inert earlier, re-test it when a second controllable-looking object appears. An action can be live only for some objects.
- When something seems permanently unreachable, check whether pushing a neighbour moves it indirectly through a chain.
- A reference picture may be only schematic. Use live per-position indicators to learn the actual predicate with the smallest configuration.
- When the reference display changes layout, assume the goal changed too, and don't reuse the old one.
- Check what a blocked action does, since partial effects (the arm moves but the block stays) may be the intended pick-up or drop.
- Find out whether a meter is a hard budget, and which actions are free.
