# SP80 — Steer streams with movable deflectors
Keywords: pour simulation, gravity direction, movable barriers, click to select, splitting streams, cup mouth, turning block, overflow loses, step budget, rotated board, second source, side-facing target

Looks like: A 16×16 or 20×20 board with a fixed nozzle at one edge, a few rectangular barriers (one highlighted as selected) and 2–4 yellow cups. The actions are move up/down/left/right, a "release" action (ACTION5), and click (ACTION6) on the board. Nothing happens until you release the fluid, and the animation then reverts unless you win.

## Rules
- There are two phases. In the arrange phase the 4 direction actions move the selected barrier one cell and ACTION6 selects the barrier under the click. ACTION5 releases the water and runs the whole simulation as a single action.
- Every action costs 1 step, including clicks on empty space, refused moves and the release itself. The budget is set per level (30/45/100/120/100/120) and the bar can't show every unit. Reaching 0 loses.
- Barriers cannot go near a source or within 1 cell of a cup. They may overlap each other.
- Water advances through empty cells and follows existing water. When it hits a barrier it goes around both ends and keeps its original direction. It fills a cup only if it enters exactly at the cup's mouth; hitting any other part of a cup treats the cup as a barrier. Cups can face sideways and then need horizontal flow from the correct side.
- A 2×2 block missing one corner (a turning block, drawn purple) turns water 90° when the water enters at the missing corner.
- A barrier can carry a coloured hole (a separate colour inside the barrier). That hole is an outlet that makes a second source just below it. It moves with the barrier and does not pass incoming water.
- Win means every cup is full and no drop touched the floor or an inner lethal wall (L5/L6 have lethal side walls). Cups that are full but with any overflow do not win.
- Rotated levels (L2/3/5) flip gravity so the water rises. Controls and clicks still follow the screen.
- After 4 failed releases the 5th loses. Barrier positions survive a failure, but steps are not refunded. RESET restores everything.

## Levels
- L1: one barrier, one source, 2 floor cups. Centre the barrier so the two streams past its ends land in the two mouths.
- L2 (hard: cleared 12/14, median 53 min, 2 stuck): board rotated 180° so water rises and cups hang from the top; 3 barriers, and the others must be selected by clicking.
- L3 (cleared 12/12, median 38 min): 3 sources and 4 barriers. Every stream end must be a mouth, so barriers are pushed against the side walls to get a single child.
- L4 (hard: cleared 9/12, median 85 min, 2 stuck): 20×20 board, 5 barriers, one with an outlet hole that is a movable second source. 4 floor cups.
- L5 (hard: cleared 5/9, median 193 min, 4 stuck): rotated board with a turning block and a side-facing cup on the left wall that needs horizontal flow. The left wall is lethal.
- L6 (hard: cleared 3/5, median 82 min, 2 stuck): two turning blocks, a vertical bar, a perforated barrier, and 3 side-facing cups. Both outer columns are lethal.

## Blind spots — where our player lost time
1. **The odd-coloured piece is a turning block, not a wall (L5, L6).**
   **Believed:** the purple piece is just another obstacle, or a "special object" never tested. Stuck copies concluded the side cup was unfillable or that two long shelves were needed. One simulator even omitted that colour.
   **Actually:** it turns water 90° at its missing corner, so water can be sent sideways into a side-facing cup.
   **Cue:** one piece differs in colour and shape (non-rectangular) from the others. A cup's mouth faces sideways and nothing rectangular can reach it.
   **Test:** park the odd piece under a stream and release once. Check where the water goes along the row.
2. **Clicks written off as inert (L2).**
   **Believed:** ACTION6 does nothing, because clicking the only (already selected) barrier in L1 did nothing. Both stuck L2 copies then built invented mechanics, such as non-movable red blocks that swap on release.
   **Actually:** clicking an unselected barrier selects it. L2 needs several barriers moved.
   **Cue:** several barriers with different colours and only one highlighted.
   **Test:** click a different-coloured barrier, then press one direction. Two actions.
3. **Compound objects mis-split (L4).**
   **Believed:** the hole or dark cell was a fixed obstacle or hidden cannon, and the pieces on either side of it were independent bars. Both stuck copies planned with the wrong object list.
   **Actually:** the pieces and the hole form one barrier, and the hole is a movable outlet.
   **Cue:** a differently coloured cell inside a barrier, and a stream that appears just below the hole once water is released.
   **Test:** click one part, move once, and see what travels with it.
4. **"All cups full" taken as the win.**
   **Believed:** maroon cups mean success, so the player kept adjusting positions.
   **Actually:** any overflow cancels the win, even when every cup filled.
   **Cue:** a failed release with all cups full also flashes the border green, and everything reverts.
   **Test:** after a full-cup failure, check for any water reaching a floor or wall cell.
5. **Misreading the hidden budget.**
   **Believed:** a fixed action cap of about 40–70, or a bar that shows the cap.
   **Actually:** each level has its own step budget in which every action costs a step, plus a limit of 4 failed releases per attempt.
   **Cue:** GAME_OVER arrives while the bar is not empty.
   **Test:** count actions since the last RESET, and plan the layout offline before spending steps.

## Breakthroughs
- Selecting barriers by clicking each one was the unlock for L2 and later. Each barrier can then be placed in turn.
- Treat the stream as a tree. Each barrier gives children at its two ends, and the leaves must be mouths. Pushing a barrier flush to a wall removes one child (L3).
- The perforated barrier and the dark cells form one piece with a movable outlet. Moving it places a source.
- Parking the turning piece under a rising stream sends it along a row into the side cup (L5). In L6 the same idea was used with several such pieces.

## Lessons that carry to other games
- When one object looks different from the others, test what it does before assuming it behaves like the rest.
- An action that did nothing in one situation may only have been inert there. Retest it where there are several objects.
- When one object appears to be a mix of parts, check by moving one part whether all the parts move together, and whether one part is a source.
- A success flag may require a negative condition (nothing leaked) in addition to the positive one (targets filled). Check both after a failed run that looked complete.
- A model that matches every recorded run can still be wrong about something no run ever exercised. List the object types it ignores.
- Count resource use per attempt from the start, as hidden counters may not match the visible bar.
