# SU15 — Click-vacuum merging with biting chasers
Keywords: click vacuum, radius pull, merge equal tiers, 2048 ladder, mass conservation, chasing enemies, bite downgrades, enemy merging, target rings, HUD goal icons, step budget, mixed-tier penalty, undo, lure and steer, decoy bait

Looks like: A black field, 64×64, with a HUD band on top and a step bar on the bottom row. It holds coloured squares of several sizes and one or more circular target rings. The only real actions are click-at-a-point and Undo, and no directional inputs exist. The first level has one square and a dotted hint path. Later boards add 8 identical small squares, then pink pixel creatures that walk toward the squares.

## Rules
- A click pulls every square within about 8 cells of the click (measured to the nearest edge) onto the click point. One square just moves, by at most a few cells per axis per tick. Two or more same-tier squares merge into one square a tier up, **landing on the click**. Merging three at once wastes mass.
- Squares of different tiers in one click's radius cause an error: nothing is kept, and a growing penalty is taken from the step budget (2, 4, 6…). Undo restores positions but refunds no steps. A click on the HUD or the bottom row is a free no-op.
- Win: counting object centres inside the ring boxes, the totals must match the HUD target icons exactly. The check runs every animation frame, so a merge click placed inside a ring wins at once. The goals can include an enemy itself, or a mix of sizes across several rings.
- After each click, enemies take up to 4 ticks toward the nearest square. On touching one they bite it: it drops a tier and is thrown about 10 cells, and a bitten smallest square is destroyed. Enemies inside the click radius are pulled instead and do not bite that turn. The smallest enemy type flies past the click in that direction, so clicks near it steer it.
- Overlapping same-type enemies upgrade (types 1→2→3). Mixed enemy types overlapping cause the same error as mixed tiers.
- Mass is 2^tier. Merging pairs conserves it; bites and triple merges lose it. When the board's mass falls below what the targets need, the rings and HUD icons turn grey. The game does not end, but the level cannot be won. Bites are also the only way to shrink a big square, so from level 6 enemies are tools as well as hazards.
- Step budget is 32 or 48 per level. A losing board can be reset or undone.

## Levels
- L1: one square, a dotted hint path and a ring (tutorial).
- L2: 8 small squares, a merge ladder, one big target (hard: cleared 14/14, median 47 min).
- L3: two rings, two required sizes; mixed sizes coexist (cleared 14/14, median 38 min).
- L4: first chasing enemy, with zero mass slack (hard: cleared 11/14, median 85 min, 2 stuck copies).
- L5: two enemies and spare small squares as bait (hard: cleared 8/11, median 62 min, 1 stuck copy).
- L6: one huge square plus an enemy. Bites shrink the square to the target size, and the enemy must also end up in a ring (cleared 8/8, median 26 min).
- L7: two big targets, built from merges and bites (cleared 7/8, median 17 min, 1 stuck copy).
- L8: the goal needs an upgraded enemy, so two enemies must be merged and parked in a ring (hard: cleared 7/7, median 71 min).
- L9: the goal needs a top-tier enemy plus two squares, with 4 enemies and room for only one scripted bite (hard: cleared 3/7, median 42 min, 3 stuck copies).

## Blind spots — where our player lost time
1. **Believed:** Squares can be assembled near a chaser if the player is quick. Squares were left parked or walked into an enemy's path.
   **Actually:** A bite drops a tier and throws the square away. The mass is gone, the rings go grey, and a reset is needed. This caused the stuck L4, L5 and L9 copies.
   **Cue:** The rings and HUD icons turn grey, or a square vanishes and leaves a lone small one.
   **Test:** Before each click, measure the enemy's distance to every square it could reach in 4 ticks. Build on the far side, or undo and check the rings recolour.
2. **Believed:** Clicking near an enemy "lunges" or "dashes" it at some fitted rule, with formulas of 10, 11 or 12 cells. Or the click is treated as harmless.
   **Actually:** The enemy is simply inside the vacuum radius. It is pulled, or in the smallest type flung past the click, and it cannot bite that turn. A merge click near it can fling it onto the new square.
   **Cue:** The enemy jumps on a click that touched no square.
   **Test:** Click empty ground about 6 cells from the enemy and watch where it lands.
3. **Believed:** "Rings grey" means a piece was touched. The player also wondered whether a grey board can still be won.
   **Actually:** Grey means total mass is below the target, whatever the cause. Eating spare small squares is harmless while enough mass remains.
   **Cue:** The colour change right after a bite.
   **Test:** Sum 2^tier over the squares and compare with the target sum. Undo once and see whether the colours return.
4. **Believed:** The goal is "put the shape on a ring". Variants tried: fully inside, anchor on the centre, any overlap, "everything merged", or "the enemy must eat the shape".
   **Actually:** The HUD icons list every required item, enemies included. Each item's centre must be inside a ring box, with exact counts. A one-cell overlap wins nothing.
   **Cue:** Nothing happens when a shape that looks right is parked on a ring, or the HUD shows an enemy icon next to the squares.
   **Test:** Compare the HUD icons item by item with what sits in the rings. Click the ring centre to land a shape there.
5. **Believed:** The click moves only the nearest object, along rays, with a reach of 6, or the ring absorbs squares. Several of these were fitted from thin data.
   **Actually:** A click pulls everything within about 8 cells of the border onto the click point, and equal tiers merge.
   **Cue:** Two squares far apart merge when one click is within reach of both.
   **Test:** Put one click between two same-size squares about 12 cells apart.
6. **Believed:** A click that looks fine will just move a piece, so mixed sizes, or an unfinished pair near the click, were not checked. The player also guessed that an "unused pair blocks movement".
   **Actually:** Any other tier in the radius is the error: a flashing animation, an escalating step penalty, and a rollback.
   **Cue:** A long flash after a click, and the bar dropping by more than usual.
   **Test:** List what sits within 8 cells of the click before pressing it.
7. **Believed (L8–L9):** Enemies are only hazards, so the merge-up chain was found late. In L9 the player merged enemies too early, parked squares first, or moved the big square onto enemies.
   **Actually:** Overlapping same-type enemies upgrade, and the goal needs a specific type. Finished squares must be safe from every enemy until the very end.
   **Cue:** The HUD shows an enemy of a different colour from the ones on the board.
   **Test:** Pull two same-colour enemies together and see whether the colour changes.

## Breakthroughs
- Steering: a harmless click about 6–10 cells from an enemy, aimed away from the work area, bought several actions of safety. The winning L4–L5 plays used it repeatedly. Spare small squares were also used as bait to keep chasers busy.
- Using the enemy as a tool: in L6 the bites turned the huge square into the target size, and the enemy was then steered into the second ring. A final merge click placed inside a ring (L5) cleared on that frame.
- Reading the HUD icons showed hidden requirements. Together with enemy merging (L8–L9), this let the stuck copies' later attempts finish the enemy goals.

## Lessons that carry to other games
- When a move or click can affect several objects at once, check which of them it reaches before using it. A multi-object action can have side effects on things you did not mean to move.
- A goal icon in the HUD may include things you have not treated as pieces (the hazard itself). Match every icon to something on the board.
- A colour change of the whole board often means the level has become unwinnable. Work out what resource is low before testing exotic theories.
- Before parking a finished piece, ask which moving agent can still reach it, and do the risky agent-related steps last.
- If a rule is first fitted from thin data, test it with one cheap probe before building a plan on it.
- Hazards that react to your actions can often be steered with a harmless action rather than avoided.
