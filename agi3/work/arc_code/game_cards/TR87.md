# TR87 — Rewriting symbols with a rule table
Keywords: string rewriting, rule table, lookup dictionary, cyclic selector, rotation-invariant symbols, cursor over editable cells, multi-step translation, segmentation, composed rules, editable rules, block-wise cycling, action budget
Looks like: A 64×64 board with an upper region of several "symbol string — symbol string" rules. Each symbol is a 7×7 coloured card holding a 5×5 black pattern. The lower region holds two rows of such cards, a fixed one above an editable one. A bracket cursor marks one card or group, and only ACTION1–4 exist: 3/4 move the cursor, 1/2 cycle the marked cards through a 7-step loop. There is no click and no submit, and the bottom row is a shrinking progress bar.

## Rules
- Rules are written as left string = right string. The lower region shows a source string (never editable) above a target string. The level clears when rewriting the source with the rules gives the target. Greedy scan: at each position the first rule in table order whose left side matches is used. There is no backtracking.
- The match is checked only after an edit (ACTION1/2), not after cursor moves. Success needs the whole source consumed, and extra target cells beyond the output are ignored.
- Card border colour fixes the alphabet (3 alphabets, 7 patterns each). ACTION1/2 step the marked card through the 7 patterns of its own alphabet, and the colour never changes.
- Every pattern is drawn at a random rotation. Rotation is cosmetic: identity is the pattern itself, so compare symbols up to rotation.
- Hidden per-level flags:
  - Composed rules (L4): a rule's right side is rewritten again by a second rule.
  - Editable rules (L5, L6): the cursor leaves the target and walks over the rule sides. It marks a whole side, and ACTION1/2 rotates all symbols on that side together, so their relative offsets never change.
  - Per-symbol expansion (L6): each symbol of a right side is expanded through a second rule.
- Budget: 128 actions per level (256 on L6), reset per level. The bottom bar shows the remaining fraction.

## Levels
- L1: one symbol → one symbol, 5-symbol source, 6 rules (one unused). Edit the target row. (hard: cleared 12/12, median 30 min)
- L2: right sides have 1–3 symbols, so the target length is the sum of the parts. (hard: cleared 11/12, median 18 min, 1 stuck copy at 80 min)
- L3: left sides also have 1–3 symbols, so the source must be segmented. The parse is unique, and one rule is a decoy. (median 8 min)
- L4: two-stage chain, A→B then B→C, all single symbols. (median 4 min)
- L5: the cursor now walks the rule sides, and the lower rows are fixed. Some sides hold 2 symbols that rotate together. (cleared 11/11, median 17 min)
- L6: three A→(2 symbols) rules plus three B→C rules, and all 12 sides are editable. (hard: cleared 11/11, median 24 min, one copy took 363 min)

## Blind spots — where our player lost time
1. **Believed:** the answer symbol is the partner glyph in a specific orientation. Variants: apply the same rotation as the source, or one transform per rule. The stuck copy also dealt answers out round-robin. **Actually:** only the pattern identity counts and rotation is noise. Answers are the concatenation of right sides in source order (L2, 6 copies).
   **Cue:** the wanted rotated glyph is not in that slot's 7-step loop, and the sum of right-side lengths already equals the number of editable cells.
   **Test:** build the concatenation, set one slot to any rotation of the right pattern, and check that the slot's loop contains it.
2. **Believed:** the left→right mapping is a computable transform of the picture (flips, rotations, XOR, cellular automata) (L1, 6 copies). **Actually:** it is an arbitrary lookup table.
   **Cue:** the sample pairs share no visible structure, and the source cards are rotated copies of the table's left cards.
   **Test:** after one dihedral/complement check, stop searching and just look the symbol up.
3. **Believed:** on the 3-symbol-source level, rules serve the source symbols in reading order of the table rows, and the two symbols on a right side can be set freely. **Actually:** rules are found by matching the symbol, and a 2-symbol side rotates as a block with a fixed offset. Only the zero-offset side can hold two equal symbols, which forces which row serves which input (L6, 6 copies; the 363-min copy tried 480+ wrong vectors).
   **Cue:** the target has a repeated pair (a palindrome) and only one side can show equal symbols.
   **Test:** press ACTION1 on each 2-symbol side 2–3 times and note whether the two symbols ever coincide.
4. **Believed:** the lower rows are the editable answer. **Actually:** on the rule-editing levels the bracket sits above the boundary, the lower rows are fixed, and the rule sides are what must be edited (L5, 4 copies). One copy then picked cards by 7×7 box instead of by whole side, and another had a wrong reading hypothesis.
   **Cue:** the bracket's first position is in the upper region.
   **Test:** press ACTION1 once and see which region changed, then ACTION4 about 8 times to see the cursor loop.
5. **Believed:** a rule or direction was contradicted (an "unreachable" class, a slot cycling backwards, a missing glyph). **Actually:** the player's own parser or driver was wrong: a rotation check that tested only 4 of 8 transforms, glyphs stored as lists, a stale board, a counter row parsed as a strip, a modulo error (5 copies).
   **Cue:** a contradiction that no other level shows.
   **Test:** replay the solver on a recorded state with a known outcome before dropping the model.

## Breakthroughs
- Copies that cleared L1–L4 quickly stopped caring about orientation and walked each slot's loop to the right pattern. Length sums (for example 1+3+2+1 = 7) confirmed the concatenation model.
- On the rule-editing level, 2-symbol sides lining up with repeated symbols in the fixed rows fixed the segmentation, and the rows could be read in table order.
- On the last level, the locked offset of 2-symbol sides forced which row serves which input. The player then realised any consistent second-stage table clears, since the check is an invariant, not a stored answer.

## Lessons that carry to other games
- If shapes appear at random rotations, test whether identity is rotation-invariant before hunting for a transform (blind spot 1).
- A displayed table of samples is probably an arbitrary dictionary. Give a generating function a couple of probes, then use it as a lookup (blind spot 2).
- When one control moves a group of items together, their differences are invariant. Measure them: they can force which piece goes where (blind spot 3, breakthrough 3).
- Find which region the cursor edits before deciding what the goal is (blind spot 4).
- Prefer matching by content to matching by position, and use total-length sums as a cheap check on a model (blind spots 1 and 3).
- When a model hits a contradiction, first replay your own reader on a recorded state (blind spot 5).
