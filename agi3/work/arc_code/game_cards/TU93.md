# TU93 — Grid maze, timed enemy types
Keywords: grid maze, node-by-node moves, action budget, stationary trap, patrol timing, delayed follower, bump to destroy, parity, occlusion, lure around a loop, forced kill order, no-op wall bump
Looks like: A small maze with an exit marker, a player block with a one-pixel facing "eye", and a magenta budget bar along the bottom row. Only the four direction actions exist, and each moves the player one node (two cells). Level 1 has no enemies. Later levels add 3x3 coloured blocks that each have an eye pixel.

## Rules
- The maze alternates node cells and passage cells. One move is one node (6 px), and only a color-2 passage lets you move. A wall bump costs 1 budget unit and changes nothing: enemies do not tick and the clock does not advance. There is no real wait.
- The budget is level-specific (50/50/35/20/50/60/30/50/50) and the bar drains accordingly. Success is checked before loss, and the budget resets on each level.
- Collisions are asymmetric. If the player enters an enemy's node, that enemy is destroyed. If an enemy enters the player's node, the player dies. Levels 2-4 and 6-9 cannot be solved without destroying at least one enemy.
- Stationary trap (color 8): after each successful move it fires only if the player ended exactly one node in front of it, in the direction its eye points. It ignores walls, and it is safe from any other side or distance. Its faced node can even be the exit.
- Patrol (color 12): after every successful move it steps one node, even through walls, and reverses when the passage ahead is closed. Its path depends only on the move count. Patrols pass through each other and can sit on top of other sprites.
- Follower (color 13): it sleeps until the player stops exactly two nodes in front of it, in its facing line. After that, on later turns it sits on the node the player occupied two moves earlier. It cannot catch a player who keeps moving forward, and it kills only if you reverse into its path.
- Sprites are drawn in list order, so a patrol can hide the exit, a trap or the follower in the final frame. Intermediate frames of the same action can reveal them.

## Levels
- L1: plain maze, no enemies, 50 actions.
- L2: first trap in the only corridor; go around and step onto it from a side it does not face (median 13 min).
- L3: three traps whose faced nodes block the route; kill them in dependency order.
- L4: trap plus patrol, budget only 20; phase the patrol with real back-and-forth moves and loop around it (median 16 min).
- L5: four patrols that sweep the corridors around the exit; pure timing, with parity forcing many extra moves (median 18 min).
- L6: six traps plus two patrols; the direct row is parity-blocked, so you take a long detour and chain kills (median 15 min).
- L7: first follower, plus a trap; outrun it and kill the trap from below (median 7 min).
- L8: the follower sits in the only corridor. Lure it round a cycle, then sprint (hard: cleared 11/12, median 42 min).
- L9: all three types, a trap aimed at the exit, and a second trap guarding the first. Forced kill order, timing against patrols, and a follower shadowing you throughout (hard: cleared 9/11, median 49 min).

## Blind spots — where our player lost time
1. **Mimic rules mis-modelled.** Seven copies' level documents record the follower's rule being corrected later.
   **Believed:** the colour-13 block was a static sentry, or it chased the player's *new* position; wake conditions were guessed as column, Manhattan distance, aligned, or graph distance.
   **Actually:** it wakes when the player stops two nodes ahead in its facing line, then follows P(t−2). A solver built on "chases the new position" declared L9 unwinnable.
   **Cue:** on every awake turn the follower sits exactly on the player's earlier cell, and its eye shows its next move.
   **Test:** after waking it, make 3-4 non-reversing moves and compare its cell with your own trail.
2. **Hidden sprites read as dead or absent.** Seven copies.
   **Believed:** a missing exit, trap or follower was gone, or a patrol was lethal where it overlapped something.
   **Actually:** a later-drawn patrol covers the earlier sprite, and two patrols stacked on one node look like one.
   **Cue:** the colour-12 pixel count drops by one block, or the exit's colour disappears for a turn.
   **Test:** keep a model carried from the level's first board and compare sets of positions. Check the intermediate frames of the same action.
3. **Waiting by bumping a wall.** Four copies.
   **Believed:** a blocked move is a free wait for patrol phase.
   **Actually:** it burns budget and freezes everything, so phase shifts need real back-and-forth moves. Parity of path length also fixes arrival time.
   **Cue:** a bump returns a single frame with only the bar changed.
   **Test:** bump once and watch whether any enemy moved.
4. **Budget unknown per level.** Three copies.
   **Believed:** the bar is about 1 per action.
   **Actually:** L4 allows 20 actions, and dithering plus probes ran it dry with no enemy near.
   **Cue:** the bar drains about 3 cells per action instead of 1.
   **Test:** read the drain over the first two moves and divide 64 by it before planning any phase-setting.
5. **Trap trigger guessed wrong.** Five copies.
   **Believed:** "same row and adjacent", "row only", or "vertical is safe". One copy swapped left and right when reading the eye.
   **Actually:** only the one node in front, along the eye direction, is lethal. Walking down a corridor toward the trap is death.
   **Cue:** the eye pixel on the block's edge.
   **Test:** read the eye, then approach from a side node and step onto the trap.
6. **Stride miscounted on L1.** Three copies.
   **Believed:** one cell per move, so a 34-move path was computed.
   **Actually:** each move covers two cells, and half the planned moves were refused.
   **Cue:** the first move shifts the block 6 px.
   **Test:** make one move and measure the shift.
7. **An enemy aimed at the exit.** One copy.
   **Believed:** reaching the exit wins at once.
   **Actually:** a live trap's lethal node (the node directly in front of it) can lie on the only approach to the exit, so the guard must be removed from the side before that approach. Standing on the exit itself is safe, because success is checked before loss.
   **Cue:** the trap's eye points toward the exit.
   **Test:** read the eye direction before the final approach.

## Breakthroughs
- Kill by entering the enemy's node from a side it does not face, and kill in dependency order. A node in front of an enemy is safe once that enemy is dead, which unlocked L3, L6 and L9.
- The follower cannot be shaken by running or waiting. Lure it round a cycle so it ends up two moves behind, then run the corridor it was guarding.
- Treat patrols as a deterministic schedule over successful moves. Search over (position, move count mod period) and add parity arguments. This showed when a direct route was impossible, such as L6's blocked row.

## Lessons that carry to other games
- Measure the step unit and the per-level budget with one or two probes before planning a long route.
- Check whether an enemy's threat is directional, and read its facing mark before assuming it is symmetric.
- A "wait" may not exist. Check whether a refused action advances the world or only costs budget.
- When a model makes a level look unsolvable, suspect the model first. Re-fit it to the recorded frames, including lag and tick-order assumptions.
- Check whether a missing object is covered by a later-drawn one, and keep a model carried from the first board.
- If a hunter keeps a fixed distance, check whether a loop lets you put it behind you.
