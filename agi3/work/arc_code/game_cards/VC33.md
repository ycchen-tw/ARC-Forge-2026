# VC33 — Retractable pistons, swap bridges, passengers
Keywords: click-only, linked pairs, conservation, push increment, lane, bridge, passenger transfer, exact alignment, hub routing, level budget, strict equality, reversible counters, rotated levels
Looks like: A 2D board of parallel strips split by thin walls, with small solid squares of one colour beside the walls and a small coloured marker sprite sitting at one strip's moving edge. Only `ACTION6` (click at x,y) exists, and a row-0 bar counts down the action budget. Later levels add thin pale bars drawn in wall gaps, perpendicular barriers, several coloured passengers, and the push direction rotates between levels.

## Rules
- The solid squares are the only real controls. Each sits beside a wall, and one click shifts that wall's two neighbouring strips by one fixed step, one growing and one shrinking. The step equals the square's size (2 or 3). The total length of a linked chain is conserved. Pressing a pair's button when the donor is empty or the receiver is full is refused but still costs an action.
- A passenger rides the moving edge of its strip, so its position is that strip's length. A passenger can leave its strip only through a swap bridge (pale bar in a wall gap).
- A bridge works only when both neighbouring strip edges sit exactly at the bridge's leading edge. The highlight colour can be stale, so trust geometry. A click then exchanges the passengers of the two strips (a swap if both are occupied). A click on an unaligned bridge is wasted.
- A barrier splits a strip into two independent pistons. An occupied piston stops short of the barrier to leave room for the passenger.
- Win: every passenger's coloured stripe lines up exactly (strict equality along the push axis) with the same-coloured wall marker, and the passenger's strip borders that marker's wall. All passengers must be in place simultaneously.
- Every click costs one action, including empty ones. The budget resets on level entry. The last click can still win, and running out ends the game, so only a reset recovers.

## Levels
- L1: two strips, one passenger, two buttons; slide to the marker.
- L2: three strips in a chain, push direction reversed; the middle strip can run dry, so you must pump through it.
- L3: five strips, three coloured passengers, no bridges, rotated; caps come from wall heights.
- L4 (hard: cleared 12/12, median 78 min): bridges appear, the push step is 3, and the buttons form two disconnected groups. The passenger must be carried across to the group that can reach the marker.
- L5 (hard: cleared 11/12, median 40 min): two passengers, three bridges, long budget; the passengers must fully trade ends and pass each other via a swap.
- L6: barrier and mixed geometry; the passenger must end in the strip that can reach the marker.
- L7 (cleared 10/11, median 22 min): upward push, two barriers, three passengers, five strips. The middle strip is a hub, and the bridge edge convention is flipped again.

## Blind spots — where our player lost time
1. **Believed:** the pale bars in the walls were valves that pass fluid, so raise the marker's lane by pouring into it (L4, 7 copies). **Actually:** nothing flows through a bar. A bar only swaps or carries the passenger when both edges are flush with it. Each linked group has a fixed total, so only one lane can ever reach the marker height. **Cue:** the buttons form two groups with no button on the middle wall; the group totals are 18 and 33 against a target height needing exactly 33. **Test:** before pumping, add up each group's total and ask which lane can hold the target height. Then test one flush bar click.
2. **Believed:** aligning each passenger with its marker anywhere on the board wins (L5, L3; 10 copies). **Actually:** the passenger's strip must border the marker's wall, and all passengers must be right at once. **Cue:** a screenshot with every stripe lined up and no level change. **Test:** after the first alignment, ask which strip borders that wall. Check whether that strip's range can reach the needed length; if not, the passenger must move strips.
3. **Believed:** a bridge opens when two neighbouring strips are equal or merely flush inside its span (L4–L6; 9 copies). **Actually:** both edges must sit exactly at the bridge edge nearest the fluid or anchor. **Cue:** the pale bar stays pale at equal lengths. **Test:** put both strips flush at each end of the bar's span and watch the colour change.
4. **Believed:** probing freely is cheap (L4, 6 copies). **Actually:** the budget there is about 50 actions, and several copies ran out and had to reset, repeating the level. **Cue:** the row-0 bar visibly shrinking during probing. **Test:** spend probes on one button pair and one bridge click, then write the full model before continuing.
5. **Believed:** a chain's end can be pushed straight to the target (L2; 10 copies). **Actually:** a refused press when the middle strip is empty blocks it; you must alternate presses on the neighbouring pairs so the middle strip is refilled. **Cue:** the passenger stops short and presses do nothing. **Test:** press the other pair once and retry.
6. **Believed:** the bridge-edge rule from the previous level carries to the next (L6/L7; 2 copies). **Actually:** the flush edge depends on the push direction, so it flips. **Cue:** the pale bar does not light up where the old rule predicts. **Test:** try a two-press probe that lines both edges up at the bar's other end.

## Breakthroughs
- L4: clearing copies compared group totals first. The marker height equals one group's total, so the passenger had to be walked through bridges into that group and then that group's strip filled to the top.
- L5: copies noticed that range limits make only one specific strip reach each marker, so the passengers must fully exchange ends. One click on a bridge with passengers on both sides swaps them.
- L7: only one assignment is feasible, with a hub strip holding the passenger whose marker is on the hub's wall. Copies fixed that assignment first, then planned bridge order.
- Several copies wrote a small simulator of the state (edge positions, caps, passenger strips) and searched it for the shortest route.

## Lessons that carry to other games
- If every target is lined up and nothing happens, check what else the win rule requires: adjacency, ordering, or all items at once.
- A visual cue that turns on at "equal" may actually mean an exact position; test the other end of the span before trusting equality.
- Sum up what each connected group can ever hold before spending actions pushing into a target.
- A rule found on one level may flip with orientation; re-derive it with a two-click probe.
- Probe sparingly when the action counter is visible, and reset only after modelling.
