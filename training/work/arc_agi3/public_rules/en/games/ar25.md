**Core rule**: The 21×21 board contains one or two **mirrors** (a vertical line and a horizontal line) and one or two **shape pieces**. Mirrors reflect pieces to the other side: for a vertical mirror in column m, `x' = 2m - x`; for a horizontal mirror in row m, `y' = 2m - y`. **Targets** are scattered across the board. Clear the level by **covering every target with a physical piece or a reflection**.

**Action mapping**
- `ACTION6(x,y)`: select the object to control (**costs no moves**). `ACTION5`: cycle the selection (**costs 1 move**). **Mirrors themselves are selectable objects**; the cycle lists mirrors before pieces, excluding fixed mirrors.
- `ACTION1-4`: move the selected object (up/down/left/right). Vertical mirrors move only left/right, horizontal mirrors only up/down; pieces can move in all directions.
- `ACTION7`: **Undo** (a position stack; only actions that actually move something enter the stack, and selection changes are not restored). Undo costs no moves and **does not refund moves already spent**; an empty stack gives a free no-op.

The puzzle is therefore to adjust both mirror and object positions until the reflected patterns cover all targets exactly where needed.

**Source details** (local/random comparisons below are historical records from the reconstructed engine; the current official engine separately verified all-level completion and resume in this review, without rerunning every local probe)

- **Reflections do not recurse into an infinite kaleidoscope**. The code does use BFS to reflect reflections, with a depth limit of 12, but **reflection is an involution** (reflecting twice in the same mirror returns to the original position), and there is at most one mirror of each orientation. The complete orbit is thus the Klein four-group: a piece produces at most **3 additional reflections** (left/right, up/down, diagonal), saturating at depth 2. The 12 is merely a generous bound for the general formulation.
  (Measured support: expressing coverage as “the 4-point orbit of each physical-piece cell ∩ board” matched the game's own coverage map `naxbskjmlg()` **cell for cell** across 8 levels × 45 random states.)
- Reflections are always drawn in **color 4**, regardless of the piece's color (physical pieces are color 5 in all eight levels). Off-board reflections are not drawn, but **can still be reflected back onto the board by the other mirror**.
- **Only the coordinate of the mirror line matters**, regardless of the mirror sprite's drawn length (41 cells, always spanning the entire board). Mirror-line coordinates are constrained to 0..20.
- **No collisions**: pieces do not block one another or mirrors and can overlap or pass through them (the two collision-check functions in the source are never called; they are dead code).
- **Only `ACTION1-4` actions that actually move something, plus `ACTION5`, consume moves**; `ACTION6` (click selection) and `ACTION7` (Undo) do not. Three other no-op cases also **cost no moves**: hitting the board boundary, pressing up/down on a vertical mirror, and pressing left/right on a horizontal mirror. Using clicks for all selection is therefore free.
- **Victory is checked only in the `ACTION1-4` branch after passing the boundary check**. Undo, clicks, and cycling selection do not check for victory; a direction action hitting the boundary also returns early and cannot finalize a win. Pressing the wrong axis on a mirror causes no movement or charge, but **still checks for victory**. The check precedes the move charge: a winning action advances directly to the next level without charging; otherwise, any charged action that reduces the balance to 0 loses.
- A mechanism in which some pieces **rotate 90° as their distance from a mirror changes** exists in the source (enabled by sprite tags `0040bwgtiqvhtu` / `0044qlxgcpzowy`), but **none of the eight levels uses a piece with either tag**, so it never occurs in actual play. Tags for responding to only one mirror orientation are likewise unused.
- **Click priority**: a piece beats a mirror in the same cell; repeated clicks at the intersection of two mirrors **toggle** between them, but the first click may preserve the existing selection rather than switching to the other mirror.
- Clicks select only **physical pieces or movable mirror lines**, never reflections; clicking empty space preserves the selection. Each piece's identity comes from its own sprite; contact, 8-neighbor adjacency, or overlap does not merge pieces into one object.
- Level 2 has a **tutorial animation**: when fewer than 50 moves remain and the player has not yet changed the selection, the physical piece flashes in color 0 (white; 8 render ticks); no separate arrow appears. The animation adds no move cost, and the final frame after it is clean.

**Reading the screen (essential for pixel-only agents; this is the easiest aspect to misread)**

Each cell is 3×3 pixels, and the board's upper-left corner is (0,0), so cell (gx,gy) = `frame[3gy:3gy+3, 3gx:3gx+3]`. **Each cell carries two layers of information**:

- **Cell body** (a corner pixel, e.g. `frame[3gy, 3gx]`) = physical board contents: 9 background, 5 physical piece, 4 reflection, 10 mirror line, 11 **uncovered** target.
- **Center pixel** (`frame[3gy+1, 3gx+1]`) = UI overlay, changing only that **one** central pixel: 11 “this cell is a target” (highest priority), 10 “this is a **fixed** mirror,” 0 “this cell belongs to the selected object,” 9 “unselected object marker.” This is not an exhaustive classification of center pixels: a reflection without another overlay can still have color 4 at its center.

Thus “covered targets disappear, and the level clears when no targets remain visible” is **wrong**: covering a target replaces only its outer 8 pixels with a reflection/piece in color 4 or 5; **its center pixel always remains 11**. Read it as “center pixel 11 = target cell; cell body still 11 = not yet covered.”

The selection mark 0 also needs care: a selected **mirror** marks **other objects'** piece cells crossed by its line with 0 as well (0 takes priority over unselected 9), while target 11 overrides 0. In level 8, moving the mirror to board column 6 or 18 puts 12 targets on its column (the initial column 3 has none), so a threshold based on “how many 0s are in the column” fails outright. A reliable criterion is: **a cell whose body is not 5 but carries a 0 mark can only come from a selected mirror** (when a piece is selected, its 0 marks must lie on its own physical cells, whose bodies must be 5).

**Measured result**: `pixel/solvers/ar25.py` used the model above (orbit-expanded coverage sets + bitmask correlation search for minimum movement) to complete levels 1–8 in 258 actions: 15/11/40/22/29/54/38/49 by level, all well below the respective budgets, and passed the harness resume test.

**Reset and observability**: Changing levels resets positions, budget, selection, Undo stack, and tutorial flag. The default selection is the first movable mirror, or the first piece if none exists. Official `RESET` restarts the current level if an action has already been sent in it; at action count 0 (e.g. just after a level change), or after completing the game, it returns to level 1 (`ONLY_RESET_LEVELS=true` restricts resets to the current level before game completion). Targets, physical pieces, mirror lines, and selection can be read from the screen; the Undo history has no complete visual representation, and the energy bar does not map one pixel to one move in every level.
