**Core rule**: Control a character (color 9, with a small color-11 side mark indicating its facing) through a vertical cavern to touch the **color-7 cross-shaped gem**. Walking into it or ending a fall on it immediately clears the level. The character can walk only left/right and cannot jump; all height changes come from **gravity-driven falling**. A fixed-direction pull always acts on the character: stepping into an unsupported cell makes it slide in that direction until blocked.

**Gravity points toward the top of the screen by default** (decreasing internal y). In other words, the character clings to the **underside** of platforms and falls upward, with traversable space below it. Clicking a color-8 block reverses gravity downward. To identify the floor, check where the camera places the character: at screen y≈36 under default gravity, or y≈26 when reversed.

**Action mapping**
- `ACTION3` / `ACTION4`: walk one cell left/right. A blocked destination triggers only a bump animation with no movement, but **still costs one move**. If the destination is unsupported, the character immediately falls all the way in the gravity direction after stepping into it.
- `ACTION6(x,y)`: click. Coordinates are **screen coordinates**, so only cells currently visible to the camera can be clicked; other parts of the map require moving the camera first. A click is effective only when “that cell contains exactly one special block.” Clicking rock, empty space, spikes, the gem, or the character's cell does nothing (but still costs one move).
  **Clickable rows (measured, validated by the pixel-only solver completing 9/9 levels)**: camera y = character row×6 − 36 (default gravity) / − 26 (reversed); clicked map row = `((screen_y + camera_y) // 6)` (`screen_y` and `camera_y` are descriptive placeholders for display y and camera offset, not engine identifiers). The clickable range is therefore **character row −6 through +4** (default) / **−5 through +6** (reversed): 11 rows by default and 12 when reversed (edge rows may be only partially visible), independent of column (column 0 is always at the screen's left edge). This window is the real obstacle in level 8: its sole color-8 block is embedded in the wall at **row 2**, so the character must first find a way to stop at row 8 to click it.
- `ACTION7`: **Undo**, restoring the scene before the previous action (including deleted blocks, gravity direction, water level, and its parity counter), all the way back to the level start. **Undo itself costs one move and does not refund moves already spent**; even an empty stack costs a move.
- `RESET`: restarts the current level and resets the move count if an action has already been sent in it; at action count 0 (e.g. just after a level change), or after completing the game, it returns to level 1 (official engine default; `ONLY_RESET_LEVELS=true` restricts resets to the current level before game completion). Changing levels rebuilds the scene: gravity returns to upward, and water level/parity counter/Undo are reset.

**Five clickable block types** (four look like 5×5 blocks with a color-5 rim and color-3 corners, distinguished by their **core color**; “dotted 12” is the sole exception, without a rim or corners, consisting only of 5 color-12 dots in an X)

| Core color | Effect |
|---|---|
| **14** | Disappears when clicked. Simple removable terrain. |
| **8** | Disappears when clicked and **reverses gravity**. Single use; many levels embed these in the ceiling/floor wall, requiring the camera to reach that end before they can be clicked. |
| **Solid 12** | Clicking changes it into a same-color **dotted (phased-out)** version that can be passed through. |
| **Dotted 12** | Clicking makes it solid again, blocking the way / providing a floor again. |
| **15** | Disappears when clicked and grows an identical block in each of the **four empty adjacent cells** (up/down/left/right). New blocks can be clicked and split again, allowing structures to grow. “Empty” means absolutely nothing occupies the cell: neither the character's cell nor a dotted-12 cell counts as empty, so no block grows there. **New blocks are created before computing where the character lands**, as described next. |

**Click/fall interaction**: If the clicked block is **adjacent to the character in the gravity direction** (underfoot), its disappearance/phasing-out immediately makes the character fall along gravity. This is the main way to change height deliberately. The following three points are measured corrections:

- **The landing position is computed from the board before the block is removed**. The engine computes the landing position first, then queues the removal animation, effectively “searching for a landing position in the fall direction starting from the clicked cell.”
- **Clicking a color-15 splitting block underfoot makes the character fall only one cell.** Its four new blocks are created **before** the landing position is computed; one lands exactly in the next cell beyond and immediately catches the character. Repeated clicks thus drill one cell per move, leaving a wall of blocks on each side. Levels 8 and 9 can use this “drilling” and its horizontal counterpart (click the adjacent block to split it, then step into the vacated cell, with the newly grown block providing floor/ceiling). Level 7 has no splitting blocks yet.
- **After a color-8 reversal, only five things count as support**: rock, color 14, solid 12, color 8, and color 15. **Dotted 12 is absent from this list**, so if it lies underfoot after reversal, the character passes through it and keeps falling. (The engine's forced-fall check explicitly lists these five names.) Whichever color-8 block is clicked, reversal always recomputes the character's landing position.

**Spikes (lethal)**: Color-15 cup-shaped cells whose opening (a color-5 rim + a color-11/0 horizontal bar) faces the incoming character. There are thus two orientations, matching default and reversed gravity. The source uses different sprite names for the orientations, but **their death checks are identical**; orientation is cosmetic. **Ending a fall on a spike loses**; walking into one horizontally merely blocks movement and does not kill. “Falling” includes a one-cell fall: step sideways with a spike immediately adjacent to the new position in the gravity direction, and that spike is the fall endpoint, **killing immediately**. Long falls are not the only danger.

**Rising water (levels 1–3 only)**: A large color-15 body with color-5 speckles and a jagged surface advances upward from the screen bottom. An internal counter **increments by 1 for every left/right action or click (including ineffective clicks)**. The surface rises one cell only on a **left/right action that causes no fall** (including a wall bump), when the counter is even. Clicks and fall-producing movement do not themselves advance the water, but change subsequent parity: for example, opening with “click empty space → move left” advances the water. Undo restores this counter but does not refund the outer move charge. On a water advance, the code compares the surface row **before rising** with the character's original row and sets lose only if they match; this must not be generalized into a contact check after every action. The water body is solid (can support the character); its surface row is passable.

**Win/loss priority and observation limits**: Normally the 64th/128th charged action exhausts the budget, but **reaching the gem in that same action wins first** (`Bp35.step` checks the inner win before lose). This differs from CD82/CN04, which reject the final action before executing it. Off-camera terrain and the gem are invisible. Camera offset hints at gravity, but water parity and complete Undo history have no separately readable UI and cannot be inferred from current position alone.

**Verified edge behavior**: After reversing gravity, the cell “directly below in the new gravity direction” is not checked for spikes: if it contains a spike, the character stops on that spike cell without dying.
