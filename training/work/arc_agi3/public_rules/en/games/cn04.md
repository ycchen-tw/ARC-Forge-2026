**Core rule**: Each level provides 2–5 visible wireframe shapes (levels 5 and 6 also have “stacks,” described below), with **two pin types** along their edges: color `8` and color `13`.
Select one shape at a time and translate/rotate it to bring pins together in pairs at the same cell. **Victory requires every pin on every visible shape to be paired**, with these pairing rules:
Count each color separately: **exactly 2 color-8 pins** at a cell form a pair, and **exactly 2 color-13 pins** also form a pair; 3 or more of the same color do not. 8+8+13 pairs the two 8s but leaves 13 unpaired; 8+8+13+13 can form two pairs simultaneously. **A single color 8 overlapping a single color 13 is not a pair**.

**The biggest trap: both pin types look identical, and the “paired” display ignores type** [tested]
- On level entry, every shape receives `color_remap(13, 8)`, so **color-13 pins also appear as color 8**. The screen alone cannot distinguish the types.
- The screen paints pins color `3` (paired) when “exactly 2 pins occupy this cell, regardless of 8 or 13”; victory instead requires “exactly 2 of the same type.”
  Thus **one 8 overlapping one 13 is shown as paired color 3, but does not count**.
  Measured in level 1: each of the two pieces has 1 color-8 and 1 color-13 pin. With B at (4,6), A's 13 overlaps B's 8; the cell appears color 3, but there are 0 same-type pairs and no victory. With B at (4,4), the two 13s overlap, giving 1 same-type pair (the two 8s are still disconnected, so there is still no victory).
- Reading limitation (in levels 3–6 only the selected piece displays pins, requiring individual inspection first): if all visible pins become color 3 without a win, some 8s are paired with 13s; try another pairing.
- Three or more pins in one cell produce another difference: for example, with 8+8+13, victory checking counts the two 8s as a pair, while the display does not show color 3 because the total is 3.

**Action mapping**
- `ACTION6(x,y)`: search the sprite list **in order** for the first shape that is “visible, and has a nontransparent rendered pixel at the clicked cell” (clicking transparency inside its bbox does not select it). This is list order, not the topmost drawn shape: overlapping shapes can result in selecting an obscured piece.
  - Click another shape = select it.
  - Click the **selected** shape: if it is a stack and the clicked cell renders as **color 0** ⇒ switch to the next variant; otherwise ⇒ deselect (direction actions and ACTION5 then do nothing, but still count).
  - `ACTION6` **does not check for victory** afterward.
- `ACTION1/2/3/4`: move the selected shape one cell up/down/left/right. Bounds are checked on both axes together (movement requires all of `x>=0 and y>=0 and x+w<=gw and y+h<=gh`); illegal movement stays in place but still counts. Victory is checked afterward.
- `ACTION5`: ordinary selected shape ⇒ rotate 90° clockwise (anchored at the bbox upper-left); stack ⇒ **switch variant, without rotating**.
  Then **clamp the position back inside the board** according to the new footprint (both ACTION5 rotation and variant switching do this; added in the current version), and check victory. **Clicking color 0 to switch variants has no clamping step and no victory check**: a larger variant can temporarily extend beyond the board. Direction actions can then move it only if that single step brings the entire bbox back inside; ACTION5 clamps it back.
- Victory advances to the next level within the same action (`step()` first sets a flag without calling `complete_action()`; when the engine calls `step()` again within that action, it executes `next_level()`).
- **Default selection on level entry**: the visible shape with the smallest `x**2 + y**2`.

**Stacks (levels 5 and 6, added in the current version)**
- Shapes sharing the same initial `(x,y)` form a stack. On level entry they are sorted by their data layer, then all set to layer 0. **Only one variant is visible at a time**; invisible variants participate in neither pairing nor victory checks.
- Switching (ACTION5, or clicking a color-0 cell of the selected variant) hides the old variant, places the next variant at the **current position** (not its initial position), and selects it; only the ACTION5 path subsequently clamps it inside the board.
- Each stack's largest variant (the 6×7 piece with 5/2 pins in level 5, and the 6×8 and 5×9 pieces in level 6) **has no color-0 cells**. Clicking it while selected only deselects it; ACTION5 is the only way to switch away.
- Switching walks **back and forth** through the stack (0→1→…→last→backward). The direction flag is one variable shared by the entire level, reset to “forward” on level entry.
- Variants differ in size and pin count (see the level table), effectively allowing the player to choose among shapes for pairing.

**Display modes** (the level data's `GreyMasking` flag; enabled for levels 3–6 in the current version, only level 5 in the old version)
- Disabled (levels 1 and 2): the selected shape's body is painted color `0`, with pins remaining 8/3; other shapes show their true colors.
- Enabled (levels 3–6): **unselected** shapes are painted entirely color **4** (their silhouettes remain visible; color 4 is not the background of any level). Only the selected shape displays its true colors (color 13 still renders as 8).
  Unselected shapes' **paired pins do not appear color 3 either**: the code first paints the whole shape 4, then looks for cells with value 8/13 to change to 3, which it can never find (the same implementation as the old version).
  Only the selected piece thus shows its own pins and pairing status; click through each piece to learn its pin positions.

**How shapes are drawn**
- A pin is a single cell attached to a wireframe edge. Bodies are single-color wireframes, with different body colors within each level. Most stack variants also have color-`0` body cells (click targets for switching); exceptions are listed above.
- Pins do not carry their owner's body color. A pin adjoining two bodies cannot be assigned to an owner from the image alone.
- The old statement “every shape has an even pin count” **does not hold in the current version** (e.g. level 5's first piece has 2 color-8 + 1 color-13 pins).

**Differences from old `cn04-65d47d14`** (the old version was covered by this page before 2026-09-12)
- The old version has 5 levels, the current one 6, with reordering: comparing initial images (64×64 pixels), current level 3 is closest to old level 4 (378 differing pixels), level 4 to old level 5 (81 differing pixels),
  level 5 to old level 3 (639 differing pixels), while level 6 is new; levels 1 and 2 also differ by 180 and 81 pixels respectively. The old level table does not apply to the current version.
- The old version had only color-8 pins, a fixed 150 actions per level, invisibility only in level 5, no stacks, and no clamping after rotation.
- The old version already reset the count each level; the earlier claim here of “150 actions shared across the game” was wrong (official engine `set_level` resets the count to 0).

**Reset, collisions, and priority**: Every operation (including blank clicks, deselection, hitting the boundary, and direction/rotation actions with nothing selected) costs 1 action. Shapes have no mutual collision constraints and may pass through or overlap one another. Action MaxSteps loses first, without movement or a victory check. Earlier direction actions check pairing whenever a piece is selected, even when they hit the boundary and stay in place. Changing levels resets positions/variants/shared switching direction/selection/action count. Official `RESET` restarts the current level if an action has already been sent in it; immediately after a level change at action count 0, or after completing the game, it returns to level 1 (`ONLY_RESET_LEVELS=true` restricts resets to the current level before game completion). Undo is not available. The true pin types and the shared switching direction have no independent UI; gray-masked shapes and hidden variants require interaction to observe, and color 3 is not proof of victory.

> Note: the current version calls its parent class **without** `available_actions`, relying on official `arcengine`'s default `[1,2,3,4,5,6]`.
> The repo's reconstructed engine (`work/arc_agi3/runtime/arcengine`) treats omission as an empty list, so current cn04 cannot accept any actions on that engine. This is a reconstructed-engine bug, not game behavior.

The solver selects each piece to read its pins, observes full cycles of stack variants on the spot, then searches combinations.
Since pixels cannot distinguish the pin types, a pairing where “all geometric pins are paired but the level does not clear” is rejected in favor of another. If every candidate for a variant combination fails, that whole combination is rejected and another observed combination is tried. No levels or action tables are preloaded.
The image is used to recognize gray-masking mode; score is used only to clear observations from the previous level. Observed shapes may overlap during transport; the selected piece being on top and other pieces staying still allow continued localization.
Validation covers the six current levels and those six handoff points, without exhaustively testing all intermediate board states.
