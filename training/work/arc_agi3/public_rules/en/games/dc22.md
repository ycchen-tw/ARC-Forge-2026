> **Current-version display and validation**: In level 6, the player and crane share a layer, and the crane can cover the player; measurements found only 2 color-14 player cells exposed. The solver's 2×2 player recognition must allow “the missing pixels are exactly occluded by an already-recognized crane.” The crane frame and suspended bridge's hook both use color 8, so same-color flood fill alone cannot separate them; occluded areas are filled in only when the exposed pixels on both sides belong to the bridge. The player or pillar may also completely hide a portal or key; a mid-game handoff must move the obstruction away and observe again.
>
> **Measured on 2026-10-20**: `runtime/pixel/solvers/dc22.py` completed all six levels from screen images alone under official arcengine 0.9.3 and unmodified `fdcac232` source. Dense handoff tests covered the same 420-action sequence after actions 5, 15, …, 415; all 42/42 fresh instances completed the game. The old search-based solver's results for levels 1–4 below serve only as historical mechanism validation. [tested] means behavior was previously confirmed by execution; collision/underfoot/key-pickup checks in the body were additionally checked against current source and a single-pixel probe.

**Core rule**: The player is a 2×2 color-14 block (`jfva`), and the goal is a 2×2 color-11 block (`goknoi`) [tested]. Terrain occupies the left half of the screen; the right half is a color-5 UI panel, separated by a color-0 line (**[correction]** this line is **dashed**, 2 solid cells then 2 empty, and level 6's goal is actually hidden in the UI half; see below). Background color 4 is void, and **the player can stand only on “floor”**: fixed color-2 blocks (`tacugo-plelvb`, etc.) plus various shape-changing colored bridge tiles. `ACTION1-4` moves **2 cells** at a time [tested]; a solid obstruction or absence of floor at the destination leaves the player in place (still costing 1 move) [tested]. Reaching **exactly the same upper-left coordinates** as the goal clears the level; exhausting the budget loses [tested].

**Exact meaning of the underfoot check** [tested]: `sxnzvaqltp` checks only **the cell at the player's upper-left corner**, accepts only sprites with `InteractionMode.INTANGIBLE`, and explicitly skips tags `vcha`, `crzsjq`, and `ignore`. The crane body and level 6's track-network tiles therefore **are not floor**; the goal block `goknoi` is floor (it is INTANGIBLE).

The terrain itself is the puzzle: the UI has several **colored buttons**, each bound to a letter group (internally `a`–`h`). Clicking one advances **all tiles in that group simultaneously to their next form**. All forms at each location are stacked in the same cell when the level loads; switching merely changes which is active. Thus a panel phase change itself **changes only shape/passability**; a bridge attached to the crane still translates with the crane [tested]. Group a's panel is color 8, b's color 9, c's color 6, and d's color 15, matching the tiles they control [tested]. Panels e (color-2, 6×6 `drfztmbrixto-buezna`) and f (color-11, 7×7 `sprite-6`) break that rule: their tiles are color 1/color 12 [tested].

**Action mapping**
- `ACTION1` up, `ACTION2` down, `ACTION3` left, `ACTION4` right: 2 cells at a time, normally costing 1 move [tested]. **Victory is checked only along this direction-action path, before charging**: a successful direction action advances immediately without charging. Even actions that “return to the original position because of no floor/collision” check victory. Thus teleporting onto the goal still requires another direction action to finalize the win; that action can win with only 1 move remaining.
- `ACTION6(x,y)`: click. Panel = switch its group (**2 moves**) [tested]; blank space = 1 move [tested]; crane buttons are described below.
- **[Correction]** `ACTION5` **is not a no-op**: it takes the “no direction” branch in `step()` and still costs 1 move (like a blank click). It is simply absent from `available_actions=[1,2,3,4,6]`, so the official harness will not send it. `ACTION7` (Undo) is likewise unavailable.

**Four tile-form families** (all driven by panel clicks)
1. **Toggle** (2 forms, same location): **solid = walkable; alternating/checkerboard = cannot stand on it**. This is the most important visual cue. Multiple tiles in a group often begin at opposite phases, so “opening this one closes that one” [tested].
   **[Correction]**: The checkerboard's “empty cells” have pixel value `-2` in the data. **DC22's custom** `collides_with` uses a `>= 0` mask, and the underfoot probe `sxnzvaqltp` also skips negative values, so these cells neither collide nor count as floor; the engine's default `Sprite.collides_with` (which counts `-2` as a hit) does not apply. The [single-pixel probe](../../../runtime/pixel/probes/dc22_collision.py) measured this difference. Moreover, a checkerboard phase **is not necessarily a solid wall**: when a level places the “solid” version, the automatically generated checkerboard twin inherits the `omvz` tag and becomes INTANGIBLE (nonblocking). Its solid pixels occur only where (x+y) is odd, while the player's upper-left coordinates are always even/even in all six levels (all six starts and every portal have even coordinates, and direction actions move 2 cells). The player therefore still **cannot stand on it**, with the same net effect for a different reason (“no floor” rather than “wall collision”).
2. **Rotating** (2 forms, same location):
   - **[Correction]** `refgps-plelvb` (10×10, level 1) **is not an L-shaped corner tile**. It is a 4-wide short bridge rotating about a 4×4 **color-13 pivot** in a corner: phase 1 extends 6 cells left from the pivot (horizontal), and phase 2 extends 6 cells upward (vertical). Both phases are walkable.
   - `brixto*` (20×20) is a 4-wide, 20-long large bridge with a central 4×4 color-13 rotation-center mark, spanning 20 cells horizontally ↔ 20 cells vertically [tested].
3. **Multiphase** (6 or 8 forms) [tested, pixels compared phase by phase]:
   - `drfztmbrixto-*` is a 2×16 **iris-like extending/retracting bridge** with 8 phases: phase 1 **completely absent**; 2/3/4 grow inward from both ends (2/4/6 cells each, color 1); **phase 5 fully connects (solid color 12)**; 6/7/8 mirror 4/3/2 as it retracts.
   - `moxubw-plelvb-*` is a 6-wide **translating platform** within a 2×16 area, moving 2 cells right per phase through 6 phases (offsets 0/2/4/6/8/10); **phase 6 changes to color 12**. Since the underfoot check looks only at the player's upper-left cell, “walk two cells → click once → walk two more” lets the platform carry the player rightward like an escalator.
4. **Portals `tewfut*`** (2×2 blocks whose two forms exchange checkerboard phases of the same color pair) [tested]: when the player **stands exactly at a portal's upper-left coordinates**, clicking its group's panel teleports the player to the other portal of the same color family; being one cell off does not trigger it at all [tested: at (6,8), click c → teleport to (12,38); at (5,8) → no movement]. The entire group also changes phase as usual. Four color families: `tewfutpibpar` = 6/7, `tewfutrefgps` = 8/13, `tewfutyefmyf` = 11/12, `tewfutblrmbx` = 9/10 [tested].

**Falling and penalty** [tested]
After every `ACTION6` whose coordinates successfully map onto the board (including blank clicks, not just `sys_click`), the program rechecks whether floor remains under the player's upper-left cell. **No floor means a fall**: a shrinking black curtain animation (color 5) plays for **15 frames**, then **20 moves are deducted** (the triggering click incurs no additional charge: exactly −20 total), and the entire scene **rolls back to the snapshot before this click** (each direction action and click saves a snapshot). If the deduction leaves ≤ 0, it loses immediately without rollback. Direction actions toward floorless positions return to the original position; thus, from a normally supported state, this check handles switching off the floor underfoot, driving away a crane carrying the bridge underfoot, and teleporting onto a floorless position. Rollback includes panel phases/keys/attachment state and does not refund the 20-move penalty.

**Keys `piyqze-*`** [tested; the first previously unconfirmed point in the original analysis is now established]: small ground objects **4 wide × 2 high** (the original “2×4” reverses width×height), collected when the player's 2×2 footprint touches them. Collection applies `panel._interaction = InteractionMode.INTANGIBLE` to the same-letter hidden panel. **INTANGIBLE means “visible but noncollidable,” so the panel becomes both visible and clickable**; measurements after collection found `is_visible=True` and a hit from the game's custom click probe `xodizggcom`. Keys are one-use and irreversible.
(Additional finding: the key sprite's two upper corner pixels are `-2`, invisible and nonblocking, but the 2×2 pickup probe `yhthylsyvr` explicitly counts `-2` as a hit, so its pickup area extends beyond what is visible.)

**Pressure plates (level 6 only)** [tested, fully consistent with the original analysis]: four 2×2 ground plates form a diamond: top (34,56) = group `b`, left (32,58) = `e`, right (36,58) = `a`, bottom (34,60) = `h`. A corresponding crane direction button in the UI becomes visible/clickable only **while the player stands on its plate**, disappearing on departure. UI background `coorbs-bg-1` already draws four empty slots (color-4, 4×4 frames), each with a small colored corner dot. A ground plate has pixels `[[1,0],[0,X]]`, with lower-right X in that same color: **slot dot = plate dot = direction-button color** (up = color 15, left = color 12, right = color 14, down = color 10) [tested: all three sets of colors compared and matched]. A portal `tewfutblrmbx2` also sits at the diamond's center (34,58), allowing a single teleport to this control station.

**Crane `crzsjq` / `brixtocrzsjq` (levels 5 and 6)**

UI locations (both levels are 64×64; these are **display click coordinates**): L5's four direction buttons in one row are **left/up/right/down**, at `(44,29)`/`(49,29)`/`(54,29)`/`(59,29)`; grab is at `(50,35)`. In L6, panel `f` is at `(56,8)` and `c` at `(50,25)`; after collecting the respective keys, grab is at `(50,18)` and color-cycle panel `d` at `(50,47)`.
- An 8×8 machine, **unaffected by direction actions**; it is controlled only by clicking four direction buttons and one grab button in the UI. Each press moves it 4 cells (in its own lattice coordinate system) [tested].
- **Does it block the player?** [tested; the second previously unconfirmed point in the original analysis is now established]: **Level 5's `crzsjq-1` is collidable and really blocks the player**. Collision is pixel-level, so only its claw-shaped solid pixels block (the middle of the right-facing claw opening is transparent and can be approached through). **Level 6's `brixtocrzsjq-1` is INTANGIBLE, neither blocking the player nor serving as floor.** The code indeed never resets the collision property; the difference comes entirely from level data.
- Track:
  - Level 5 uses a hard-coded 4×4 lattice (coordinates `(2+4·crzsjq_x, 24−4·crzsjq_y)`), but **up/down is possible only in the leftmost column (`crzsjq_x==0`), and left/right only in the top and bottom rows (`crzsjq_y == 0 or crzsjq_y == 3`)**. This forms a three-sided track open to the right [tested: up is blocked at `crzsjq_x==1`, but works after returning to 0]. Color-0 `sprite-9` strips draw the track (tagged `ignore`, purely decorative and nonblocking).
  - Level 6's track is a route made from 19 `vcha` tiles (`bg`, color-0 4×4 blocks). The crane's **center** must lie on a track cell to move [tested]. **[Correction]** In this mode, `crzsjq_x/crzsjq_y` **have no 0–3 bounds** (measured down to −4); the track network alone constrains movement.
- Grab button: pressing it when the hook point **coincides with a movable object's center** attaches the object, changes the crane icon to its “loaded” appearance, and makes the object follow forever afterward. **There is no release branch in the code: attachment cannot be undone** [tested: pressing grab again leaves the same object attached]. Hook position: in level 5 (hard-coded mode), `(crzsjq.x+9, crzsjq.y+4)`, or **one cell beyond the crane's right edge**; in level 6 (network mode), the crane's own center `(x+4, y+4)` [tested].
- Move charges [tested]: level 5's direction buttons carry only `sys_click`, not `buezna`; a successful move costs 1, and bumping against a blocked track also costs 1. **Pressing grab with nothing in range merely flashes 5 times (6 frames in the runtime) and costs no moves**; a successful grab costs 1. Level 6's direction buttons are also `buezna` panels, so **successful movement costs 2 moves**, but **blocked movement costs only 1** (the blocked path returns early, before the additional panel charge).
- The movable object itself is walkable floor: level 5 has `sprite-10`, a color-12 pillar centered at (23,16), whose **6-wide ×12-high bounding box includes a side hook; the main body is only 4 cells wide, and transparent notches provide no floor**; the crane must reach lattice point (3,3) to grab it [tested]. Level 6 has the 20×20 large bridge `brixto-orckhi`, **which the `c` panel can still rotate while suspended** [tested]. The crane effectively provides “a bridge you can drive around,” and is also the only thing that can pull floor out from under the player.
- **Historical evidence that level 5 requires the crane**: an old-version exhaustive search using only the four panels and never the crane **exhausted 1920 states**, without reaching goal (10,50). Current data still has start (32,34), goal (10,50), and the liftable pillar. The current completion route uses the crane, but this review did not redo an exhaustive current-version proof that completion without it is impossible; a completion PASS cannot replace necessity evidence.

**Color-cycling portal (level 6 only)** [tested]: For level 6, `on_set_level` specifically tags the portal at (18,48) with `tewfut-color-cycle` and assigns it to group `d`. Group `d`'s panel `renrjo-buezna` is a 4×4 button whose four cells show colors 8/11/10/7 (one from each portal family), unlocked by the `d` key. Clicking it **cycles that portal's colors**: `tewfutpibpar`(6/7) → `tewfutrefgps`(8/13) → `tewfutyefmyf`(11/12) → `tewfutblrmbx`(9/10) → back to 6/7 [tested: four successive clicks return to the original]. The destination is “the other portal of the same color family in the same letter group,” so changing color changes destination: 6/7 leads to the platform beside the start (32,52), 11/12 to the upper-left isolated island (4,4), and 9/10 to the pressure-plate control station (34,58); 8/13 has no counterpart here (a dead color). This panel also uses a **dedicated branch**: it changes only color, **without** changing other group tiles' phases or triggering teleportation.

**Level 6's goal is hidden in the UI half** [tested, missed by the original analysis]: Goal `goknoi-dokmdr` is at (46,6), to the right of the divider. UI base `coorbs-bg-1` has an 80-pixel transparent hole at x40–49 / y4–11, and divider `merged-sprite-1` is already dashed (2 solid, 2 empty), so the player can cross horizontally into the UI half at **y ≡ 2 (mod 4)** (the two rows y=6 or y=10). Measured: four rightward moves from (38,6) reach (46,6) → `game_over=True, won=True` (game complete).
Incidentally, `goknoi-dokmdr` itself also carries `buezna` but has no letter tag, so clicking the goal wastes 2 moves and does nothing.

**Other verified details**
- A nonwinning branch loses when the remaining budget reaches zero; `ncuydqtllw` **subtracts first, then checks `>0`**. Normally at least 1 must remain after charging, but saying “only StepCounter−1 moves can be spent” would miss the exception that direction actions check victory first. `RESET` restarts the current level and restores the full budget if an action has already been sent in it; it returns to level 1 only at action count 0 (e.g. just after a level change) or after completing the game (`ONLY_RESET_LEVELS=true` restricts resets to the current level before game completion).
- Changing or restarting a level rebuilds it from clean sprite data: keys/hidden panels/pressure plates/phases/crane position/attachment relations/remaining budget all reset; there are no cross-level items. No Undo is available; falling rollback is only automatic recovery from that click.
- `merged-sprite*` dividers in all six levels use the same 2-solid, 2-empty dash pattern (opaque rows `y mod 4 ∈ {0,1}`). The first five levels merely lack floor on the right, concealing the gaps' significance [tested].
- A complete scene snapshot (`vqqdlnuxnr`) is saved at the start of each `sys_click` action, and a fall rolls back to it.
- Level 5's crane direction buttons carry letter tags such as `a`/`b`/`e` but **no** `buezna`, so those letters are inert in that level (corresponding panel clicks collect them into the group, but phase switching immediately skips them because their names do not end in a digit).

**Historical limitations and source notes** (the unused-sprite/dead-code lists below were statically checked only for the old version)
- **The historical limitation is resolved**: the old solver searching directly through the engine did not converge on levels 5 and 6; the current pixel solver has completed all six end to end. It builds its own transition model from images, without engine search or per-level action tables.
- Current `swwqomvyrt` (10181–10198) checks whether a large bridge moved by the crane overlaps another active large bridge. **Level 2 does have two large bridges**, so the old claim “no two large bridges ever coexist in any of the six levels” was wrong. However, level 2 has no crane, and level 6 has only one large bridge when it can be lifted; therefore existing levels will not trigger this restriction because of “another large bridge.” This is a static conclusion from current data and branch conditions, without exhaustive state search.
- **Unused-sprite list [tested; the original analysis was correct but missed 3]**: after loading all six levels and cycling level 6's color-changing portal all the way around, 81 names actually appeared. The 99 definitions comprise 81 instantiated names, 2 definitions used only for loaded-crane pixels, and **16 never instantiated**: `bgeg-collision1/2` (14×12 solid color 5), `hhxv-jbyz1/2` (20×20 color 9/10), `hhxv-upry1-1/-2` (20×20 color 6/7), `jpug-bjuk-zbhi` (13×5 color 6), `kbqq-efzv-2`, `sprite-1/5/7/11`, `sprite_101` (4×4 fully transparent), **plus three omitted by the original analysis: `itkibgeg2` (color changes only stop at phase 1, so the bgeg family's phase 2 is never used), `sprite_81-3` (a fifth, color-11 pressure plate), and `zbhi-jpug-bjuk` (color-6 `d` key)**. Also, `nxhz-2` and `hhxvnxhz-2` are never independent sprites: they only supply pixels for the “loaded” appearance, copied onto the crane.
- `pbgnzhjtys()` (derive direction from crane lattice position) and `paydlrciup()` are dead code, never called [confirmed by grep].
- **Historical patch only for the reconstructed engine (official validation does not depend on it)**: dc22 writes `sprite._interaction = ...` directly when collecting keys, while this project's `arcengine.Sprite._interaction` originally had only a getter, raising `AttributeError`. A setter was added (equivalent to `set_interaction`). That assignment itself directly demonstrates that official-engine `_interaction` is the **primary storage**, from which `is_visible/is_collidable` are derived.

**Observability**: Images reveal unobscured player/floor/bridge phases/panels/crane state, but matching colors or occlusion do not establish that pixels belong to one object. Hidden panels and obscured keys/portals are not guaranteed to be visible at the time. The fall snapshot has no on-screen state field, so a single image cannot reconstruct the complete rollback state; the bottom 64-pixel bar also cannot show remaining budgets of 128–1024 with exact move-by-move precision.
