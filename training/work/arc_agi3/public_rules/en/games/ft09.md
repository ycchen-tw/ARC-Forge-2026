**Core rule**: The board (32×32 cells in every level) contains 3×3 “light tiles,” with centers 4 cells apart (3-cell tile + 1-cell gap). Clicking a light tile **cycles it to the next color** (the cycle is defined by level data `cwU`). The board also contains **clue tiles**; each clue specifies the required light colors in its 8 surrounding directions. **Satisfy every clue simultaneously to clear the level.**

On entering a level, **all lights start at color index 0 of the cycle**.

The progress bar has only 64 pixels and is not an exact remaining-action counter: normal play in level 6 showed all pixels in spent color 11 with 1 of 128 actions still available; the next unsolved effective click lost. Use the action history and game state to track the exact balance.

**Three click outcomes (measured)**
- Click a **light tile** → change color → check for victory → spend 1 action only if the level remains unsolved.
- Click a **clue tile or blank space** → no color change, **no budget cost and no victory check**. In level 1 only, clicking a non-clue blank/demonstration area also plays a flashing hint.
- **Victory is checked before spending budget**: With budget N, N−1 unsolved effective clicks are allowed; click N can still solve the level directly, otherwise reaching zero loses. A blank click cannot request another victory check.

**Reading a clue tile (the central rule to infer)**: A clue is a 3×3 pattern. Its center pixel is its “reference color” (always in the cycle). Each of its 8 edge pixels corresponds to a direction:
- Color **0** (white) → the light in that direction **must equal** the reference color.
- Color **2** (gray) → that light **must differ from** the reference color.
- Color **3** (dark gray) → **there is no light in that direction**. Measured across all directions in all 6 levels with zero exceptions: color 3 ⟺ no light there; color 0/2 ⟺ a light is present.
  The source actually checks only “is it 0,” putting 3 into the “not equal” branch; however, without a light in that direction, the whole constraint is skipped, so both readings give the same result. **For interpreting the board, only “color 3 = no light here” makes sense.**

**No light in a direction → skip that constraint entirely.** Outside the board, or a cell occupied by another clue (for example, the vertically adjacent clues at `(11,18)` and `(11,22)` in level 5), both count as no light.

**Two types of light tile**
- Ordinary light (`Hkx`): A solid 3×3 tile in one cycle color. Clicking changes only itself.
- **Linked light (`NTi` family)**: Clicking it **changes itself and also** a set of neighbors. The affected neighbors are drawn on the tile: **color 6** cells mark relative directions that also change, again in units of 4 cells. Color 6 is not repainted when colors change, so this pattern remains visible throughout the level; the pattern itself is the instruction. Nothing happens in a linked direction with no light.
  - Level 5 `NTi` has a **cross** (the 4 cardinal directions).
  - Level 6 `ZkU` marks **just one direction: directly above**, rather than a cross.

**Color-cycle legend**: A stack of **2×2-cell** color swatches at the upper right, **ordered top to bottom in cycle order**. It is not always against the right edge (x=27 in level 5, x=30 elsewhere). **Level 1 has no legend**; it teaches through three already-solved demonstration boards instead. Apart from the legend, row 0 always has the background color, making the legend easy to identify.

**Solution**: Each clue supplies one constraint for each direction **that actually contains a light**. Intersect these constraints to get each light's allowed color set.
With ordinary lights only, lights are independent: choose the allowed color needing the fewest cycle steps for each. Levels 5 and 6, which have linked lights, both use two-color cycles, so each constrained light has just one allowed color. The problem reduces to Lights Out over GF(2), solvable by Gaussian elimination.
