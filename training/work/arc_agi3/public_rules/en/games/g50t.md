**Core rule**: Move cell by cell through a maze. Enter the hollow color-9 frame (the goal) to clear the level. The check is
`goal sprite coordinates+1 == player coordinates`: **fit exactly inside the frame**.

The map uses **6 px per cell**: every standable position has x, y ∈ {1, 7, 13, …, 61}, in all seven levels.
Each move covers exactly 6 px. Move legality checks only whether the **player's center** falls on a nontransparent pixel of a “floor sprite” and whether a closed gate covers that cell. Clones and patrollers **do not block movement**, and characters do not collide with one another when moving.

**Pressure plates** connect to their devices through visible **colored wires** (wire color = plate color = device color). Wiring is determined by geometric overlap. The code permits one wire to have several outputs, but all 7 levels use “one plate → one wire → one device.” A plate needs someone **standing on it** to work, and you have only one body.

**The central action is `ACTION5`: “record” your current trip as a clone.** Pressing it rewinds the entire trip to the starting point. The old body becomes a color-2 **clone standing at the start**, overlapping the new body, and you restart there with the new body. Thereafter, **each step you take makes every clone replay its recorded step n in sync**. When a recording ends, the clone stays still, so it can hold a plate forever. To hold two plates simultaneously: record a trip to plate A, record another trip to plate B, then send the third body to the goal.

The row of little squares at the upper left is the **body counter** (2 in level 1, 3 in levels 2–7): solid color 1 = unused, hollow color 9 = current body, hollow color 2 = already a clone. The short color-9 line beneath is the cursor.
**Pressing `ACTION5` with the last body, after recording a legal move, clears all clones, returns the player to the start, and resets the body counter to its first slot.**
**This clears bodies/recordings only; toggle gates are not guaranteed to return to their initial states, and spent actions are not refunded.** Thus the usable clone count is square count − 1 (1 in level 1, 2 in levels 2–7).

**Actions**
- `ACTION1/2/3/4`: Up / down / left / right, one cell (6 px).
- `ACTION5`: Save the current trip as a clone, rewind to the start, and switch to the next body. **It has no effect if the current trip contains no moves**, but still costs one action. The entire rewind takes **1 action**.
- No `ACTION6` (click) or `ACTION7` (Undo).

**Only the player advances time.** The first thing `move()` does is check whether the player's own move is legal. **An illegal move returns immediately**: clones stay still, patrollers stay still, everything stays still, but **the action budget is still charged**. There is therefore **no “wait in place for one turn” action**: to advance a clone or patroller one step, you must make a genuinely legal move yourself. A frequent difficulty is arranging enough legal filler steps, which requires detours.

### Recognizing the screen (for a pixel-only agent)

| Object | Appearance |
|---|---|
| Walkable cell | Floor color 5; walls are background color 0. **A standable cell has no color 0 anywhere in its 7×7 area**. Wires are surrounded by a 3 px-wide color-5 conduit that crosses walls, so checking only cell centers can mislead (level 1 (40,16) is such a trap). Apply this test to the underlying map without gate occlusion: an open gate may slide over a wall and hide color 0, which does not create a new walkable cell |
| Player | 7×7 footprint; visible **solid 5×5 color-9 core with one color-5 pixel at its exact center**, rather than a solid block |
| Clone | Same shape, color 2 |
| Patroller | Same shape, color 14 |
| Goal | 9×9 sprite showing a 7×7 color-9 frame, **with a gap**: the middle 5 px of one side are empty, exposing the floor, and the frame center has a color-9 dot. **The gap rotates with the level** (270° in level 3); detect it by requiring “at least three of the four sides are entirely color 9” |
| Pressure plate | Solid 3×3 device-colored block at the cell center |
| Gate | 7×7: color-5 border, device-colored 5×5 interior, and a 1 px **tab** projecting from the center of the opposite side. The tab points in the direction the gate slides open |
| Teleport pad | Hollow 7×7 color-15 outline |
| Wire | 1 px-wide device-colored line; connected regions of the same color form one group |

**Only color-8 gates have a dashed hinge.** The hinge-side row of a color-8 hold-open gate is dashed as `C 5 C 5 C`; **color-11 toggle gates are solid, with no dashed line**. Color distinguishes the two types; when the hinge is visible, its dashed or solid pattern provides another cue.

**There is no direct visual marker of whether a gate is open or closed, but geometry reveals it** (this playtest checked the visible open and closed positions of all 17 gate instances):
**A closed gate always covers a walkable cell, with at least 2 floor cells among its four neighbors. An open gate has slid into a wall, with only the cell it previously covered remaining as a floor neighbor.** For hold-open gates, current plate occupancy provides an additional check.

**A patroller's track is a hidden sprite, but measurements show it exactly matches its floor connected component when gates are ignored.** Every cell matched in levels 6 and 7, and that entire component is inaccessible to the player.

### Four device types (all driven by plate → wire → device)

- **Color 8: hold-open gate**. Something on the plate → the gate slides one cell in the tab direction; it slides back when occupancy ends. With multiple occupants, this is an “anyone present means open” latch: it closes only after the last occupant leaves.
- **Color 11: toggle gate**. **The trigger is the edge from “unoccupied” to “occupied,” rather than each individual arrival.** Another arrival while somebody remains on the plate does not toggle it; two simultaneous arrivals toggle it only once. Leaving after stepping on does not close it, so one body can open it permanently. However, clone replay triggers another occupancy edge, so track the parity yourself.
- **Color 15: teleporter**. One color-15 plate + two color-15 pads. Pressing the plate charges it through a little over ten animation frames, then **swaps the objects registered on the two pads**. If one side is empty, this moves the other side's contents over.
  Crucially, **the swap happens after all characters have registered during that action**, so someone who has just stepped onto a pad in that same action is also transported. In levels 4, 5, and 7 the goal area cannot be reached on foot; teleporting is essential.
- **Color 14: patroller**. Moves along a hidden track, one step per player step. Direction preference is “continue straight → turn left → turn right → turn back,” based on track connectivity. If terrain or a gate actually blocks movement, it reverses direction and retries, up to 4 times. It **presses plates**, its real purpose in levels 6 and 7, and can be **crushed by a closing gate**, after which it stops until `ACTION5` rewinds and revives it. The code makes a patroller overlapping the player cause player death → lose, but **the player cannot enter the patroller's component in any of these 7 levels** (neither pair of teleporters in level 7 leads there). In practice it serves as a remote button rather than a threat.

### Easily missed implementation details (all measured)

1. **Clone playback still checks legality**: A closed gate stops a clone in place, but its playback index still advances. Blocked instructions are never retried, so later positions can differ from the recording; a later reconnection with the route may restore the original position. Planning must check the clone's actual route.
2. **Within one action the order is “release → move → press.”** Leaving a plate takes effect at the instant of movement; pressing waits until all movement in the action is complete. Therefore, “A leaves and B enters in the same step” closes a hold-open gate before reopening it. That instant of closure kills a player standing in the gate cell.
3. **During rewind a teleporter's trigger is reversed**: Releasing the plate, rather than pressing it, causes an **immediate** swap with no charging animation. On the **first rewind step**, the rewinding flag is not set yet, so releasing on that step does not swap. A swap can move someone before other characters have decided where to move; reproducing the order exactly is necessary for agreement.
4. **Rewinding does not guarantee restoring toggle gates**: The start and end cells of a trip are each triggered an odd number of times; intermediate cells are crossed once going out and once coming back, an even number. Thus ending the trip on a toggle plate leaves one toggle behind.
5. **A crushed patroller's sprite is removed, but its coordinates persist and `ayhgaxoxce` still gets called on it**, so it keeps pressing the plate beneath it (source-derived only: no gate covers a plate in these 7 levels, so this situation cannot be produced there).

**Winning and losing**
- Win: Checked after each action, including `ACTION5`. When the **current body**, rather than a clone, is centered in the goal frame, call `next_level()`. A clone on the goal does not count.
- Lose, in two ways: (1) That level's 130th action calls `lose()`, leaving effectively 129 usable actions. The color-9 progress bar on row 63 shrinks leftward by 1 px every 2 actions, but already looks empty after actions 128 and 129 while play can continue; an empty bar is not the loss condition. (2) The current body is struck by a patroller or crushed by a closing gate; after 9 death-animation frames, call `lose()`. This is irreversible; rewind cannot rescue it.
- **Loss is checked before victory**. If both hold in one action, it is a loss.

**Difficulties** (why this is considered especially hard for AI)

1. **`ACTION5` is easily misread as “restart.”** The visible result is the entire trip rewinding to the start, with the clone still at the start and not moving; it looks like a reset. Its purpose emerges only when you start moving again, and when the previous trip happened to end on a plate. A single pair of before/after frames cannot establish “recording + playback.”
2. **The clock is tied to your own body.** Clones and patrollers advance only with **legal player movement**. Walking into walls costs budget without advancing the world. A plan must satisfy both route and timing constraints, with no no-op available.
3. **The two gate types can be distinguished by color and the visible dashed/solid hinge pattern** (8 hold-open / 11 toggle), despite behaving as temporary versus permanent. Clone replay adds another edge trigger, so parity must be tracked across bodies.
4. **Key history is not displayed**: the patroller track, teleporter logic objects, clone recordings, steps taken on the current trip, and starting position require history or structural inference. The current body index is shown in the upper-left HUD; gate positions are visible, and their open/closed meaning can be inferred geometrically as above.
5. **Levels 6 and 7 require treating the “enemy” as a tool**, including the counterintuitive step of first clearing its path.
6. **A resume cannot reconstruct recordings from one frame alone.** If legal movement is available, repeated “move + `ACTION5`” can cycle back to the first body and clear clones. This is a partial reset, and there is no universal guarantee of at most 6 steps. Official-engine level-3 counterexample: repeat `1,1,4,4,4,4,2,2,5` three times. After 27 actions the first body is active with no clones, but the color-11 gate has changed from closed to open. A resumed solver still needs to identify remaining gate/patroller state and spent budget. A true level reset is engine `RESET`, rather than `ACTION5`.

**Still not validated by execution**

- Swapping when **both** pads are occupied is source-derived only: swap both occupancy sets and coordinates. The 7-level solutions did not encounter an actual trigger of this case. The model and engine agreed over 120,000 random actions, but coverage of this case is not guaranteed.
- One wire driving **multiple** output devices is source-derived only; all 7 levels are one-to-one.
- Sprite `gehwhuvxqq` (61×61, color 2, tag `uxqxubarib`) is present and hidden in all 7 levels. No logic reads the tag, so it is classified as dead data.
- Human baselines 175 / 179 / 230 for levels 2, 3, and 4 exceed the 130-action cap (under the old definition, only level 2's 175 did). They cannot be used as the action limit of a single successful route; this page does not infer failure/retry scoring from baselines. The old reconstructed engine cleared level 2 in 31 measured actions.
