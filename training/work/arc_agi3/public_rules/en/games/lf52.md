> The level-data `grid_size=(8,8)` is misleading: all real boards are hardcoded in dictionary `kciatvszkc`, with dimensions varying by level from 7×8 to 28×9. One cell = 6×6 pixels.

**Core rule**: This is **peg solitaire**. Pieces occupy a square grid. A legal move is a **jump capture**: select a piece and move in a cardinal direction; **the adjacent cell must contain a piece or pillar, and the cell beyond it must be empty**. The piece jumps over the middle occupant and captures it. Reduce the piece count to the required remainder to clear the level.

There can be several boards: most levels have 2–7 disconnected boards linked by **black rails** carrying **carts**. A piece can jump onto a cart, ride it using the arrow keys to another board, then jump off. The game combines peg solitaire with logistics.

**Visual elements (color indices)**
- Background = 10 (light blue). Board cells are 6×6 blocks with white (0) borders and off-white (1) centers; each board has a black (5) border and blue (9) shadow.
- Green piece = 14 (can capture and be captured; included in the count).
- Red piece = 8; blue piece = 9 (see below).
- **Cart** = block with yellow (11) border and orange (12) center.
- **Pillar** = purple (15) block with white (0)/pink (7) inside and a black bottom edge.
- **Rail** = thin black (5) line.
- A selected piece is surrounded by a **dark-gray (3) ring**; every legal destination has a flashing **gray (2) hollow ring**.

**Actions**
- `ACTION6(x,y)`: The only way to move a piece, in two stages.
  1. Click a piece to select it (dark-gray ring) and display gray hollow rings at **every legal destination**. A piece with no legal moves is not selected; it only shakes in place.
  2. Click a gray ring to execute the jump capture. Clicking blank space normally cancels selection; **level 1 with all five initial pieces remaining is an exception**: it shows the hint and preserves selection, so a subsequent destination click can still execute the jump.
- `ACTION1/2/3/4` (up/down/left/right): **Every cart attempts to move one cell in that direction**. Carts are processed from the front of the movement direction backward. A cart moves only if its destination has rail and is not occupied by another cart at that instant; its piece/pillar travels with it. Carts do not push one another, but a rear cart can follow into a cell already vacated by the front cart during the same action, allowing an entire contiguous train to advance.
- `ACTION7`: Undo returns to the state before the previous directional/click action, **including the action counter** (see below). Selection, blank clicks, and actions in which no cart can move each have their own undo point. With no history, internal state stays unchanged.

**Special mechanisms**

1. **Carts and rails**: A cart is itself a landable cell: pieces can jump onto it, and its cell counts as empty for landing checks. Once it reaches another board, its piece can jump onto that board. The check asks only whether the destination has a rail object; corner/T-junction graphics **do not restrict direction** in code. All four directional actions are **global**: every movable cart attempts to move, so individual carts cannot be controlled separately.

2. **Pillars (purple)**: A piece can jump over a pillar as a stepping stone, but the pillar is **never captured** and **cannot be a landing cell**. It is both an inexhaustible resource and an obstacle.

3. **Red / blue pieces = movable stepping stones that do not disappear**: Both can be selected and jump themselves, but
   - jumping over either does not capture it;
   - when either jumps over another piece, it does not capture that piece, and only plays an afterimage animation.

   These non-capture rules describe the existing levels, which each have at most one red piece. The actual source condition for capture is “jumper and middle piece have the same name, and the middle piece is not blue.” Green jumping over green captures; jumping over red/blue/pillars does not. Existing red pieces have no same-type piece to capture. **Red pieces** count toward the total, so levels 6 and 7 require **2 pieces remaining** (1 green + 1 red). From level 8 onward, the initial number of **blue pieces** is subtracted from the total, leaving a threshold of **1 counted piece remaining**.

4. **Camera scrolling**: Most boards are wider than the 64×64 view (up to 28 cells = 168px), which acts as a moving window. Two triggers cause scrolling:
   - **Moving a cart carrying a green piece** shifts the whole board: level 3 scrolls horizontally only, 8px per step; levels 5 and 6 horizontally only, 6px per step; level 8 vertically only, 6px per step; levels 4 and 9 in both axes, 6px per step (in level 4, scrolling is suppressed when the green piece’s board-row index before movement satisfies `row >= 11`, including row 11 itself); levels 1, 2, 7, and 10 never scroll. Carrying red, blue, or a pillar does not scroll. An additional guard prevents further rightward scrolling once the view has returned to the origin. This guard directly `return`s from the entire cart-moving loop, so **all carts later in the processing order also stay still on that step**; the cart that triggered scrolling has already moved.
   - A piece **jumping onto specific cells** triggers a large shift to bring the next region into view: level 4 (7,3)→(−30,0), (12,7)→**(15,−30)** (the only diagonal shift); level 5 (7,3)→(−18,0), (12,3)→(−33,0); level 6 (7,6)→(−20,0), (18,2)→(−44,0); level 7 (8,8)→(−44,0), **all of col 16**, rather than a single cell, →(−40,0); level 9 (6,5)→(−20,0). Shifts range from 15–44px. Levels 6 and 7 require the camera to be exactly at specified positions. Levels 4, 5, and 9 have no such guard, so jumping into the same cell again can trigger another shift; these cannot all be called one-time events. Except for level 6's (18,2), **none checks the color of the jumping piece**.

   Level 10 does not scroll (camera fixed at (5,3)). In col 8, rows 11–13 are below the view and only 1px of row 10 is visible. Those initial cells are `7` = **blue piece + cart + rail**. Thus 3 of the 8 carts and 3 of the 10 blue pieces are entirely invisible initially; **objects can move into view**, rather than staying hidden forever. Four consecutive up actions at the start move five adjacent carts from rows 9–13 to rows 5–9, bringing them all onscreen.

5. **Dead-state detection + reset button**: Jumping to certain hardcoded “no longer solvable” cells turns **all pieces gray (2)** and slides a **purple reset button** into the lower-left corner. Clicking the lower-left area (x < 16 and y > 48) then plays a noise transition and **fully resets the level**: board restored, undo stack cleared, internal action counter zeroed.
   Detection is **hardcoded**, only in levels 1, 2, 3, and 6. Other levels give no warning when you enter a dead end; use Undo yourself.

6. **Built-in level-1 hint**: In level 1 only, while all 5 initial pieces remain, clicking anywhere other than a piece or destination ring, including background or empty board cells, flashes a **blue circle** around the piece to click. If that piece is already selected, it points to the destination instead. It stops after the first capture, but becomes available again after Undo back to 5 pieces or a reset.

**Action count and progress bar** (two different counters)
- The internal counter determines loss: `ACTION1-4` each add 1 even when no cart can move; every `ACTION6` adds 1, including selection, jumps, and blank clicks. `ACTION7` restores the snapshot counter; the reset button zeroes it. **Reaching the cap, rather than exceeding it, loses unless you win**. A winning jump changes the level first, so action N can be the winning jump.
- The progress bar on row 0 uses another counter: **every submitted direction, click, and Undo adds 1, including empty Undo, and the reset button does not clear it**. Every 64 operations progressively fills the row with the next color (0 → 1 → 2 → 3 → 4). **From operation 256 onward the entire row saturates at color 4**; operations 320/321 look the same, with no color-5 black stage. It therefore gives only a rough indication of “total operations”: without Undo, the level-1 budget occupies 1/4 of its distinguishable range; the HUD saturates with 64 steps left in levels 2–5 and 384 left in levels 6–10. Changing levels rebuilds both counters.
