## LS20 — Transform through a maze (official label: Agent reasoning)

| Version | Current version `ls20-9607627b`. No version change on 2026-04-14; its source is byte-for-byte identical to the old snapshot. Pixel solver run on the official `arcengine 0.9.3` on 2026-10-20: **7/7 PASS, 353 actions, passed at 3 resume cut points**. |
| Step limit | `StepCounter` is 42 in every level; each charged action deducts 1 in levels 1/4/6 and 2 in the others. **Reaching 0 still allows play; only going negative costs a life**: Without replenishment, this occurs on the 43rd/22nd charged action. **3 lives** per level |

**Controls**: ACTION1/2/3/4 move up/down/left/right on screen. The shape indices and angle values in the level table are source labels, not printed HUD numbers; compare the bottom-left stamp with the target stamp, and identify a station by the attribute it changes.

**Core rules**: The player character has three attributes—**shape** (6 types), **color** (4 types: colors 12/9/14/8), and **orientation** (4 directions). The map has one or two **target tiles**, each bearing a stamp **on top** (`kvynsvxbpi`, placed at the target's +1,+1, so it lies on the tile rather than “standing beside it”), showing its required “shape + color + orientation” combination. The player must first use **modification stations** along the route to acquire the right shape, color, and orientation, then step onto a target. Satisfy all targets to clear the level—**if the attributes do not match, you cannot even enter the tile** (movement is blocked and the screen flashes), rather than “standing there without it counting”.

**Bumping a target tile does not cost steps** (the original statement that it “still deducts steps” was wrong). When blocked, `step()` sets `akoadfsur = 5` for the blinking animation, then returns directly at `if self.akoadfsur: return`, **skipping `mfyzdfvxsm()`**—the only call that deducts steps. Thus repeated target bumps waste actions but not steps; bumping a **wall** does deduct steps. The level-1 hint animation when the attributes match (`vqfjzzkhid()` returns True) uses the same early return, so that step is free as well.

**The character on the map does not reveal its attributes**: The player sprite (`sfqyzhzkij`) remains a two-color 5×5 block throughout; attributes appear only in the **status panel at the bottom left** (`eqatonpohu` backing + `wgmbtyhvbc` stamp, enlarged 2 times). The player therefore always compares “the bottom-left stamp” with “the stamp on the target tile”. Level 1 also provides a tutorial hint: Once attributes match, the target border and panel border light up (`vqfjzzkhid` returns False immediately for `level_index > 0`, so this exists only in level 1). **However, the hint lasts only 5 frames and is turned off before the action ends**. The official API returns all frames of that action; clients retaining only the last image miss it and must keep intermediate frames to use it as a signal.

**Board elements**: Maze walls (76–116 per level, including duplicate placements; 75–107 after deduplication), three kinds of modification stations (shape `ttfwljgohq` / color `soyhouuebz` / orientation `rhsxkxzdjz`, each incrementing its attribute by +1 cyclically), **one-way launch gates** (up/down/left/right), **step-replenishment items**, and **fog** in the final level.

**The one-way gates are actually launchers**: A gate is a 5×5 sprite with a light-gray line (color 1) on just one edge; the line sits on the boundary between two cells. **You are launched as soon as you enter the gate's own cell** (`prpxgfxlcm` checks collision after movement, so the player can never remain on a gate cell). `ullzqnksoj()` scans in the arrow direction, finds **the next obstacle**, then moves the gate together with the player to the cell just before it and snaps the gate back—the player is **pushed through several cells**, rather than only through one. In a measured level-3 example, entering the rightward gate at (9,5) immediately throws the player to (34,5), crossing 5 cells.

Three launch details (all verified by measurement):

* The scan's obstacle set `fjzuynaokm` is **walls + target tiles**, not just walls. A gate therefore never delivers you onto a target tile; it stops one cell before it. “Crashing” onto a target via a gate is impossible.
* The launching step still incurs one deduction (deduction happens before the launch). **Modification stations and step-replenishment items at the landing point work normally**, but **winning is not checked at landing**—the animation-completion branch calls only `txnfzvzetn()`, not `pbznecvnfr()`.
* The light-gray line (color 1) is at the arrow's **tail**: For a downward gate, the line is on the last row of the cell above; for a rightward gate, it is on the last column of the cell to the left. The gate cell is always on the side the arrow points toward. Gate blocking is set to BOUNDING_BOX, so theoretically the cell across the line could trigger a launch too, but in all seven levels that cell **is always a wall** and cannot be reached in practice.

**Step-replenishment item** (`npxgalaybz`, a small color-11 3×3 ring): Collecting it **fully refills** steps, and that step **costs no steps at all** (`bkuguqrpvq = not yubyobdoss and ...` short-circuits the deduction call); the item then disappears. Every level from level 2 onward has them, with as many as 6 in level 7.

**Patrolling modification stations**: Actually enabled on official `arcengine 0.9.3`, with **1/3/1 stations** bound in levels 5/6/7 respectively. Tracks carry the `xfmluydglp` tag and are drawn in color 14 but invisible; stations move back and forth on straight tracks (11×1, 21×1, 1×29), and around an 11×11 square loop. After a successful move, the stations advance one cell; when the player is blocked, the stations return to their pre-action positions.

The official probe confirmed: L5 orientation station `(14,35)→(19,35)`; L6's three stations `(14,10)→(19,10)`, `(34,40)→(29,40)`, `(24,30)→(19,30)`; L7 orientation station `(54,10)→(54,15)` (all after pressing up at the start). The original draft called them dead code because the reconstructed engine treated `-2` pixels as transparent, leaving the binding set empty; the official `Sprite.collides_with()` mask excludes only `-1`, and `ignoreMode=True` does not switch to a bbox test. **Current official play requires observing station positions step by step.**

**Losing a life resets the entire level**: Back to the start, attributes reset, collected supplies restored, and completed targets restored, without replenishing lives; only exhausting all three lives calls `lose()`. Conversely, **deliberately burning through the step count is an available tactic**: One life buys “all supplies and targets restored, with map knowledge retained”, which is much more useful in a dead end than running into the action cap.

**Order of checks**: First move stations, attempt movement, and trigger the cell; then deduct steps. If the count is not exhausted, a launch may activate; otherwise targets are checked before life loss is handled. **If the final deduction makes the count negative while also completing all targets, the level still clears**. In L6, completed targets disappear and remain recorded; standing on both at once is unnecessary. Losing a life restores both, and patrolling stations also return to their starts. Wall bumps are charged but roll back station movement; there is no player undo key. Step pressure counts charged deductions, rather than charging every API action.

**Fog** (level 7 only): All screen locations more than **20 pixels (= 4 cells)** from the player are painted color 5. The bottom-left status panel is **redrawn** after the fog overlay; the bottom resource bar and life indicators also remain visible, so the player's attributes and resources are always visible.

**Two more facts found only through measurement**:

* **Level 2 has a wall sprite misaligned with the grid**: `ihdgageizm` is placed at `(54, 34)` (y is not a multiple of 5). Collision test `mrznumynfe` uses the cell containing the sprite's top-left corner, so this counts as cell `(54,30)`; but its **drawing** occupies y=34..38, painting four rows of cell `(54,35)` in wall color even though that cell is walkable. Images alone can misclassify it.
* **The status panel covers the maze's bottom-left corner**: `eqatonpohu`/`xvrpzkggig` occupies frame rows 52–63 × cols 0–11, completely hiding cell `(4,55)` and covering one corner each of `(4,50)/(9,50)/(9,55)`. All four cells are walls in all seven levels, so this has no gameplay effect, but parsers must exclude that pixel region.

### Corrections during replication

While writing `arc_agi3/replica/games/ls20.py`, the official source was checked point by point. This article originally had three errors and two omissions, corrected above. The evidence is retained here:

1. **“The view is only 16×16, the map is 64×64, and the screen scrolls with the character” is wrong—the full 64×64 map is in fact always visible.**
   The source does specify `Camera(width=16, height=16)`, but this value does not take effect: The official engine's `set_level()` calls `camera.resize(64,64)` based on the level's `grid_size`, overriding the view; moreover, ls20 **never changes `camera.x/y`**. If it actually ran at 16×16, the view would stay stuck in the top-left patch, rather than becoming a scrolling window.
   Stronger evidence comes from the official file itself: `render_interface()` draws the step bar at `frame[61:63, 13:55]` and lives at `frame[61:63, 56:64]`; fog compares each point of `range(64)×range(64)` with the player's **world-coordinate** position `(gudziatsk.y+1.5, gudziatsk.x+1.5)`; the bottom-left status panel `eqatonpohu`/`ghizzeqtoh`/`wgmbtyhvbc` is directly placed as level sprites at world coordinates (1,53)/(3,55). These coordinates make sense only with “screen = full 64×64 world, 1:1”. The current official engine draws the complete 64×64 map at the dimensions set by `set_level()` (a 12×12-cell maze of 5×5 tiles + 4 bottom HUD rows).
   The difficulty is therefore maze length, the number of cycles needed through attributes, step pressure, and level-7 fog, rather than inability to see the whole map. The official `Agent reasoning` label corresponds to “planning the order of attribute changes”, rather than exploration memory.

2. **There are no “pushable boxes”.** The entire file contains no pushable objects; the only thing that “pushes” is the one-way gate, and it pushes **the player** (`self.aqxtoxeino = aqxtoxeino` binds the player sprite).

3. **Level 5's “one-way gates ×6” was wrong; there are actually 8** (`kapcaakvb_b`×2, `lujfinsby_t`×2, `tihiodtoj_l`×2, `yjgargdic_r`×2). The previously omitted 2 gates in level 6 and 3 in level 7 have also been added.

4. **“Step-replenishment items” were omitted** (see above); these are key to making the step allowance last from level 2 onward.

5. **The old reconstructed-engine conclusion about patrolling stations has been overturned**: Official 0.9.3 binds L5/L6/L7＝1/3/1; the original draft's “none move” was solely a reconstructed-engine difference. Current gameplay follows the description above.

6. Two further small corrections: **The signs are drawn on the target tiles** (offset +1,+1), rather than standing beside them; **the wall count is 76–116** (the original text said 68–116).

7. **Two additional pixel-solver corrections** (see above and “Measured”): Bumping a target with mismatched attributes **does not deduct steps** (the original said it “still deducts”); a one-way gate's scan treats **target tiles as obstacles too** (the original said only “the next wall”), and **does not check for a win at the landing point**.
