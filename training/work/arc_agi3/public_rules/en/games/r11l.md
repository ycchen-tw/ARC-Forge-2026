**Core mechanism**: Each spider consists of a 5×5 octagonal body and 2–4 diamond-shaped legs.
The body's top-left corner is always the floored arithmetic mean of the legs' top-left coordinates: `body.x = floor(sum(leg.x)/n)`, and likewise for y.
The player can move only the legs; the body follows their centroid. In levels 1–4, each body has its own assigned target; levels 5–6 switch to color absorption and matching.

**Click rules**: Clicking inside any leg's **5×5 bounding rectangle** selects it; clicking elsewhere moves the selected leg,
with its new top-left corner at `(click_x-2, click_y-2)`. Selecting a leg also consumes an action. The leg closest to the origin is automatically selected at the start.
A destination is rejected if the leg's solid pixels collide with a wall (color 2), still consuming an action; **walls block legs, not bodies**.
A move reaches its destination immediately, with no check along the path. Avoid clicking the destination center inside another leg's bounding rectangle, or the click will merely switch the selected leg.
The selection marker consists of the 12 outer pixels of the leg's diamond: color 0 when selected and color 3 when unselected. Its center pixel retains its own color when selection changes. There is no undo key; hazard rewind is an automatic rollback.

**Hazards**: Large color-10 regions. If any body touches a hazard after moving, the action is consumed and one mistake is added.
The first four mistakes in a level rewind the move; the fifth loses immediately, leaving the attempted leg and body positions in the final frame instead of rewinding. Legs may stand on hazards. Small color-10 patches may instead be decoration or fragments, not hazards.
Mistakes accumulate across actions and reset on entering a new level. Hazard checking precedes targets; targets are not permanently unlocked one by one—**all real targets must be satisfied simultaneously** to clear the level. Color absorption, target-arrival blinking, and hazard rewind all finish in internal frames of the same action without consuming extra clicks; click 60 does not even select a leg, let alone allow an early win.

**Distinguish the target's visible shape from its invisible collision area**: Its four diagonal arms in a 7×7 box are each 3 pixels long, totaling 12 visible pixels.
The four arms can have different colors:

```text
..A.B..
.A...B.
A.....B
.......
C.....D
.C...D.
..C.D..
```

The source for real targets also contains **25 invisible interior `-2` cells**. The official renderer draws only nonnegative pixels, while collision includes every non-`-1`
pixel; a real target therefore has 37 collision cells. There are 97 intersecting relative placements of body and target,
and **a symmetric placement directly in the center also counts as touching**. The previous description that “the exact center is a trap” was wrong.
The solver conservatively uses only the 96 placements that touch the 12 visible arms, which suffices to clear all levels; it does not treat the invisible interior as image information.

**Color absorption (levels 5–6)**: Bodies start entirely color 0. On contact with a 5×5 octagonal fragment, the fragment disappears,
and its non-0 pixels are copied onto the body at the **same array indices**, rather than aligned according to the contact location; colors collected later may overwrite earlier ones.
Any blank body can serve any real target, provided it touches that target and the body's and target's **sets of positive colors are exactly equal**
(ignoring color 0, with the position condition still required). Checking only whether the body “contains the target colors” is insufficient.

**Information limits for distinguishing real and fake targets**: The game skips targets whose internal names contain `dirwzt` in the win check;
those names are not displayed. Fake Xs have the same shape as the visible arms of real targets, with no directly readable “fake” marker;
the `-2` interior of real targets is also invisible. “Monochrome means fake target” cannot be used as a rule: real targets in levels 1–3 are themselves monochrome.
Decoration can also make a fake X appear multicolored; for example, a decoration over the base-color-9 fake X in level 5 makes its visible set `{9,12}`.

**Solver method (not a game rule)**: The solver infers feasible matches from current bodies, fragment stamps, and visible targets; when reachable target color sets strictly contain one another,
it prioritizes the full set, e.g. `{14}` in level 5 is a subset of `{11,14}`. Otherwise it would demand that two spiders simultaneously serve three reachable Xs,
which is exactly where the original solver failed. This is an **observational inference** verifiable on the current levels, not the engine's formula for distinguishing real and fake targets,
and it does not guarantee that real and fake targets with identical observations can be distinguished in arbitrary new levels. Fake Xs are still retained as objects to avoid, preventing loss of parsing after occlusion.
An isolated one-cell marker at a body's center can be excluded; if, after absorption, that center color also occupies other cells, it is a body color
and cannot be ignored. Otherwise, once a completed body covers a real target, the solver reassigns that body to a monochrome fake X and cycles back and forth.

**Image parsing**: Color-1 connecting lines overwrite only background color 5 and color 10; leg groupings are inferred from the Bresenham lines from legs to centroids,
and color 10 covered by those lines must also be restored. Bodies are on layer 1; walls are on layer 2 in levels 1–3 and layer 0 in levels 4–6.
Legs, targets, fragments, and decorations are drawn in order within their shared layer, and bodies may cover them. Column 0 is UI and cannot be used to identify sprites.

**Version history**: In the old `aa269680` version, level 4 had only 1 fake X/2 decorations, and levels 5–6 had none.
The current version adds fake Xs and decorations and reassigns colors; the level table and verification results here have all been updated to the current version.
