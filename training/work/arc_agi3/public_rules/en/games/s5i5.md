> **Versions and draw order**: Both versions use a custom Camera, drawing sprites one by one in list order rather than sorting by layer; the current version reorders the list and changes obfuscated names, with identical level geometry and control rules. On official 0.9.3, the two versions' initial frames differ by 1–6 pixels per level: In levels 1–7, the current version draws color-13 hands/target markers above links; in level 8, a fixed end is occluded.
>
> **The occluded part in level 8 is the rotational pivot**: `0050niyswwmhla` is a color-11 root link of just 1 segment at (48,32), rotating with four child links attached. The untagged color-11 decorative block `0085lrybbtqnvv` at the same position occurs later in the list, completely covering the color-3 row (48–50,34) of its fixed end. It has no slider, and the child links have no individual rotation buttons; the corrected solver reads the rotation phase from the directions of visible spokes, without guessing the occluded true orientation. This handling covers only the geometric conditions described above.
>
> **Engine results must not be mixed**: The reconstructed engine sorts by layer before handing sprites to the custom Camera, so old-version levels 1–7 happen to look like the current official version. The old “8/8” result was from the reconstructed engine; the same solver fails on level 1 of the old official version and cannot be cited as passing both official versions.

**Core rules**: The board contains a mechanical arm made of **colored links**; there are “hands” (small color-13 squares) at arm ends, and **targets** of the same color 13 on the map.
The win check visits each **target**: **Every target must have a hand on it** (`sprite.x`/`sprite.y` must be exactly equal; overlap alone does not count).
Hands have no assigned targets; any hand can serve any target.
All targets must be satisfied **simultaneously**; a hand reaching a target is neither locked nor permanently registered and may temporarily leave before being returned. There is no undo key; collision rollback happens automatically.

**The player cannot drag the arm directly**, only use two kinds of controls at the screen edges (the color of a control's icon determines which color of links it controls—**all** links of that color):

- **Slider** (a long bar, each corresponding to one color): Clicking its **second half** (right half when horizontal, bottom half when vertical) **extends** every link of that color by one segment; clicking its **first half** **shortens** them by one segment (minimum 1 segment; further shortening has no effect). The exact middle cell is a divider; clicking it does nothing (**but still costs one step**, measured).
- **Rotation button** (square): A click turns that color's links 90° **counterclockwise about their own fixed ends** (`np.rot90`), taking each entire subtree around **the same** pivot. If that turn would make a child link point **exactly opposite** to its parent (folding back onto it), it automatically **turns once more to skip** that direction.

**Each click first costs 1 step**, including background clicks, divider clicks, shortening at minimum length, and collision rollback; internal animation/rollback frames cost no more. All targets are checked before checking whether the count reaches zero, so the last unit of allowance can still clear the level. A new level resets geometry, control bindings, rollback snapshots, and steps; previous lengths/phases are not carried over.

> **Measured supplement (direction cycle while rotating)**: Because of the skip rule above, **a link with a parent has only 3 possible resting directions**;
> its angle relative to the parent cycles `+90° → 0° → -90° → +90°` and **can never reach 180°**. Root links do not have this restriction
> and follow the full 4-direction cycle `0 → 270 → 180 → 90`. (Confirmed in level 6 by repeatedly clicking the same button in the engine and reading the sprite after each click.)

> **Measured supplement (same-color length invariant)**: All links controlled by a slider **always have equal lengths**—they start equal,
> extend/shorten as a group, stop at 1 segment as a group, and roll back on collision as a group. This holds in all eight levels.
> (Useful with image-only observations: If one link extends offscreen and cannot be measured, a measurable link in the same group can stand in for it.)

Each link's fixed end is drawn in color 3 (a 3-cell-wide strip); the link grows only outward from that end. **A hand is an independent sprite attached to a link's endpoint** (the link it overlaps initially is its parent), so it always follows that link's endpoint. In all eight levels, hands initially occupy exactly the end cell.

**Links form trees** (parent-child relations are defined by `Children` in the level data): When a parent link extends or rotates, attached child links **move together along the chain/rotate around the parent's fixed end**. This is the real difficulty—one action propagates through an entire subtree.

> **Parameter representation for planning**: Operations without collisions or minimum-length clipping can be described by phase and length. Rotation means “rotate the entire subtree around the fixed end of the clicked link”; when a parent rotates,
> its child links' pivots move with it. Given fixed legal relative directions, local rotation phases describe the parent-child pose. Extension/shortening translates the whole subtree along the link's own direction,
> and can share a set of local parameters with rotation. Thus **the final pose is simply a function of a parameter set**: One length per slider and one phase per rotation button.
> Collisions and minimum lengths restrict available transitions. The solver first computes final parameters, then orders the actions
> (direct A* over poses, using “maze distance from hands to targets” as its heuristic, still failed to solve level 7 after 300,000 expansions).

**Collision rollback**: If an operation causes any two links to overlap (walls also count as links), the entire action is **undone**.

> **Correction (the original text was wrong)**: The old text said “undo is delayed until the next action; the player's next click is used only to bounce back,
> without decrementing `StepCounter`.” **Measurements show otherwise**: On collision, `step()` does `return` directly **without** calling
> `complete_action()`, so the engine calls step again **within the same action** to restore the pose. The net effect is
> “nothing happened this step and the image is unchanged, but `StepCounter` still drops by one”; the colliding pose appears only as an
> **intermediate frame** (one action returns 2 frames). The player's next click is an entirely normal action.
> (Current official-engine probe: Consecutive extensions in L1 hit the wall on the 11th; `len(frames) == 2`, final geometry unchanged, steps 40→39.)

Two other kinds of objects also carry the `0001qwdmnlybkb` (link) tag but have no controls:

- **Color-15 walls**: Enclose the movement area or serve as obstacles; an arm hitting them triggers collision rollback.
- **Color-1 links**: No slider or rotation button (they appear in `Children`); they can only be carried by a parent, with fixed length.

> **Measured supplement (walls extend offscreen, with asymmetric sides)**: Links can extend outside 64×64, and so can walls.
> Level 7's `0006vwqootnonz` is a 70×51 frame at `(-3,-3)`: **Its top edge has a wall only where x ≥ 39**
> (the top is open for x < 39, allowing links to extend out); **its left edge has a wall only where y ≥ 27**;
> its right edge lies at x=64..66 (one cell offscreen), and its bottom at y=45..47. Level 8's `0027viwvygcybo` (30×64 at `(-3,0)`) has only its left edge offscreen; the remainder consists of two small on-screen patches.
> In other words, **the screen boundary does not tell you whether a wall exists outside it**; with images alone, treat it as empty initially and record a wall after a collision.
> Level 7 actually requires using offscreen space: The color-8 arm must turn “upward, beyond the top of the screen” to clear the way.

Note that “where the hands are” and “what the chains look like” are separate questions: In levels 3 and 4, the hands are attached to **independent single links**, with parent-child chains acting as obstacles; **this does not generalize to levels 5 and 7**. Normal-play tests show that the color-12 slider in level 5 moves the hand on a color-14 link, and the color-11 rotation button in level 7 moves the hand on a color-12 link. Planning must include the effects of ancestor links. In level 4, the child links of all four pairs and the hand-carrying link **all share color 11**, so one slider moves five links at once.

> **Measured supplement (one hand starts on a target in levels 5 and 7)**: At `(36,24)` in level 5 and `(21,6)` in level 7,
> a hand starts overlapping its target (shown as a **solid** cross rather than a hollow cross). The other hand is the one that needs solving,
> but this hand must be returned in the final pose. In level 7 it is attached to the color-10 link, and changing its length temporarily leaves the target; **it is not locked**. The route below explicitly shortens it by two segments before extending it again.

> **Historical route examples (levels 7 and 8)**: The following 55/38-step routes are from the pixel-only solver on the old version's reconstructed engine, to illustrate the geometry; they are not current official-engine per-level action counts or proofs of a unique solution:
>
> - **Level 7** (55 steps): Press the color-11 button 2 times to point the root link down, then extend the chain into “down 9 → left 14 → up 8 → right 7”
>   to go around the central partition at x=39..41—the horizontal wall at y=27..29 has two gates at x=6..8 and x=30..32.
>   This route keeps the final link 90° clockwise from the previous link, **uses the horizontal gap at y=39..41,
>   then goes up through the gate at x=6..8** to reach the target; other shapes have not been proved impossible.
>   The color-8 arm blocks the final section, so first shorten color 10 by two segments to make room, rotate color 8 2 times (pointing up, extending beyond the top of the screen),
>   then extend color 10 again.
> - **Level 8** (38 steps): Extending the hand-carrying `0043dhmhlmzfqb` downward to 14 segments reaches the target, but **the same-color `0051pxvxhqyquq`
>   is enclosed in the 21×21 room at (39,23)** and hits a wall after extending left by two segments. First press the color-11 pivot button once, turning this color-14 child link downward
>   to align it with the doorway at x=48..50 at the bottom of the room, allowing it to extend out. Two horizontal arms, colors 7 and 10, also block the downward path;
>   shorten each to 2 segments to clear it (3 segments does not work—the passive child link would be pushed into the path).
