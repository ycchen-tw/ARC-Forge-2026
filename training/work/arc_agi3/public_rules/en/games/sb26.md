**Core rules**: The upper half of the screen is a **program**: several colored **frames**, each with a row of instruction slots. The lower half (below the dividing line) is the **instruction library**. At the very top is an **output tape**, with a required color marked in each cell.

Move instructions into slots and press execute. The program **expands through a call stack**, outputting colors one by one. Each output is compared with the next tape cell: **the correct color fills it and advances; a wrong color fails the entire tape and starts it over**. Fill the whole tape to clear the level.

**There are only two instruction types** (both are 6×6 blocks; color supplies the parameter)
- **Solid color block** (sprite `lngftsryyw`): output this color.
- **Hollow frame** (sprite `vgszefyyyp`): **call** the frame whose **border has the same color**—insert that entire frame's expansion, then return to this slot and continue onward.

**A frame is a subroutine, not a counted loop**
- Frames have no “repeat count.” The entry point is the **topmost frame (first after `sort by (y,x)`)**, executed from its leftmost slot toward the right.
- Once a frame's slots finish, execution **returns to the slot that called it**, then advances one slot right (the pop + `ppsxsxiod` inside `dbfxrigdqx()` implements return-and-advance).
- **Repetition** comes from several instructions calling the same frame; **nesting** comes from putting calls inside frames; a **loop** comes from a frame calling itself indirectly, and may keep outputting until the tape is full (level 8). The game explicitly blocks only the case “the call is in slot 0, the same `(frame,0)` is already on the stack, and the parent is also stopped in slot 0.” This does not guarantee other recursion will complete; a cycle without continuing output may hit the official engine's animation-frame limit for a single action.
- **The entry point is visible**: color-0 corner brackets (`lnzhvcagos`) always surround `qaagahahj[0]`, the topmost frame.

**How the output-tape pointer advances** (visible only by examining `dbfxrigdqx()` tick by tick; this explains why calls do not occupy tape cells)

Pointer `pmygakdvy` **increments only when advancing to the next slot in the same frame**, and filling a cell and advancing take **two separate ticks**:

1. Reach a color block → compare against `tape[p]` → if correct, fill it; `p` stays unchanged.
2. On the next tick at the same slot, notice that this cell is filled → check for victory first; if not won, advance to the next slot and do `p += 1`.
3. Returning from the end of a frame uses `pop`, **without changing `p`**; only after returning to the caller's slot does the caller perform the advance from step 2.

The advance omitted by the called frame is supplied by the caller. The net result is **one pointer advance per output cell**; call instructions consume no output-tape cells themselves.

**Victory is checked** when “the pointer is on the last cell and that cell is filled,” by `dbfxrigdqx()` each time it executes a slot. Thus **the program does not need to terminate**: recursion wins as soon as it fills the tape.

**Failure conditions** (`sibihgzarf()`): an output color disagrees with the tape, execution reaches an **empty slot**, or the program finishes before the tape is full. Failure clears the entire tape, but **your placed instructions remain**, so you can edit them and run again.

**Actions**
- `ACTION6(x,y)`: click an instruction to pick it up, then an empty slot to place it (**costs 1 step**); click its own slot to put it back (free). While holding an instruction, click another occupied position to swap them (costs 1 step, except when both are still in the library: that only changes which one is held and is free). Instructions may also be moved back into empty library positions.
- `ACTION5`: **execute the program** (costs 1 step).
- `ACTION7`: **Undo** (return to the previous placement state, **without charging a step or refunding steps already spent**).

**Timing and outcome order**: The fade animation for a placement/swap, or an entire program run, belongs to the same action; no new input can be inserted midway. If placement/swap reduces energy to **0**, you lose immediately after its animation. If `ACTION5` reduces energy to 0, it still runs the program: **filling the tape succeeds; a failed run loses only when its failure finishes**. Failure does not always mean GAME_OVER: with energy left, the program remains, the tape is cleared, and editing continues. Blank clicks and clicking an empty slot while holding nothing are free and ineffective.

**Level changes and display limits**: `on_set_level` rebuilds everything from a clean level copy, restores energy to 64, and clears the held item, call stack, output pointer, animation, and Undo history. Slot positions, colors, entry point, and energy can be read from the display. Past configurations in Undo cannot be recovered from a single frame; the full call stack during execution also requires tracking the instructions and animation history.

**Clickable regions exceed the visible patterns**: The visible part of an instruction is the central 4×4 of its 6×6 cell; an empty slot shows only its central 2×2. The outer area is **`-2`, which the official engine still accepts as a hit**. Thus the entire 6×6 bbox is clickable, including its upper-left corner. The central points `(cell x+2,cell y+2)`–`(cell x+3,cell y+3)` also work, but are not the only hit region. Hidden library-position bases also participate in hit testing; selection priority depends on sprite layer/tag.

**Instructions initially inside frames are locked**: `on_set_level` removes the `sys_click` tag from all instructions in the upper half, so they cannot be clicked and must be treated as given components.

### Runtime notes (differences in the reconstructed engine, not game rules)

When placing an instruction, `hjewbkcejq()` does:

```python
pcxhwtsymi = self.current_level.get_sprite_at(held.x, held.y, "susublrply")
assert pcxhwtsymi
pcxhwtsymi.set_visible(True)
```

It looks up **the `susublrply` underneath the held instruction's original position**. This sprite has both properties:
it is `set_visible(False)` (hence the subsequent `set_visible(True)`), and the query point
`(held.x, held.y)` is the upper-left corner of the 6×6 cell, where its pixel is **-2**.
Official 0.9.3 `Level.get_sprite_at()` does not exclude invisible sprites, and PIXEL_PERFECT hit testing skips only `-1`, so this query succeeds. `-2` may be undrawn yet still participate in hits and collisions. The reconstructed engine's erroneous “skip invisible/-2” filters were fixed on 2026-09-12; do not treat that old bug as a current rule. The query's `ignore_collidable` controls only whether collidability is required.

### Earlier mistakes in this document, corrected against source

1. ~~“The number on a loop frame is its repeat count”~~ → **There are no numbers on frames**. Source function `rfdjlhefnd()` scans slots using `int(frame.name[-1])` (`x = frame.x + 2 + i*6`); the number is the sprite name's **slot count**, present only in source and never drawn onscreen. Frames are **subroutines**; repetition requires multiple calls or mutual calls.
2. ~~“The output sequence expands as `(inner × n) × m`”~~ → Expansion follows a **call/return stack**: `buvfjfmpp` is a stack of `(frame, slot)`; calls `push` and frame completion `pop`s. There is no multiplication counter.
3. ~~“Budget 64, but only execute costs a step; placing instructions is free”~~ → `hjewbkcejq()` performs `self.sjcuorclg -= 1` after **every successful placement/swap**, just like `ACTION5`. The free operations are picking up, canceling, changing the held library item, and `ACTION7` (`kxrrueustb()` never touches `sjcuorclg`).
4. Previously omitted: call instructions **name their target frame by color** (`next(f for f in frames if f.pixels[0,0] == item.pixels[1,1])`); **preplaced upper-half instructions lose `sys_click` and cannot be clicked**; and **reaching an empty slot also fails**.

### Further corrections after testing (2026-08-10, verified level by level with `pixel/solvers/sb26.py`)

5. ~~“All four frames in level 6 have their own color locked in the 1st slot”~~ → **Only the last three frames** do; the entry frame has three empty slots.
   The earlier text was internally inconsistent: four locked blocks would leave only 8 empty slots, contradicting “9 empty slots” in the same row.
6. Added the output-pointer advance rules (fill and advance on separate ticks; no advance on return), which explain why calls do not occupy tape cells.
   The earlier text gave only the result, inviting the mistaken inference that a call also consumes a cell.
7. Added that the budget **resets each level**, and that remaining energy is read directly by counting color-2 pixels on row 53.
8. The old reconstructed engine described hit regions as “central 4×4 for instructions, central 2×2 for empty slots.” The current official engine also hits the `-2` outer area; targeted probes support the corrected full 6×6 region. Color-0 corner brackets mark the entry point.
