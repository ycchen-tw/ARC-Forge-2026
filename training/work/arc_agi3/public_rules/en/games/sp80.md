> The old version/reconstructed engine was checked against the game's `step()` on 1800 random layouts (overflow cells, filled cups, and water traces matched cell for cell). The current official engine was separately confirmed to clear 6/6 levels and pass resume; that batch of random layouts was not rerun.

**Core rules**: Two phases: arrange first, simulate afterward. Place barriers, then press `ACTION5` to release water. Water flows downward from its sources, splits sideways when it hits barriers, and fills a cup when it enters the mouth (color 11 → 13). You clear the level only when **all cups are full and not a single drop has reached the floor**.

**Two phases (state machine)**
1. `change` (arrangement): `ACTION6(x,y)` selects a barrier (it turns color 9); `ACTION1-4` move it up/down/left/right respectively in display coordinates.
   - Every action deducts 1 step first, **including clicking blank space, hitting a wall, and releasing water**; reaching **0 immediately loses**. Thus a level with a budget of 30 permits at most 29 actions completed without losing; you cannot reserve the 30th action for releasing water.
   - Hit testing uses the sprite's **bounding box**, not its solid pixels, so clicking the transparent missing corner of a turning block still selects it.
   - Barriers cannot be placed at y<3 (near the sources) or next to cups (each cup's bbox expanded by 1 cell in every direction is forbidden).
   - If a move collides only with **other barriers/turning blocks**, the official implementation permits it (if the collision list returned by `try_move_sprite` contains only these types, it still calls `move`): **barriers may overlap one another**. Walls, the floor, and cups block movement.
2. `spill` (simulation): Submit with `ACTION5`. The entire water propagation and resolution **counts as one action** (`step()` is called repeatedly until the process ends, then calls `complete_action()`); the player cannot intervene midway.

The barrier nearest the internal origin is selected automatically at the start, so you need not click it first. `ACTION7` is unavailable; there is no Undo. When the budget reaches 0, the caller may still change the selection or position, but the official state is already `GAME_OVER`; a changed frame does not mean play can continue. After releasing water, success—“all cups full and no overflow”—is checked only once the water has completely stopped, rather than the instant the cups become full.

**Water propagation rules** (the physics to infer)

A drop advances with direction `(dx,dy)` and checks **the cell immediately ahead** each tick. “Both sides” means left/right for vertical flow and up/down for horizontal flow:

- Empty cell → create a drop ahead, retaining its direction.
- **Existing drop** → advance directly into that cell, retaining direction (flow follows existing water traces; drops remain on the board, making the entire route visible).
- Hit a **barrier** (color 8) → create a drop on each empty side, **retaining the original direction**. Thus falling water hitting a 1xN horizontal barrier crawls along the row above it toward both ends, then continues downward at columns (left end−1) and (right end+1). Horizontal flow hitting an upright barrier similarly goes around its top and bottom edges and continues horizontally. If both sides are occupied, that flow path disappears (this does not count as failure).
- Hit a **cup** (color 11) → fill it only if the drop is **exactly at its mouth cell** (both side queries return the same cup object); otherwise treat it as a barrier and go around its sides. Cups can rotate: `dojfslwbg=0` opens upward (requiring falling water), `90` opens right (requiring horizontal flow **from right to left**), and `270` opens left (requiring left-to-right flow).
- Hit a **turning block** (a color-15 2×2 shape missing one corner) → the missing corner is the inlet; water entering there turns 90°. `[[15,-1],[15,15]]` turns falling water right; `[[-1,15],[15,15]]` turns it left.
- Hit the **floor/lethal wall** (color 1, drawn inside the board) → overflow; this attempt fails.
  **The outer frame is also color 1, but lies outside the board, is invisible, and is not lethal**—water touching it is silently absorbed. Thus “color 1 = death” applies only to cells inside the board.

**Actual turning-block branches** (checked against current source; execution comparisons for unusual layouts come from the old reconstructed engine): The code uses `if / if / else`, so the `else` may still execute after the first condition succeeds. Moreover, the outgoing drop **does not check whether its destination is empty** and is placed directly over the block. Usually that outgoing drop occupies the empty cell the `else` would use. When the turning destination is itself part of the block (horizontal incoming flow, or flow coming upward from below), the `else` may create extra side drops retaining the original direction. Predictions for levels 5 and 6 must preserve this branch order.

**Failure and retries**: After a simulation, overflow or an unfilled cup causes 6 flashing frames, then **drops are cleared, cups emptied, and floor colors restored**; the failure count increases by 1. **Barrier positions remain unchanged** (`lpqbikobah()` changes only water and colors, not sprite positions). **After 4 failed attempts, the 5th press to release water loses immediately** (`ACTION5` checks failure count `>=4` at entry). You can still arrange barriers after the fourth failure finishes, but there is no fifth actual water release. Exhausting the step budget also loses.

**Resets and invisible memory**: A failure does not refund steps; it preserves barrier positions and automatically reselects the block nearest the internal origin. Changing levels or sending official RESET replenishes the budget, resets the failure count, clears water and selection, then automatically selects the first block. The failure count has no separate onscreen counter; the 64-pixel budget bar also cannot distinguish every remaining value in levels with 100/120 steps. When barriers overlap, object boundaries and list order may not be uniquely recoverable from a single frame. A click on an overlap selects the first block in the `fbrwmvzsym()` list, which is not necessarily the topmost visible block.

**The trickiest mechanism**: `level.data['dojfslwbg']` rotates the entire 64×64 frame (180° in levels 2/3/5) and symmetrically remaps the directional inputs. These cancel out, so **controls follow the displayed image directly**: clicking selects the block at that point, and pressing up moves it up. Only **gravity** is inverted—in rotated levels water visibly flows upward, with sources at the bottom. The most reliable rotation clue is the budget bar: drawn on internal row 0, it moves to the last displayed row after a 180° rotation (that row contains only colors 14 and 0).

**Display geometry**: 16×16 levels use scale 4 without letterboxing; 20×20 levels use scale 3 with a 2-pixel letterbox on every side (color 1). Before rotation, the budget bar covers frame row 0; after rotation in L2/3/5 it lies on row 63, which grid reading must avoid.

**Verified level-6 strategy**: One fixed source plus one outlet on a perforated barrier must feed 4 cups, 3 requiring horizontal flow. Split the flow with the perforated horizontal barrier, then send leftward water into the **upright 4×1** barrier. Flow goes around its top and bottom edges, producing two streams 5 rows apart aligned with the two cup mouths on the left wall. This is the method used by the verified route; no exhaustive search established that it is the only solution.

The “hole” marks an outlet, not a passage for incoming water: normal play in level 6 showed water arriving directly above the color-4 hole cell splitting left and right, while the outlet independently created the drop below it.

**Current pixel-solver recognition limit**: If same-color barriers are four-neighbor adjacent, connected components alone read them as one object, so the current solver forbids placing them too close. This is a planner restriction; the game permits adjacency and overlap. It also means moving long barriers on the 20-cell-wide board requires moving obstructing blocks away as intermediate steps.
