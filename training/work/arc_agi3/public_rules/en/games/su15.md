> **Clicks outside the clickable region are complete no-ops** (tested). `axaxyjxqoe()` immediately `return`s without setting `vsfwpngmx`, so `step()` directly calls `complete_action()` and **runs no animation frames at all**—enemies do not advance and cooldowns do not decrease. Such clicks cannot “skip a turn and let enemies move.” Their sole side effect is that `hlsimbcfgc()` has already pushed an Undo save.

**Core rules**: Square “fruit” objects lie scattered around. Each has a **tier**, which determines its side length and color. Clicking a point performs a **vacuum**: all fruit within a circle of **radius 8** centered on the click (Euclidean distance to the nearest point on the sprite border) are drawn toward it.

- Only **one** fruit in range → its center moves toward the click. Each tick changes x and y **independently** by at most 4 pixels; this is not a Euclidean displacement limit of 4. Normally 4 ticks suffice to reach the click; near edges, the object is clamped inside the screen according to its size. Enemy knockback is another way fruit can move.
- **Two or more same-tier** fruit in range → after being pulled to overlap at the click, they **merge into one fruit one tier higher**, **landing at the click point**. Merging resolves only after 4 animation frames (`mdetahtgad`), by which time every center has moved to the click. The code's “average of all member centers” is therefore the click itself, except when boundary clamping offsets them. Merging N same-tier fruit still produces only one, so merging 3 at once wastes mass.
- **Different-tier** fruit in range → resolving their overlap causes an error: two fruit flash for 16 ticks, with a penalty of **2 / 4 / 6 / 8 …** steps (2 for the first error each level, increasing by 2 per subsequent error; reset only on level change). **Automatic Undo to before the click occurs only if the remaining budget is still greater than 0 after the penalty**. At 0 or below, GAME_OVER is immediate and the board is not rolled back. The erroneous action is not additionally charged the normal 1 step.

  Precisely, the condition is “two **vacuumed** fruit have **overlapping bounding boxes** and different tiers at the end of the 4 animation frames,” rather than simply “different tiers exist within the radius” (`mdetahtgad` first checks overlap with `nbfpjkxrnp` and `continue`s if they do not overlap). In practice all vacuumed fruit land at the click and necessarily overlap, so these descriptions agree; **boundary clamping** can keep them from actually overlapping, in which case there is no error. Enemies use the same error branch (`qzquneuehr`), but enemy overlap checking covers **all enemies on the board**, not only those vacuumed.

**Victory condition**: Level data `xkstxyqbs` contains `(type, count)` pairs. Count objects by type whose centers lie within **any target ring's bounding box** (9×9, color 9), **summing across rings**. Every listed count must match exactly (one extra fails; unlisted types have no effect). This does not check circular pixels or require one object per ring; an object in overlapping rings is still counted once. Center = `(x + width//2, y + height//2)`, excluding bbox right/bottom boundaries. For example, L3 `[[3,1],[2,1]]` requires a total of 1 tier-3 and 1 tier-2 fruit inside the rings. Once satisfied, the HUD and rings turn white (color 0); the next level starts after 11 ticks.

> **Outcomes are checked every frame, rather than only at action completion** (tested; very important). The vacuum branch of `step()` calls `cbdhpcilgb()` on every animation frame, so satisfying the condition **even momentarily** wins. This makes two things much easier: (1) an enemy need not be “stopped” inside a target ring; the frame in which it **passes through** while chasing fruit clears the level; (2) merged fruit land at the click, so the final merge can be clicked directly inside a target ring for immediate resolution.

**Failure conditions**: The step budget reaches zero, or all fruit have been eaten (`zmlxwcvwb` count is 0).

**Actions**
- `ACTION6(x,y)`: Vacuum. Whether or not anything is pulled, a **normal, nonwinning, nonerroneous** vacuum inside the legal region costs 1 step at its end. Basic animation lasts 4 ticks; bitten fruit shaking/knockback may extend the whole action. Each tick moves at most 4 pixels per axis and draws a white circle (color 0) shrinking from radius 8 to 0; enemy1's fixed-direction flight is handled separately.
- `ACTION7`: Undo. **No step cost**. Every `ACTION6` first saves the board (**including entirely ineffective clicks outside the clickable region**), permitting repeated rollback to the level's start. Undo restores only fruit/enemy positions and tiers; **it does not refund spent steps**, nor roll back the penalty count. It can rescue the board, but not the budget. Old human baselines for L4/L8 were 151/179; current medians are 115/40. These statistics alone do not establish how often players used Undo.

**Evaluation order**: Each vacuum tick first moves pulled objects and handles enemy chasing/biting. The chasing routine also checks for overlapping enemy merges; **enemies can merge before tick 4**. At tick 4, all enemies are checked again before vacuumed fruit merges resolve; a merge error enters the penalty branch. Each frame's target check precedes normal charging and the “all fruit eaten” loss check. **Once the clearing branch is entered, that action no longer pays the normal 1 step**, so a final vacuum can win with 1 step left. A normal nonwinning action checks fruit count and charges only after bite-extended animation finishes. The entire animation is one action; Undo cannot interrupt it.

**Level changes**: Rebuild fruit/enemies/targets from level data, reset steps and penalty, and clear vacuuming, cooldown, knockback, error hints, and Undo history. Undo rebuilds fruit and enemies; it does not restore whether tutorial hints have disappeared or reconstruct the whole animation history. It is not a game snapshot containing every field.

**Mass conservation and the “unsolvable” warning**
Define a fruit's mass as `2^tier`. Merging two equal-tier fruit conserves mass (`2^k + 2^k = 2^(k+1)`); merging three or more together, or enemy bites, **permanently loses mass**. At the end of each vacuum the game checks “total board mass ≥ total mass required by the targets.” If false, **the HUD target icons and all target rings turn entirely color 2**, indicating that the level can no longer be won (the game does not automatically lose at this point; Undo or continue until the budget is exhausted). Colors recover automatically when Undo restores enough mass. This is the game's most important immediate feedback.

**Enemies (`ybnveypak` family)**
Pixel creatures with **4 rows × 5 columns (width 5, height 4)** in three types, colors 7 / 14 / 13.

- **Behavior**: During the first 4 ticks of each legal vacuum, enemies that are neither being pulled nor cooling down move toward the **nearest fruit not in a bitten animation**. enemy1/enemy2 move 1 pixel per axis per tick, enemy3 moves 2. Diagonal movement may change both x and y; these are not Euclidean vectors of fixed length 1/2.
- **Biting**: An enemy touching fruit bites it → the fruit **drops one tier** and is knocked about 10 cells away in the opposite direction (4 shaking frames then 4 flying frames at 2.5 cells/frame). Biting tier-0 fruit **destroys it outright**. The enemy then gets a 9-frame cooldown, **during which it neither bites nor moves** (both pursuit and biting loops in `sgmsqapcxe` skip cooling-down enemies).
- **Cooldown does not persist across actions** (tested; a particularly easy point to misunderstand). At the very start of each click, `axaxyjxqoe()` clears all of `yghzqxumz` (along with `ksuxajatq`, `ppvvgaayv`, `idbgnpolc`), so cooldown left by a bite on the previous click **disappears at the next click's start**. Therefore **cooldown need not be tracked across actions**. Object insertion order and occlusion still have the observability limits listed below; penalty does affect charges across actions.
- **Being vacuumed**: Enemies inside the vacuum radius are pulled too and **do not bite during that turn** (vacuuming can restrain them, even parking them on fruit). enemy2/enemy3 **stop at the click**. enemy1 instead flies in the “enemy→click” direction at a fixed speed for 4 frames, speed `4 × 0.85 = 3.4` cells/frame, **about 13.6 cells total**, **overshooting the click point** (even farther if a fruit bite extends the animation). Each frame applies `round()`, clamps to the bounds, then continues accumulating from that clamped integer, so 13.6 is approximate and edges truncate it. Only the **direction** is precisely controllable; sending enemy1 into a target ring requires working backward from the landing point to the click.
- **Enemies merge too**: Two overlapping enemies of the same type (checked during chasing, without waiting for tick 4) → upgrade (enemy1→enemy2→enemy3), with the new enemy at **the average of member centers** and a 4-frame cooldown. Two overlapping enemy3 → **both disappear**. Different types overlapping → the same error as mixed-tier fruit: flashing + penalty + automatic Undo. Enemies can collide and upgrade while independently chasing fruit, or be deliberately pulled together with one vacuum.
- From L6 onward enemies become **tools** as well as hazards: there is no other way to “split” fruit, so **feeding fruit to enemies** is the only way to reduce its tier.

**HUD (rows 0–9, not clickable)**
- Left side of rows 1–2: merge-chain legend (colors 10→6→15→11→12→8, tiers 0–5). In L2–L5 the last two cells (tiers 4/5) are drawn on panel color 4, meaning they are unused; L6–L9 place the whole chain on background color 5. L1 has no legend.
- Middle of rows 2–6 (starting around x≈30): **target icons**, drawing exactly what `xkstxyqbs` requires at original size (fruit squares + enemy patterns). **These are machine-readable victory conditions**: scan `y ≤ 9`, `x ≥ 25` (background color 4; the legend is left at x ≤ 22), and count solid fruit squares and enemy pixel patterns by color to recover `xkstxyqbs` itself, without external knowledge. Required multiplicities are explicitly drawn (L7 `[3,2]` draws two 4×4 color-11 squares; L8 `[[4,2],[ybnveypak2,1]]` draws two 5×5 color-12 squares and one color-14 enemy).
- Far left of rows 5–8 (L8, L9): enemy upgrade-chain legend (7→14→13).

> “Mass surplus” = total board `2^tier` minus required target mass. It is **not** a fixed “number of bites”: biting tier-k loses `2^(k-1)`, so L6's surplus 24 exactly covers biting tier5 (−16), then tier4 (−8); likewise for L7. L8/L9's 16 exactly covers “one tier5 bite.” In other words, the surplus permits only **those scripted bites**; any additional bite necessarily makes the level unsolvable (rings turn color 2).

> L5's surplus 4 has a better use: the four tier1 fruit alone make tier3 (3 merges); the four tier0 fruit **need not participate at all** and instead provide free bait for the two enemies. Cleared in 6 steps in testing.

**What the display cannot reveal (observability limits)**

The board provides most geometry/tier information, but **one frame cannot be claimed to equal the full transition state**. The old version/reconstructed engine had 2725 random cross-checks; rereading current source retains these limits:

- **Sprite insertion order**. Nearest-fruit pursuit uses squared center distance, breaking ties with the first fruit in `lkujttxgs`; bites and merges in the same frame also depend on list order. A single frame omits that order. Complete action/merge history can track it; it is not engine randomness. Planning from frames alone should avoid ties or replan after observing results.
- **Objects covered by the budget bar**. Row 63 is drawn as the bar last. A tier-0 fruit at `y = 63` and `x <` bar length is entirely invisible (only knockback can send it there). Likewise, an enemy completely beneath a large fruit disappears (enemy layer −1, fruit layers 0/9/10); two same-tier fruit exactly overlapping at one location also look like one.

  Such occlusions occurred in 10 of the old reconstructed engine's 2725 random frames; this does not prove their absence on all normal current-version routes.

- **Penalty and Undo history**. The next mixed-tier penalty depends on accumulated errors, without a separate visible counter. Identical fruit/enemy displays can have different penalties and rollback results. For this game's 32/48-step budgets, the bar distinguishes remaining positive steps one by one, but cannot reveal penalty. Mid-animation memory also includes the pulled set, velocities, biting, and merge stages. “Cooldown does not persist across legal clicks” does not mean arbitrary intermediate frames are memoryless states.

Supplemental checks:

- **Transition function matches**: Boards reconstructed from frames were fed to a custom `simulate()` and compared against the real engine for 2725 random clicks (including merging, mixed-tier errors, bites, knockback, enemy merging, budget, and penalty accounting). **Every pixel matched except in 17 uncertain “tie/simultaneous overlap” ordering cases**. Those 17 are exactly the observability limit described above; the solver detects and avoids them.
- **Mid-run handoff**: Split the clearing sequence at **every** action (82 cut points), hand over to a fresh solver instance; all 82/82 still finished.
- **Unusual boards**: Randomly click 1–4 times per level before handing over to the solver. Of 108 states, 105 still cleared. Manual accounting found the other 3 **truly impossible to assemble**—because mass could not be **partitioned into two portions**, rather than insufficient total mass. For example, L7 after random clicks had `tier0 + 3×tier1 + tier4` (mass 23 ≥ 16); biting tier4 makes the first tier3, but the remainder `1+2+2+2 = 7 < 8` cannot make the second. L8 had `tier2 + tier3 + tier5`; after biting tier5 into tier4, `4 + 8 = 12 < 16`. In such states the game **does not** gray the rings (total mass still passes); only partitioning reveals the dead end. The solver reports that the target cannot be assembled rather than making arbitrary moves.

Three strategically useful facts:

1. **The merge point is the landing point**, so clicking the final merge directly inside a target ring resolves the goal without a separate transport phase.
2. **Victory is checked every frame**. L6 cleared on the **3rd frame** of the final vacuum: one vacuum first flung enemy1 into the upper-left ring (it stopped there after flying), then the next pulled tier3 toward the lower-right ring. The exact frame the fruit center entered the ring resolved victory, without waiting for animation completion or requiring the enemy to settle.
3. **Cooldown does not persist across actions**, permitting local transition search; insertion-order ties and occlusion still must be avoided. The L4 old reconstructed-engine route used 11 steps.
