### The maze alternates nodes and passages; not every cell is a walkable position

Everything uses 3×3-pixel cells, but **there are two cell types**:

- **Node cells** (color **0** in the maze sprite): The player and enemies stand only on nodes.
- **Passage cells** (color **2**): Between neighboring nodes; color 2 means that edge is open, any other color means a wall.

Nodes have even cell coordinates; passages have odd cell coordinates. ⚠️ Thus **one move covers 6 pixels = 2 cells
= 1 node** (`lvzrmdesux` moves 1 pixel per tick until both x and y relative to the maze origin are
multiples of 6). Treating every 3×3 cell as one move doubles all distances.

Maze walls and everything outside the maze use color 5 (`BACKGROUND_COLOR` = `PADDING_COLOR` = 5).
Screen row 63 contains the action bar (full portion color 6, consumed portion color 0). A full action bar ⟺ the first image of this level.

### Actions

`ACTION1-4` = up/down/left/right, each moving **1 node** (6 animation frames for player movement, then 6 for enemies).

⚠️ **Hitting a wall is a “wait one turn,” not a dead end**: The end of `step()` still calls
`complete_action()`, so a wall hit consumes 1 budget unit while the player position/facing, enemies, and mimic queues **all remain unchanged**.
**Even wake-up checks do not run**: Only successful movement enters phase 1→2, with `gmwsemdsae` called in phase 2.
Waiting against a wall therefore cannot advance enemies or improve the situation; planning can omit it.

### ⚠️ Collisions are asymmetric

The earlier statement “contact with an enemy makes both explode” was **wrong**. Only the party whose position is entered explodes:

| Situation | Result |
|---|---|
| **Player enters an enemy's node** | That **enemy** is exploded by `uneirnujpq` and removed; the player is unharmed |
| **Enemy enters the player's node** | The **player** explodes → `ttauyadveo` becomes empty → `lose()` |

The resolution order is hardcoded in `step()`'s three-phase state machine: finish player movement and remove the enemy underfoot
(`onfzuzfvcl`) → tracker checks (`ixnhjkzwic`) → patrol movement (`itwvxwpzyb`) →
mimic movement (`wcmxxknbpe`) → patrol reversal (`rgwzxyjuqc`) → mimic wake-up/facing update
(`gmwsemdsae`) → check success, then check loss.

Thus “actively bumping into an enemy to remove it” is not merely possible; it is **a core tactic**. Measured exhaustive BFS
for routes that “never touch any enemy” found **no solution in levels 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9** (only level 1
has no enemies, and level 5 permits avoidance). A typical case has a tracker blocking the only corridor, with its lethal node on
your only approach route—you must approach from another direction and step onto it to remove it.

### ⚠️ Sprites show facing direction

An enemy sprite has an outer ring in its body color and one “eye” pixel at the midpoint of its top edge:

```
[8, 15, 8]      Tracker 8 / patrol 12 / mimic 13 / player 9 (eye color 4)
[8,  8, 8]      Exit: solid color-14 3×3, no eye
[8,  8, 8]
```

Engine `rotation` rotates the entire sprite clockwise, so **the eye indicates facing**: top midpoint = up
(rotation 0), right midpoint = right (90), bottom midpoint = down (180), left midpoint = left (270).
Facing can be read from one image; consecutive frames are unnecessary.

Once triggered, trackers/mimics have their eye permanently changed by `qlzvpfwmqv` to color **11** (`ziedssriec`).

### Three enemy types

| Enemy | tag / appearance | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| **Tracker** | `0001haidilggfh`, color 8 | Normally completely stationary. After every successful player move, checks whether the player is **exactly 1 node directly ahead** (`wlhbetxehh(..., gtjyscyfcu=6)`, 6 pixels = 2 cells = 1 node). If so, advances 1 node—exactly onto the player, so ⚠️**triggering is immediately fatal**, with no intermediate “approach, then chase” state. The check **ignores walls**, including walls between them.<br>In practice it is a static mine: never stop one node directly in front, and all other nodes (including its own position) are safe. |
| **Patrol** | `0020npxxteirsg`, color 12 | After every successful player move, **unconditionally** moves 1 node forward (`itwvxwpzyb` performs no checks, ⚠️not even walls; if its initial facing points into a wall, it passes straight through). Only after landing does `rgwzxyjuqc` check whether the passage cell ahead is color 2; if not, it reverses. Its trajectory is independent of the player and can be tabulated in advance. |
| **Mimic** | `0023otenflmryc`, color 13 | Wakes when the player stops **2 nodes directly ahead** (12 pixels = 4 cells), then replays the player's direction sequence with a delay. ⚠️ The net effect is simple: starting with turn 2 after waking, **on subsequent successful-movement turns it occupies the player's node from two successful-movement turns earlier** (`M(t) = P(t−2)`). Thus it cannot hit you unless you “immediately backtrack.” Conversely, **once awake and maintaining this delay relationship**, you cannot actively bump into it either; before waking, you can still approach from the side and remove it. Hitting it after waking would require `P(t) = P(t−3)`, forbidden by grid parity. |

The mimic's replay queue (`ylmdnwbdyy`) can be recovered from images when the necessary conditions hold: its next direction is
shown directly on its face when unobscured; once the two-turn delay relationship is established, the following move can be inferred from the positional difference minus its current 6 px direction vector.
This requires a known wake-up phase and unobscured player/mimic positions; it is not valid to claim that every arbitrary single image has no hidden state.

### Success and loss

- Success: At the end of resolution, the player's coordinates exactly match the exit sprite → `next_level()`.
- Loss: Player removed, or action budget reaches zero. **Success is checked before loss**, so reaching the exit with the final
  budget unit still wins.
- On entering each level, `upxtibxaya()` resets the budget to that level's `StepCounter` (no accumulation across levels).
- ⚠️ Level 4 baseline 42 > action limit 20: The baseline cannot directly estimate “moves needed in one life.”

### Occlusion and intermediate frames

When two actors overlap, the final frame may show only the upper actor. Current-version drawing follows list order; the old assumption “the exit always covers enemies” no longer applies. The level-5 tracker fully obscured at initialization never moves and **cannot** be recovered through animation; distinguish this from recoverable moving occlusion.

Moving actors are offset from their endpoints in intermediate `obs.frames`, allowing landing positions to be inferred from visible pixels, facing, and the node grid. Enemies removed by player collisions may briefly appear in intermediate frames but must be excluded at resolution. This recovery method has been used in current clearing routes; it has not been shown to handle arbitrary multiple occlusions.

**Check intermediate frames for the exit too**: In current level 5, a patrol on the exit leaves no color 14 in the final frame. The exit is fixed, and color 14 belongs exclusively to it. Find exposed color 14 in intermediate frames of the same action, then locate the exit on the 6-pixel node grid. Do not reuse the previous level's animation; a level-transition action returns both the old level's ending and the new level's initial image.
